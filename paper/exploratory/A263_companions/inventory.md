Conventions
- **matched** = `pseudo_waveforms(kind="matched")` (expression-matched non-clock genes outside `CLOCK_LIKE`, `controls.pseudo_clock_genes`);
  **responsive** = `kind="responsive"` (GC / immediate-early genes, `controls.responsive_pseudo_genes`, pool of `RESPONSIVE_GENES`).
- "Same perms?" asks whether the companion is computed under the same permutations / bootstrap draws as the statistic (the sense of
  `Dataset.contrast(companion=)`, results.py l.141). **In every gene-set companion below the answer is no:** the pseudo sets are
  evaluated once on the unpermuted data (200 sets, or 50 in A208/A231/A234/A235) and the clock value is ranked against the set
  distribution (q95, "share of sets >= observed"); the permutation null of the clock statistic is a separate computation. The only
  same-permutation companions in the tables are compartment companions (A208/A231 "3 lowest-coupling tissues", A235 module minus
  companion), not gene-set companions.
- Entry point and `kind=`: `clocksync.paired.pseudo_waveforms(log_mean, n_sets, rng, waveform, kind="matched"|"responsive")` accepts `kind`
  (paired.py l.160). `clocksync.paired.paired(..., pseudo_responsive=True)` takes a boolean, not `kind` (l.204-215). `Dataset.fit / gate /
  contrast / agreement / timed` have no `kind` or gene-set companion argument (Dataset.contrast has `companion=` = a compartment, not a
  gene set); a gene-set companion there is built by passing a pseudo `Waveform` as `cs.Dataset(..., waveform=w)`. `common/development.py`
  `l1_test` / `l2_prepare` loop over `("matched","responsive")` (l.152, l.172).

## The brain block (A142 H142.1; r2_readout; sheep / baboon)

**Statistic.** (`exploratory/A142_organ_blocks/scripts/compute_organ_blocks.py`, statistic of A103 `_shared.block_stat`, generalised.) GTEx v10 fast
deaths (Hardy 1-2), tissues with >= 40 donors (40 tissues: 13 brain, 27 peripheral). Per sample a **timed-fit departure**: the 14-gene
clock-plane state is fitted per tissue on the recorded time of death (`_shared.timed_fit`, `beta = mu + a e^{i w t} + e`), and the departure is
`u = e / (a e^{i w t})`, linear phase departure `Im u / omega` (`_shared.departures`). Departures are standardised per tissue
(`zwide`), the block score of a donor is the mean over the block's tissues present (>= 2), r is the Pearson correlation over donors between the brain
score and the score of the other tissues, and **r\* = r / sqrt(rel_brain x rel_periph)** with split-half reliability (tissues split at random,
Spearman-Brown, mean over `N_SPLIT = 50` splits, `_shared.reliability`). Values: r 0.5748, rel 0.8832 and 0.9155, r\* 0.6393, 205 donors
(`tests.csv`). Note that this r\* uses recorded time of death, so it is a timed statistic, whereas the sheep and baboon r\* of `r1_*` use
the untimed `Dataset.agreement` (r\* of two hosts fitted on the brain and on the peripheral tissues, same reliability recipe), and the
sheep / baboon rows quoted in the roadmap (0.205 vs 0.416; 0.420 vs 0.712) are a third statistic (Mann-Whitney of tissue couplings).

**Interval.** Percentile 2.5 / 97.5 % of 200 donor bootstraps (rows of the donor x tissue matrix drawn with replacement among donors
with >= 2 tissues in each block; reliabilities recomputed in each replicate with `N_SPLIT` splits, non-finite r\* set to +inf, so an undefined
upper bound appears when many replicates fail, e.g. food_exposed `r_star_hi` empty in `targets.csv`) (`boot`, l.55-61): [0.5302, 0.7283].
The rng of the bootstrap is one shared stream (`default_rng(142)`) consumed sequentially together with the split draws.

**Reference median.** For k = 13 (= number of brain tissues) the script draws 1,000 random 13-tissue blocks from the 27 peripheral tissues; each
block is the target "A" and the remaining peripheral tissues are "B" (14 others; the brain target has 27), seed `SEED*100000 + 1000*k + i` (`reference`,
`_ref_one`); the r\* of every random block is computed with `block_stat`. Reference median 0.9783, mean 0.9777, sd 0.0245, q05 0.9359 (`reference_summary.csv`,
`tests.csv`); one-sided p = (1 + #{ref <= r\*}) / (1 + 1,000) = 0.000999. So "specific to brain" is tested against **random tissue blocks**, not
against random gene sets.

**What a companion could be, on the same resampling.** Question: is brain's lower agreement with the body specific to clock genes?
Two designs, both reusing the draws that already define the statistic.
(i) *Untimed route (preferred, entirely in clocksync).* Build the human GTEx fast-death pseudobulk with the pseudo genes
(`common/gtex.py::load(genes=<union of pseudo genes>)`, `depth="sum"` keeps the library size) and for each of 200 matched and 200 responsive
`Waveform`s from `pseudo_waveforms(log_mean, 200, rng, cs.gtex(), kind=...)` (log_mean from the GTEx genome table, as `A208::pseudo_sets`) call
`cs.Dataset(pb, anchors=..., waveform=w).agreement({"brain": brain, "periphery": per}, n_boot=200, seed=0)`. `Dataset.agreement` draws its
donor bootstrap from `default_rng(10_000 + seed + s)` (`_core/agreement.py` l.76), so the same donor resamples are applied to every waveform
(and to the clock waveform) when the dataset has the same donors; reliability splits are seeded by `seed + s`, also identical across waveforms.
This gives r\*(clock) and r\*(set j) with identical bootstrap draws and identical tissue splits; specificity is read as the clock r\* against the
distribution of set r\*, with the same interval logic (clock upper bound < 1 and clock r\* below q05 of the matched and responsive sets); a
paired difference r\*(clock) - r\*(set j) per bootstrap replicate would give an interval for "brain is more separate for the clock than for non-clock genes".
Sets with undefined reliability (<= 0) must be counted and reported, as in A142 (reference blocks with undefined r\* dropped, 15 of 1,000 at k = 4).
(ii) *Timed route (the statistic as registered).* Replace `phase_departures(pb, t, genes)` by a version taking a `Waveform` (the function builds
`cs.gtex().subset(genes)`; `_shared.dataset(pb, waveform=w)` already accepts one) and recompute `zwide` for each set, then `block_stat(Z_j, brain, periph)`;
the 1,000 reference tissue blocks use fixed seeds (`SEED*100000 + 1000*k + i`) so the same blocks can be reused for every set, giving a per-set
reference median and a per-set p, and the target-minus-reference-median (clock: 0.6393 - 0.9783 = -0.339) can be compared with the same quantity
of the sets. For the bootstrap, draw one integer index matrix of 200 donor resamples and pass it to `boot` (currently `boot` consumes the
global rng, so a refactor is needed to make the draws identical across sets).
**Caveat for (ii):** the departure divides the residual by the tissue's own fitted timed amplitude `a`; for non-rhythmic pseudo genes `a` is near zero,
so the departure is heavy-tailed and dominated by noise (the same mechanism that sends pseudo hosts to the estimator bound in A234). The matched
sets should be restricted to or at least reported against their timed amplitude, or the amplitude-free residual `e` used; the untimed route (i) avoids this.
**Cost (extrapolation, no log).** The full A142 run (3 planes, reference sizes 13, 7, 4, 5, 5 targets x 200 bootstraps) took 1,509 s on 4 jobs
(`jobs.tsv`, `compute_organ_blocks.py`, peak 1.3 GB). A brain-only target with 1,000 reference blocks and 200 bootstraps is roughly a quarter of one plane,
so about 100 to 150 s per set at 4 jobs: 200 + 200 sets is about 11 to 17 h, 50 + 50 sets about 3 to 4 h; route (i) has no reference blocks and costs one
`agreement` call (50 splits x 201 refits) per set, which is the slow, serial part reported as "hours" for sheep and baboon (README).
**Related evidence already on disk:** A253 shows region pairing in post-mortem brain controls is matched by responsive sets (R 0.734; matched
q95 0.549; responsive median 0.648, q95 0.758; `results_preliminary.md` l.4650-4652), which is the reason the roadmap lists A142 as needing a
responsive companion; A143 shows the split is not explained by sample quality or isolation batch (H143.1 pass: r\* 0.627).

## Ambiguities and flags

- The roadmap row "Brain couples less ... in three species" mixes three statistics: human r\* (timed departures, 0.639), sheep and baboon
  Mann-Whitney on tissue couplings (r2_readout). The baboon `Dataset.agreement` r\* is **0.9696 [0.8498, 1.1199], separate False** (`r1_baboon_readout.csv`),
  i.e. the r\* readout does not support "partly separate" in the baboon (12 animals).
- A138's responsive companion (q95 0.3097, Holm 0.00995, `output/responsive/`) is not in the roadmap row nor in
  `exploratory/A138_cancer_estimability/output/score/tests.csv` (which has matched only); the headline can be upgraded by citation.
- The matched companions of A208/A231/A234/A235 use 50 sets (others 200) and are evaluated on the observed estimate only; "same permutations" does not hold for any gene-set companion.
- The A231 d* companion in the roadmap context is a compartment companion, not a gene-set one.
- Runtimes for A251, A252, A255, A256, A257, A201, A123 (working-hours script) and A132b are not in `jobs.tsv` or `paper/results/logs/` (scripts run outside `run.sh`).
