## 1. Wefers 2018 (muscle) in detail

Source read: PMC6065021 full text via Europe PMC (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6065021/fullTextXML); GEO https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE106800.

Design: 14 healthy lean young men (22.4 +- 2.8 y, BMI 22.3), randomized crossover, washout 4-10 wk; 3-d control (alignment) vs 3.5-d misalignment ("induced by a 12-h rapid shift of the behavioral cycle"), dim light (4 lx awake), respiration chamber. Two biopsies per condition (vastus lateralis), Affymetrix Human Gene 2.1 ST; 47 arrays used in the PLS-DA.

Methods quotes:
- "Skeletal Muscle Biopsies. During each study period, two skeletal muscle biopsies were obtained, at 8 AM and 8 PM from the m. vastus lateralis under local anesthesia" (Results/figure legends use 7 AM and 7 PM for plasma and qPCR; internal inconsistency, 1 h).
- Control: "admitted to the research unit at 7:45 PM on day 1 and stayed until 5 PM on day 4. Sleep opportunities were from 11 PM until 7 AM ... During the test days (except for the last test day), participants received breakfast, lunch, a snack, and dinner ... at fixed times (8 AM, 12:30 PM, 3 PM, and 8 PM, respectively)." Low-intensity stepping activity 1 h after each meal.
- Misaligned: "participants were admitted at 7:45 PM on day 1 and had a normal sleeping opportunity from 11 PM until 7 AM. On day 2, circadian misalignment was imposed by shifting the behavioral cycle by 12 h. Participants were instructed to switch off the lights, remain lying in bed and try to sleep from 3 PM until 7 PM. At 7 PM participants started a new behavioral morning with all study procedures equal to the control period but shifted by 12 h."
- Plasma: Fig. 2 legend: "measured in the evening before dinner (nonfasted) and in the overnight fasted state 15 min after awaking, 7 AM and 7 PM in the control and misalignment condition, respectively." Clamp "after an overnight fast, either at 8 AM or ..." (8 AM control / 8 PM misaligned).
- Clock: "Intriguingly, upon circadian misalignment, mRNA expression levels of the core clock genes kept the same diurnal pattern as in the control condition, albeit blunted for PER and CRY, indicating that the core molecular clock in skeletal muscle indeed was misaligned relative to the inverted behavioral cycle." Microarray: BMAL1 (ARNTL), PER1/2/3, CIART did not reverse, consistent across subjects. CBT (SI Fig S1) still followed the central clock: decreased in the late biological evening despite subjects awake.
- Outputs: genes differing 7 AM vs 7 PM: 296 (FDR<0.05) in control and 238 in misalignment; "many of the most highly enriched gene sets among the genes changed in expression from 7 AM to 7 PM in the misaligned condition were related to fatty acid metabolism and PPAR signaling"; glucose-metabolism sets not changed. Non-oxidative glucose disposal 23.7 +- 2.4 vs 18.4 +- 1.4 mg/kg/min (P=0.024). No fraction-of-genes-reversed number reported. Sample clustering: only 3 of 47 samples misclassified by the 4-cluster PLS-DA.

Timing consequences (derived by me from the text, not stated verbatim except where quoted):
- Aligned: morning biopsy 8 AM = ~1 h after waking at 7 AM, overnight-fasted state (plasma drawn 15 min after waking; breakfast scheduled 8 AM, biopsy order relative to breakfast is not stated). Evening biopsy 8 PM = non-fasted "before dinner" (dinner at 8 PM), ~13 h awake, last meal (snack) 3 PM, sleep the night before 11 PM-7 AM.
- Misaligned: all procedures shifted 12 h, so "behavioral morning" starts 7 PM (waking after a 3-7 PM in-bed/sleep opportunity on day 2, then presumably the shifted sleep 11 AM-7 PM on later days; the paper does not list the shifted times, this is inference from "shifted by 12 h"). The 8 PM (or 7 PM) biopsy is therefore at behavioral morning (fasted, ~1 h after waking, breakfast at 8 PM), and the 8 AM (7 AM) biopsy is at behavioral evening (pre-dinner, nonfasted, dinner at 8 AM, last snack 3 AM). So within each condition both biopsies are at the same behavioral phase pairs (morning fasted / evening pre-dinner), and the clock-time contrast is inverted relative to behavior. Which test day the biopsies were taken on is in Fig. 1 (graphic), not in the text.
- Practical consequence for any "host" analysis: the misaligned 7 AM vs 7 PM contrast mixes clock time (same as control) with behavioral phase (inverted); the central clock is only partly shifted (dim light, 3.5 d), and the muscle clock stayed with the central/clock-time pattern.

## 2. Weger 2021 (liver)
Design (Results): WT, Bmal1 KO, Cry1/2 KO mice, ad libitum (AL) vs night-restricted feeding (NRF) under LD; RNA-seq every 4 h, "two independent replicates"; dryR differential rhythmicity.
Key numbers (verbatim): "at least 32% and 54% of rhythmic transcripts under NRF lost rhythmicity in the absence of rhythmic food intake (AL) in Bmal1 and Cry1/2 KOs, respectively"; "transcripts assigned to the food-driven model comprised 22% (Bmal1 KO) and 31% (Cry1/2 KO) of all rhythmic genes"; "of all transcripts assigned to a model with identical rhythms in WT AL and NRF, a comparable proportion was detected to be regulated by the circadian clock (clock-driven) in both Bmal1 KO (30%) and Cry1/2 KO (37%)". Food-driven genes have "on average 36% lower amplitudes". Abstract: "High-amplitude mRNA expression rhythms in wild-type livers were driven by the circadian clock, but rhythmic feeding also contributed ... albeit with significantly lower amplitudes." Clock genes: "The vast majority of circadian core clock genes lost rhythmicity in Bmal1 and Cry1/2 KOs irrespective of the offered feeding regimen ... except for Per2".
Data availability verbatim: "GEO Series accession nos. GSE135898 and GSE135875"; code dryR (github naef-lab/dryR).

## 3. Vollmers 2009
Abstract (verified): "Livers of mice held under constant darkness exhibit circadian rhythm in abundance in up to 15% of expressed transcripts"; "In the absence of feeding, only a small subset of transcripts continued to express circadian patterns. Conversely, temporally restricted feeding restored rhythmic transcription of hundreds of genes in oscillator-deficient mouse liver." Exact counts NOT verified (full text unreadable). GEO GSE13093 is a SuperSeries "Feeding schedule and the circadian clock shape rhythms in hepatic gene expression", PMID 19940241.

## 4. Greco 2021
Verbatim: "13.7% of oscillating genes were autonomous" (liver clock only), classes: autonomous / integrated (liver clock + feeding) / feeding-driven / network-dependent. "Of the 1236 nonautonomous genes, 666 (53.9%) gained oscillation under NF in Liver-RE. Of those, 442 (66.4%) were integrated by hepatic BMAL1, while only 224 (33.6%) oscillated in KO and were thus feeding-driven." Abstract: "liver rhythms tied to redox and lipid metabolism required communication with the skeletal muscle clock". Design: Liver-RE (Bmal1 reconstituted in hepatocytes in otherwise Bmal1-null mice), ad libitum vs night feeding (NF), ZT4 vs ZT16 serum measures, time-course RNA-seq (GEO above; the accessions are quoted from the Methods).

## 5. Kumar 2024
Abstract verbatim (Europe PMC): "In arrhythmic mice showing premature aging, we created a basic clock module involving a central and a peripheral (muscle) clock. Reconstituting the brain-muscle clock network is sufficient to preserve fundamental daily homeostatic functions and prevent premature muscle aging. However, achieving whole muscle physiology requires contributions from other peripheral clocks. Mechanistically, the muscle peripheral clock acts as a gatekeeper, selectively suppressing detrimental signals from the central clock while integrating important muscle homeostatic functions. ... underscores the impact of eating patterns on these interactions." No numbers on the share of rhythmic muscle transcripts could be verified (Science page 403; UB repository PDF returned HTML; press release has no numbers). TODO: read the paper directly before quoting any percentage. Preprint-based press says TRF in old mice (66-74 wk) restored muscle gene rhythms (secondary source, unverified).

## 6. Archer 2014
Abstract verbatim: "mistimed sleep leads to a reduction of rhythmic transcripts in the human blood transcriptome from 6.4% at baseline to 1.0% during forced desynchrony of sleep and centrally driven circadian rhythms." Affected: chromatin modification, transcription, translation, cold-inducible RNA-binding proteins, "core clock genes including CLOCK and ARNTL (BMAL1)". "the sleep-wake cycle coordinates the timing of transcription and translation in particular."
GEO GSE48113 (read): "22 healthy volunteers ... forced-desynchrony protocol, in which the sleep-wake cycle and the associated fasting-feeding cycle is scheduled to a 28-hour period, of which one third (i.e. 9h 20min) is scheduled for sleep ... the phase of the melatonin rhythm occurred at approximately the same clock time during the first (D1) and fourth (D4) 28-h cycle ... During D1 and D4 7 blood samples were taken, RNA extracted from leukocytes ... 287 samples". Note: sleep AND fasting-feeding both desynchronized; so "mistimed sleep" is sleep+feeding+posture.

## 7. Koronowski 2019
Abstract verbatim: "circadian expression is restricted to ~10% of normally rhythmic transcripts. Finally, rhythmic clock gene expression is lost in Liver-RE mice under constant darkness." Results verbatim: "RNA sequencing revealed 18,079 coding transcripts, of which 2,010 are Bmal1-dependent transcripts oscillating in WT. Of these, 218 (10.8%) also oscillated in Liver-RE mice. A small group of genes (302) oscillated exclusively in Liver-RE and 144 genes oscillated exclusively in KO mice. Only 6 genes oscillated independently of BMAL1 in WT, KO and Liver-RE mice." Core clock genes restored in LD ("albeit with slightly dampened amplitude and advanced timing"), JTK_CYCLE adj p<0.01, ad libitum feeding, LD 12:12. GEO GSE117134. (Cell volume 177, pp. 1448-1462; the companion Welz 2019 is Cell 177:1436.)

## 8. Greenwell 2019
Abstract verbatim: "more than 70% of the cycling mouse liver transcriptome loses rhythmicity under arrhythmic feeding. Remarkably, core clock genes are not among the 70% of genes losing rhythmic expression, and their expression continues to exhibit normal oscillations in arrhythmically fed mice." GEO GSE118967 (read): "mice fed ad libitum, only at night, or arrhythmically (mouse eating 1/8th of their daily food intake every 3 hours)"; "3' mRNA sequencing on mice livers under 4 different feeding paradigms at 6 timepoints in triplicate". Counts of rhythmic transcripts not read.

## 9. Kornmann 2007
Verbatim: "351 circadian transcripts (represented by 432 feature sets) for Dox-treated animals" (clock running); "we identified 31 different transcripts (represented by 41 feature sets), whose circadian accumulation was not affected by the Dox treatment" (31/351 = 8.8%, not a figure in the paper). Abstract: "the rhythmic transcription of most genes depended on functional hepatocyte clocks. However, we discovered 31 genes, including the core clock gene mPer2, whose expression oscillated robustly irrespective of whether the liver clock was running or not." Model: LAP-tTA/TRE-Rev-erb-alpha, dox-dependent. ArrayExpress E-MEXP-842.

## 10. Dyar 2014
Verbatim: "Out of the 684 (Ctrl TA) and 1359 (Ctrl SOL) 24-h cycling genes identified by the JTK_CYCLE algorithm, 487 genes in TA and 666 genes in SOL lost their 24-h oscillation in the absence of Bmal1, showing that a significant proportion of cycling genes in mouse skeletal muscles is controlled by the intrinsic muscle clock." (71% TA, 49% SOL, computed by me). Also "931 genes in TA and 706 in SOL ... differentially regulated (maSigPro)". Design: mKO (HSA-Cre) and inducible imKO; ZT0,4,8,12,16,20; ad libitum, LD 12:12. Note erratum exists (Mol Metab 3:857, PMID 25503566).

## 11. Lundell 2020
Verbatim abstract: "skeletal muscle and serum metabolic and transcriptomic profiles from 11 men with overweight/obesity after TRF (8 h day-1) and extended feeding (EXF, 15 h day-1) in a randomised cross-over design (ACTRN12617000165381). ... muscle core clock gene expression was similar after both interventions. TRF increases the amplitude of oscillating muscle transcripts". Methods: 5 days of provided isoenergetic meals; TRF 1000-1800 h (meals 1000, 1300, 1700), EXF 0700-2200 h (0700, 1400, 2100); on day 5 arrival ~0630 after overnight fast, 24 h in lab, 6 vastus lateralis biopsies every 4 h from 0700, blood every 4 h, sleep in dark. RAIN, FDR<0.05. Results: 24 h period in "8% EXF and 15% TRF" of 14,954 muscle transcripts; "1,609 oscillating skeletal muscle transcripts unique to TRF, and 615 unique to EXF, with 582 shared". GEO GSE129843 (RNA-seq; supplementary file GSE129843_RESTRICT.txt.gz; the GEO summary says "4-10% of transcripts" periodic). Clock genes only tested on a few genes (not perturbed).

## 12. Perrin 2018
10 healthy volunteers, vastus lateralis biopsies every 4 h over 24 h (6 time points, 57 samples), controlled lab routine (hourly liquid meal-replacement during waking hours, 800 lux, semi-recumbent). "5748 rhythmic genes that were rhythmic at the pre-mRNA or/and mRNA level with a False Discovery Rate (FDR) of 5%"; 4792 rhythmic at pre-mRNA level; myotubes: 994 rhythmic genes (7.65%) of 12,985; CLOCK siRNA "significantly affected the expression of ~8% of all genes"; abstract: "a large part of the in vivo mRNA rhythmicity was lost in vitro". GEO GSE108539 (muscle, 57 samples, normalized read counts available), GSE109825.

## 13. Kervezee 2018: what is "73%"
Abstract (verbatim, read on PMC page): "73% of transcripts rhythmically expressed at baseline remained rhythmic during the night shift condition with a similar phase relative to habitual bedtimes, but with lower amplitudes." (Abstract wording: "transcripts"; Results wording: probe sets.)
Results (verbatim): "Interestingly, this approach revealed that 73% of the probe sets (676 out of 929 probe sets) that were rhythmic at baseline remained cycling with a similar rhythmic pattern during the night shift condition." Denominator = 929 probe sets rhythmic at baseline (700 rhythmic in both + 229 rhythmic at baseline only); 1,092 probe sets (1,078 transcripts, 9.7% of expressed) rhythmic in either condition; 676 = rhythmic in both conditions with similar amplitude and phase (model-selection categories i-ii; BIC weight >= 0.4); 24 probe sets showed a shifted rhythm (mostly delays); ~3.0% (331 probe sets) altered mesor only. Cosinor results: group-level rhythmic probe sets fell from 3.8% (n=444) at baseline to 0.5% (n=62) during night shift (FDR 5%); individual-level 11.8% -> 6.5%.
Important: "similar phase" is relative to habitual bedtime, i.e. behavior-referenced; in clock time the sleep was delayed by 10 h, so these transcripts were NOT carried with the shifted sleep. Press release (McGill / ScienceDaily, quoting Dr Cermakian): "Almost 25% of the rhythmic genes lost their biological rhythm", "73% did not adapt to the night shift and stayed tuned to their daytime rhythm", "less than 3% partly adapted to the night shift schedule". So the 73% in the press release and the 73% in the paper are the SAME 676/929 (stayed rhythmic AND stayed with the old/central schedule); press says "did not adapt", abstract says "remained rhythmic". Do not write "73% of rhythmic transcripts lost" or "73% of all rhythmic genes". (Press-release text read at healthenews.mcgill.ca and sciencedaily.com; EurekAlert version has no numbers.) The ~25% lost = 229/929 = 24.6% (my arithmetic; matches categories v-vi).
Design: 8 healthy subjects (7 M, 1 F; mean age 22.5, range 18-29); GEO GSE107537: "6-day simulated shift work protocol", baseline 24-h measurement (habitual sleep, then 16-h constant posture with hourly isocaloric snacks), "On days 3-6, the sleep period was delayed by 10 hours", second 24-h measurement after the third night; blood every 4 h (7 samples per period; 103 RNA samples total); PBMC, microarray. Central marker: plasma melatonin; paper states the central clock "did not adapt to the shifted schedule" in a similar protocol (cited reference, not re-measured here as per my read); cortisol not mentioned in the text I read. Sleep schedule: yes, delayed by 10 h, 8-h sleep opportunities; subjects slept on average 38 +- 10 min less per opportunity. Note: sleep, feeding and posture were all shifted together during days 3-6 (the 4-d protocol); the measurement periods themselves were constant posture with hourly snacks.

## 14. Kervezee 2019 (JBR), GSE122725
Exists. 11 healthy participants (mean age 24, range 18-30; 10 M/1 F), same 4-day simulated night shift protocol (habitual sleep delayed by 10 h). 3 exposed to bright light (~6,500 lux per paper; GEO says ~6,000 lux) for 8 h during the waking period (nightly waking), 8 control in dim light (~10 lux). Two 24-h measurement periods (baseline and day 4 after shift), blood every 4 h, PBMC transcriptome (141 samples). Result: "average phase delay in the bright light group was approximately 8 to 9 h, whereas the average phase delay in the control group was approximately 1 to 2 h". The 8 dim-light participants are the same protocol arm as GSE107537 (8 subjects; likely overlapping/identical, not verified).
