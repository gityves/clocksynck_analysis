## 1. CoFE

Ananthasubramaniam B, Venkataramanan R. "Rhythm profiling using COFE reveals multi-omic circadian rhythms in human cancers in vivo." PLoS Biol 2025 (27 May), doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3003196; PMC12136439 [FT main text; S4 Text file not readable, only its title "Analysis of TCGA data" is known]. Preprint: bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.03.13.584582 (v of 19 Dec 2024) [FT, via local pdftotext]. (The PMC page lists a single author name repeated; the bioRxiv PDF gives the two authors above.)

**Estimator** (Methods, Theory/Implementation). Sparse cyclic PCA (scPCA): rank-2 fit of the z-scored N x p matrix, X ~ d(u1 v1' + u2 v2'), with the sample scores constrained to the unit circle (u1i^2 + u2i^2 = 1), l1 bound s and l2 norm 1 on the loadings v1, v2. Features with non-zero loading are called rhythmic. Phase = arctan(u2/u1), scaled to [0,1]. The cross term of the objective is dropped (<0.1% of the error, S1I Fig/S6 Table). Alternating maximisation, local optimum only; many random restarts, smallest approximation error kept. Two cyclic components fixed; the tuned quantity is s.

**Validity signal.** Unsupervised K-fold cross-validation (entries deleted in circular pseudo-diagonal blocks, imputed by column means then by EM-like re-imputation); the CV imputation error is plotted against s. A clear interior minimum is read as "rhythms present and well sampled"; no non-trivial minimum for arrhythmic data (S2D Fig) or for samples restricted to part of the cycle (S2E-H Fig; caption: 200 samples, 2000 features of which 400 rhythmic, repeated 5-fold CV, fractions of the 24 h cycle). Optimal s scales ~ sqrt(number of rhythmic features) (S2B-C). Text: "to flag degenerate situations (no rhythms, poor sampling over a cycle) by means of the CV output" (Results, validation section). The fractions of the cycle tested and the resulting curves are in S2 Fig, not read.

**Validation.** Synthetic: N=2000 features, 400 rhythmic in the example; SNR, N and rhythmic fraction varied; metrics MAPE, precision, recall, MCC (S1-S3 Text, S1-S3 Fig); comparators CYCLOPS, PAGA, scPrisma; only COFE and CYCLOPS accurate across SNR (Results). Timed data (S1 Table, S4 Fig): mouse liver RNA-seq SRP197108, hourly over 48 h; human blood monocytes (Nanostring), accuracy 0.75 h; human skin GSE205155, accuracy 1.2 h; P. falciparum strains. All timed validation sets span the full day; per-dataset n and noise: not found in the text read. No validation on tumour data, and none with a restricted day window beyond the synthetic S2E-H.

**Tumour claims** (Results, Fig 2-4). 11 adenocarcinomas with >250 samples in one histological type: BRCA, KIRC, KIRP, PRAD, OV, UCEC, LUAD, COAD, THCA, BLCA, LIHC (per-cancer n in S2 Table: not found). ~13,000 expressed genes per cancer; 2,000 (UCEC) to >6,000 (OV) "rhythmic", independent of n (rs(9)=0.04); CV curves have clear minima in all 11 (S6D Fig). Core clock: 15 of 20 genes expressed; consensus order E-box, E/D-box, D-box, RRE spanning about half a cycle, similar to healthy tissue except mistimed NR1D2 (~8 h late), TEF, BHLHE40, PER2 (Fig 2D). Only 7 rhythmic genes shared by all 11 cancers (Fig 2E). Predicted times "lay throughout the circadian cycle in all ACs" (Fig 3A), taken as surprising because biopsies are assumed to be taken during the day; patient-matched normal vs tumour time differences (10 of 11 cancers; N per cancer about 19 to 90, Fig 3A panel labels) are spread over the cycle, tumour-vs-tumour control near 0 (S7B); hence "clocks in tumors do not have a fixed timing relationship with clocks in healthy tissue" and are "uncoupled" from the systemic clock. S/G2M scores oscillate over predicted time in all but KIRC (Fig 3C). 237/258 TCPA proteins rhythmic in at least one cancer. 51/53 FDA drugs have a rhythmic target (Fig 4).

**Caveats stated by the authors** (Discussion): no direct in vivo confirmation possible; population, not individual, rhythms; no period from TCGA, circadian status inferred from core-clock order/similarity to healthy tissue; uncoupling is a hypothesis ("cannot assess systemic clock phase"); the "collected around the clock" inference rests on the CV output and is described as unlikely under standard clinical schedules; cell-cycle override of tissue-systemic coupling "speculation"; gene-expression-based drug analysis only.

**Points relevant to PI hypothesis (i)** [ours, from the text above]: (a) the only evidence that tumour samples cover the whole day is the CV minimum itself; the same text states that whole-day coverage is implausible, and that contradiction is left unresolved. (b) A CV minimum shows low-rank circular structure, not that the axis is time; between-patient heterogeneity with circular geometry (cell-cycle phase, purity, hormone state) would give one. (c) The uniform tumour-minus-normal difference is exactly what is expected if the two labels are unrelated axes, or if one is noise. (d) No negative control from tumour-like heterogeneity (no clock) is reported in the text read; S4 Text may contain one (not read). (e) The scoped claim "peak-time order resembles a healthy clock" (core-clock order) is the part most likely to survive, since the circle is fitted by the clock-genes' own covariance only if they dominate the loadings; not shown.

## 2. CYCLOPS and CYCLOPS 2.0

**CYCLOPS.** Anafi RC, Francey LJ, Hogenesch JB, Kim J. "CYCLOPS reveals human transcriptional rhythms in health and disease." PNAS 2017;114:5312-5317, doi:10.1073/pnas.1619320114; PMC5441789 [FT, via fetch summary]. Estimator (Methods): top 10,000 probes by mean expression, winsorised at 2.5/97.5 percentiles, scaled; eigengenes (SVD) keeping 85% variance; a linear autoencoder with a single circular bottleneck (two coupled nodes on the unit circle), phase = atan2 of the node pair; SGD, batch 10, rate 0.3, momentum 0.5, 40 random restarts, lowest error kept; seed genes = human homologues of mouse genes cycling (JTK q<0.05) in Zhang et al. 2014, restricted to those cycling in >75% of mouse tissues or tissue-specific; quality: Metsmooth (<1) and Staterr (bootstrap p). Phases are relative; PAR-bZip acrophase fixes absolute time.
Stated requirements (Discussion): ">250 samples are required to order biopsy samples"; the method "requires data from the entire periodic cycle to form an ellipse"; biopsies are mostly daytime, so shift-worker or off-hours samples are needed (Fig S5). Validation (Table S2, Fig 1-2, S2): mouse liver 48 samples hourly (rho_c, eta_c >0.9); mouse atlas 24 samples/tissue (unseeded failed for muscle, cerebellum, brainstem; seeded worked); human prefrontal cortex autopsy n=202 (140 with time of death): rho_c=0.68, median abs. error 1.69 h; human lung n=445 and 499: phase consistency rho_c=0.66, ~1.2 h. No dedicated partial-coverage test (paper-stated). Tumour application: normal liver n=249 (243 adjacent to tumour) ordered; HCC n=268: seeded ordering failed, so HCC was projected onto the normal-liver eigenvectors before ordering (Fig 3A legend), then ~15% of normal-cycling transcripts lost rhythm; most core clock genes still cycle except PER1, CRY1 (Fig 3, S6). **[ours]** This HCC analysis therefore uses the normal-tissue axis to define the tumour ordering.

**CYCLOPS 2.0.** No standalone peer-reviewed methods paper found (searched PubMed-indexed titles via web search, bioRxiv). The method is described in: Li S-Y, Hammarlund JA, et al., Anafi RC, Meng Q-J. PNAS 2024;121:e2311854121, doi:10.1073/pnas.2311854121, SI Appendix Fig S3 [FT main text; SI not read]; and Hammarlund JA, PhD dissertation "CYCLOPS 2.0: improving and applying circadian phase reconstruction in confounded datasets", Drexel University (advisors Anafi, Kriete) [Abs, catalogue abstract]. Code: github.com/ranafi/tumor-circadian-clock-strength-... (listed, not inspected). Description (Li 2024): the autoencoder gets explicit confounder covariates (batch, tumour status), learning batch adjustments and a shared circular structure jointly; may use a subset of time-stamped samples with 10-fold CV to set their weight; benchmarked on real, semi-synthetic and synthetic data "with different temporal biases" (SI Fig S3B-D, not read); seed genes: orthologues of mouse mammary cycling transcripts plus transcripts cycling in >75% of mouse tissues, ~70 seeds; assumes a fixed relative phase relationship between seed genes across subjects. Coverage of the day is **not** stated as a requirement in the main text; the text notes that TCGA phases cluster in the presumed working day and GTEx is more uniform but biased (Fig 1D). Correct attribution for the user's question: CYCLOPS 2.0 = Hammarlund/Anafi lab (Penn/Drexel), first used in Li et al. 2024.

## 4. Critiques and benchmarks

- Authors' own statements: CYCLOPS needs ~250 samples and whole-cycle coverage (Anafi 2017); Shilts 2018 and Schwartz 2023 repeat this; Schwartz also admits a coverage gap in n=50 normals and reads ordering failure in PDAC as disruption while conceding it "may be a limitation of CYCLOPS" (Discussion) **[ours]**: conflicts with our rule that a failed gate is uninformative.
- Independent benchmarks with partial coverage: none found. Closest: Shilts 2018 (co-expression method, not ordering) detects signal with 8 h coverage in mouse; COFE S2E-H (synthetic, CV flat when coverage partial; fractions not read).
- Third-party comparisons, all in method papers [Abs, search snippets]: DCPR (Springer BMC Bioinformatics 2025, doi:10.1186/s12859-025-06363-2) reports CHIRAL and DCPR more robust than Cyclum and CYCLOPS on non-uniform datasets and notes that systematic quantitative comparison of robustness is lacking; Tempo (Auerbach et al., Nat Commun 2022, doi:10.1038/s41467-022-34185-w) reports Cyclum and CYCLOPS unstable in single-cell simulations; PROTECT (iScience 2025) reports CYCLOPS fails on proteomics. These were not read in full.
- COFE vs CYCLOPS: only COFE's own synthetic comparison (Results, S3 Fig).
- No published critique of the COFE TCGA analysis found (search 2026-10-25); PMC page shows no comments.

Concrete published claims we can test against:
1. COFE: CV minimum implies whole-day coverage and valid time labels (Results Fig 3A/S6D); test with 12 h window and with a no-clock heterogeneity null.
2. COFE: tumour-minus-normal label differences uniform means "uncoupled" (Fig 3B); test what a coupled-with-patient-offset clock gives under label estimation error and window truncation.
3. COFE: core-clock order resembles healthy except NR1D2 (+8 h), TEF, BHLHE40, PER2 (Fig 2D); test whether this order is recoverable under heterogeneity or is an eigen-structure of clock-gene co-variation.
4. COFE: 2,000-6,000 of 13,000 genes rhythmic, independent of n (rs=0.04); under a null, does the rhythmic fraction also stay n-independent?
5. COFE: S/G2M cell-cycle scores oscillate over predicted time (Fig 3C); a purity or proliferation axis would produce this under the null.
6. CYCLOPS 2.0 (Li 2024): tumour phases poorly correlated with matched normal; CYCLOPS magnitude predicts survival (P=0.047); magnitude is a radial distance and thus tied to eigengene scale and purity.
7. CYCLOPS (Anafi 2017, Schwartz 2023): ordering failure indicates disrupted clock (PDAC p=0.43); test whether failure arises from 12 h coverage or small n with an intact clock.
8. Shilts 2018: dCCD>0 in 20/20 datasets; coupling scale with purity (already close to our spread-dependent coupling).

### 6a. What Li et al. 2024 / CYCLOPS 2.0 did about partial day coverage

**Short answer: the PI's belief is not what the paper describes.** We found no passage in which GTEx (or other healthy whole-day samples) were added to tumours to fill missing hours, and no assumption of shared Fourier coefficients between tumour and healthy tissue. What was done, in four separate pieces:

1. **Healthy reference set = non-cancerous samples only, from three sources, ordered jointly.** GTEx breast N=167 (2 batch sites), TCGA normal N=106 (4 sites), Manchester N=26 timed, 1 site (Table 4.2.2.1, p. 62). Quote (4.2.2, p. 61): "We used informatic tools to integrate RNAseq data from newly collected time-stamped breast samples (N = 26, with noncancerous samples with >20 million reads) with RNAseq data from female breast samples in public databases. We incorporated data from TCGA and GTEx ... We did not include samples collected in centers where only a small number (n < 5) of noncancerous samples were processed." The purpose is stated as batch/bias handling, not coverage: "Differences between sample collection sites, processing methods, and patient populations complicate the use of aggregate data. These differences may be particularly problematic when different centers have different biases in collection time." (p. 61-62).
2. **Temporal bias is handled by covariates, not by filling.** 3.3.1 (p. 27): "Biopsies are routine clinical procedures commonly scheduled between working hours of the day, often between 6AM and 6PM. Death has no temporal constraint ... This creates a temporal bias between the datasets, where the biopsies favor daytime hours compared to autopsy data." Remedy (p. 27-28): "a covariate-aware encoding that could be incorporated with CYCLOPS to learn the effects of annotated covariate groups and rhythmic patterns simultaneously"; "Specific scale and offset factors are fit for each covariate group (column) and eigengene (row)." Result (4.2.2, p. 63-64, Fig. 1D): "As expected, the sample phases assigned to the TCGA biopsies were concentrated during the presumed working day ... In contrast, phases assigned to the autopsy-derived GTEx samples was more uniformly distributed, but still showed a significant bias." So coverage of the day is assumed to come from the GTEx autopsies inside the **normal** set; this is an observation after ordering, not a stated requirement or test.
3. **Timed anchors are a secondary loss, only the 26 Manchester normals.** (4.2.2, p. 63): "Finally, we allowed the ordering process to use information from a subset of time-stamped samples. We performed 10-fold cross-validation to determine the relative weight given to predicting time in these samples and identify a common circular structure for all samples." Validation: phase vs recorded time for these 26, CorrFisher = 0.69, pFisher < 0.01 (Fig. 4.2.2.2/1C legend). Note these 26 were in the training objective, so this is an in-sample-weighted check; whether the CV weight was applied out-of-sample is **not stated in the text read** (SI not read). No timed tumour was used as an anchor.
4. **Tumours were not ordered with healthy samples filling hours; they were projected onto the normal eigenspace and given their own parameters.** (4.2.4, p. 82-83): "There is likely significant noncircadian heterogeneity among luminal A samples. We projected the luminal A data onto the eigengene space computed from the noncancerous samples to emphasize circadian variation. Using the CYCLOPS 2.0 model, we included data from both noncancerous and luminal A samples, now listing tumor status as a covariate." Ch. 6 (p. 171) restates: "we performed SVD on the expression matrix of normal margin samples and used the resulting eigengenes to transform the tumor samples into this biologically meaningful space". Covariate coding (4.3.3, p. 102-103): batch (7 batches) plus a binary tumour-status flag, i.e. tumour samples get their own offset and scale per eigengene (a tumour-specific linear map into the shared circle). Downstream, rhythm parameters are tumour-specific: "Separate models were fit to luminal A (red) and noncancerous samples (blue)" and nested regression "tests the importance of tumor-dependent cycling parameters while accounting for tumor-dependent differences in mean expression" (p. 83). So tumour-specific amplitudes/acrophases are allowed in the readout; what is **shared** is the eigenspace and the circular phase axis (seed genes assumed to keep a fixed relative phase relationship: "assumes a fixed relative phase relationship between the cycling seed genes across subjects (e.g., BMAL1 precedes NR1D1 by a relatively fixed amount in the oscillations in each sample)", p. 125-126).
5. **Samples in tumour ordering:** luminal A N=193 (TCGA) + 18 (Manchester timed) (Table at p. 80-81, Fig. 3 legend). Tumours restricted to "those samples collected from the centers that contribute to the non-tumor sample cohort" (Methods, p. 122-123).

**Validation reported.** (i) Synthetic and semi-synthetic benchmarks "with different temporal biases" (BA11 brain + lung data; 400 synthetic samples, 300+100 two-centre designs; Figs 3.4.1.1, 3.4.2.2, 3.4.3.1): CYCLOPS 2.0 "demonstrates improved accuracy with realistic levels of noncircadian noise" (p. 63); benchmark figures not examined in detail, and they concern batch plus time-biased **healthy** data, not tumour-vs-normal differences in coefficients. (ii) 26 timed normals, CorrFisher 0.69. (iii) Quality metrics for the normal ordering: Statsmooth = 0.75, Staterror = 0.015. (iv) For tumours: no timed tumour check. The text states plainly that TCGA contains few matched pairs: "Of note, TCGA includes a limited number of matched luminal A tumors and noncancerous samples from the same patient" (p. ~83), and that tumour phases overall do not track the matched normal phase except in the top CMag tertile (Fig. 4.2.4.5; p. 89: "Unlike luminal A samples as a whole ..., the circadian molecular phases assigned to high CMag luminal A samples generally match the phases assigned to their noncancerous pair ... This suggests that higher CMag in luminal A samples is indicative of a more robust clock").

**Implications for A138 [ours].**
- The CYCLOPS 2.0 tumour pipeline assumes (a) the normal eigenspace carries the circadian circle for tumours, (b) one common phase axis, with tumour offset/scale per eigengene. It does not require tumour and normal to share gene amplitudes or phases, but the phase of a tumour is defined by its projection onto a normal-derived circle, so the ordering is anchored in the normal set (including 26 timed samples and autopsy GTEx). It is **not** a "GTEx fills the missing hours" design.
- Day coverage of the tumour set itself is never tested; the authors note it is working-day biased (Fig. 1D, TCGA phases). No coverage criterion (as in Anafi 2017) is applied, and no timed tumours validate the phase axis. Tumour-tumour phase spread is therefore not validated; only magnitude and gene-level cycling are interpreted.
- Residual risk to test in A138: a tumour-status offset/scale per eigengene lets a non-circadian low-rank axis be absorbed into the circle; "CMag" is a radial distance in that space (see Section 5, claim 6).
- The PI's reading (shared Fourier coefficients) is nearest to the dissertation's **proposed refinement**, not to the paper: Ch. 6 (p. 171) suggests restricting the projection reference to high-integrity samples ("include only GTEx samples from individuals classified as experiencing rapid death according to the Hardy scale, in addition to the TCGA normal margin samples"). That is future work in the dissertation, not done in Li 2024.
- A related shared-coefficient assumption does appear in Ch. 5 (GTEx cQTL analysis, p. 156): GTEx tissue samples were ordered with the matched human time-course samples "treating the time course data as equivalent to GTEx samples in the 'acute' death group ... valid because acute deaths likely have little to no amplitude decay in oscillating genes, similar to living individuals". This is healthy tissue only (adipose, muscle, skin), not tumour.

The only paper that explicitly rejects CYCLOPS-type ordering on coverage grounds is Altman 2025 (blood, row 7) and Shilts 2018 (Section 3 row 3).

**Not found:** (i) a standalone CYCLOPS 2.0 methods paper or bioRxiv preprint (the 2023 bioRxiv of the breast paper carries it); (ii) any use of a healthy whole-day reference to fill tumour hours, apart from the normal-eigenspace projection above; (iii) single-cell or spatial tumour ordering with CYCLOPS; (iv) the Li 2024 SI text itself (Fig S3 benchmarks) beyond the dissertation's reproduction; (v) per-figure sample counts for tumours in the SI.

**Suggested wording for the PI [ours]:** "In Li 2024 healthy GTEx and TCGA normals are ordered together with 26 timed biopsies, with batch covariates; tumours are then projected onto the eigenspace of the normals and carry a tumour covariate. Tumours do not share the normal's gene amplitudes or phases, but they do share the normal-defined circular axis, and the whole-day coverage of the tumours is neither assumed nor tested."
