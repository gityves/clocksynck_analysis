## Framing: three levels of clock organisation

The paper's thesis is robustness and its limits: a person's tissues share one internal time, this organisation resists age, chronic metabolic disease and loss of daily cues, and it breaks locally (brain partly apart, tumour states, psoriatic skin). "Embryo to elderly" is not the spine, because the embryo (A121) and ageing (A19, A130) arms are currently fails or nulls.

Every figure reports the same three levels, so that coherence (cancer) and coupling (organs) stop looking like two unrelated statistics:

| Level | What it measures | Readout in the repo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1, gene order within a compartment | do the 14 clock genes keep their waveform relations across donors | coherence R (`decompose.components`), `log_plane` |
| L2, compartment to host | how much of a compartment's clock variance follows the person's host state | Δc, d\*, z², ψ (never absolute c with intervals) |
| L3, organ phase relations | do organs keep their healthy offsets | arg z, Fisher-Lee ρ, folded distance to 0/12 h |

For each condition, the question is which level breaks. Current reading, to be confirmed by the tests below: cues lost leaves L1 and the shared state intact but displaces L3; metabolic disease leaves L2 unchanged (A164); ER− and proliferating tumours of the replicated types (breast, lung adenocarcinoma, gastric) lose L1, while L2 to the patient's normal tissue survives in TCGA overall (H138.4). Whether it survives in the ER− and proliferating tumours themselves is C1.

**Caveat on L1.** Coherence R is each gene's leave-one-out phase matched against the waveform, so it depends on signal-to-noise. A lower R can mean disordered clock relations, or intact clocks swamped by non-clock variance. C3 separates the two before any "clock loss" wording. New registrations should name the level they test.

## Priority 1: cancer with the host-referenced method

The strongest human positive (L1 coherence loss in ER− and proliferating breast, lung adenocarcinoma and gastric tumours) does not use clocksync's host referencing, and a reviewer will ask why the method is needed. Tumour and adjacent normal tissue from one operation are exactly the untimed, hospital-hours, within-person design the method was built for. These tests put L2 behind Figure 5.

1. **C1. Does the tumour follow its own normal tissue less when it is ER− or proliferating?** (highest priority)
   - Data: TCGA tumour–normal pairs from H138.4 (517 pairs, 10 gated cancers); BRCA split by ER; all cancers split by proliferation tertile within cancer.
   - Readout: the H138.4 pairing statistic per stratum, difference between strata with a label-permutation null within cancer; pseudo-clock and responsive companions as in H138.4.
   - Power first: BRCA tumour–normal pairs with an ER− tumour are few (count them before registering the ER split). Proliferation tertiles within cancer are the better-powered arm.
   - Status: secondary (TCGA is seen). State both readings in advance: lower pairing means the L1 loss comes with loss of host following; unchanged pairing means coherence is lost inside a tumour that still tracks the person. Both are publishable.
   - Replication (primary): GSE14520 HCC (213 pairs, on disk); GSE66229 gastric (verify that normals are paired before registering); scout CPTAC cohorts with normal adjacent tissue (LUAD first) and any paired LUAD or gastric GEO series. Run the MDE step first and stop a cohort if MDE exceeds the TCGA stratum difference.
2. **C2. Dropped (PI, 2026-10-31).** Shared sampling time is the clock signal itself, not a confound of the tumour–normal pairing. The responsive-gene null (H138.4-R) already covers shared acute non-clock responses. Do not reintroduce a "time-of-day confound" test.
3. **C3. Swamped or disordered: in-plane vs off-plane variance.** Already proposed in STATE section 5; register before reading.
   - Data: SCAN-B, METABRIC, GSE72094, GSE68465, GSE62254, GSE15459.
   - Readout: `log_plane` and off-plane variance by ER and proliferation. Falling in-plane share with rising off-plane variance means "swamped" (intact clocks, less clock variance); in-plane variance held while R falls means "disordered".
4. **C4. Is the proliferation effect cell-cycle locking of the clock?** Coupled-oscillator work shows the mammalian clock can phase-lock to the cell cycle, with the locking ratio depending on division rate (see Literature). If so, the loss is a mechanism, not a confound.
   - Single cell: in malignant cells (A87 set, 79 patients; Wu + Pal breast; a LUAD atlas with malignant labels, scout first) infer per-cell cell-cycle phase and test whether clock-gene state depends on cell-cycle phase, and whether that dependence is stronger in high-proliferation tumours.
   - Bulk: regress clock state on G1/S and G2/M scores; test whether conditioning on cell-cycle composition restores coherence.
   - Add a proliferation-responsive pseudo pool (genes as cell-cycle sensitive as the clock genes) to every coherence test; the current pool excludes them.
5. **C5. Within-tumour L2 by tumour state.** Do malignant cells couple less to their own tumour stroma (fibroblast, endothelial host) in ER− or high-proliferation tumours?
   - H138.1 had MDE about 0.2, so start with the power step on pooled cohorts. Stop if MDE on d\* exceeds 0.3; report descriptively.
6. **C6. A blind test of type specificity (PI, 2026-10-31).** The breast, lung and gastric types were chosen after
   TCGA values, and nearly every TCGA adenocarcinoma has already been scored. Do the following:
   - Register blind GEO replications (cohorts ≥ 150 tumours, never read) of cancer types whose TCGA value was weak
     or null: colorectal, pancreatic, endometrial and prostate. Add squamous controls: head and neck, cervical,
     oesophageal squamous, and lung squamous from cohorts not read in A173.
   - State each type's seen TCGA value in the registration.
   - Predictions: the replicated types lose coherence; these types do not, unless the TCGA value was a
     false negative.
   - The test tells whether the effect is type-specific rather than histology-specific.
7. **C7. Robustness still missing.** Leave-one-gene-out of all 14 genes in SCAN-B and METABRIC; re-score the two cohorts sitting at the pseudo 5 % line (GSE72094, GSE62254) with a larger pseudo pool (≥ 1,000 sets) and report the percentile with its interval.
8. **C8. Internal time of the tumour as a patient variable** \[PI: outcomes wait until C1 and C3 are in, then ask again\]. Replicate A146 in a second cohort with recorded resection times; then test whether internal time or tumour-to-stroma offset (L3) relates to endocrine response or survival. SCAN-B showed no survival association for coherence (HR 0.94, p 0.35), so frame this as exploratory.

## Priority 2: hospital-hours material beyond GTEx

The single-cell disease atlases cannot detect a 0.2 change in coupling (A153: no compartment with MDE ≤ 0.2). The fix is not more single-cell atlases but larger bulk within-person designs from routine clinical sampling, where host referencing has enough donors. Note that A123's "working hours" is a GTEx subset; it simulates the use case and must not be presented as hospital material.

1. **H1. Paired within-person bulk biopsies.** Scout GEO/ArrayExpress for series with two or more sites or states per patient from one procedure: IBD inflamed vs uninflamed (and ileum vs colon), psoriasis lesional vs uninvolved (A115 already suggests a trait component), tumour vs normal (C1). Requirements to list in the scout: patients with ≥ 2 compartments (required; designs with one compartment per person are out), total n, platform, and whether collection time is recorded. Register the MDE on d\* before any contrast; target MDE ≤ 0.15.
2. **H2. Internal time vs recorded time on clinical samples.** Every cohort with recorded collection times (A146 breast, V25.5 breast, any new one found in H1) gets the A113-style internal-time estimate and the comparison with recorded time. This is the hospital-material validation that A123c could not give (GTEx truth reliability 0.54). \[PI\] Ask whether the local hospital biobank can record excision time on future samples; that would give a prospective truth.
3. **H3. Anaesthesia, surgery duration and ischaemia as acute perturbations.** Rodent studies report anaesthetic-induced phase shifts of peripheral clocks, and perioperative dexamethasone is routine. Scout cohorts with ischaemia time, surgery duration or anaesthesia metadata (CPTAC records some procurement times). Readout: L3 offset and the responsive-gene state against these covariates. This is the living-patient counterpart of A136 (draft only) and of the ventilated-donor result.
4. **H4. Transplantation: does a graft re-couple to a new host?** Scout serial biopsy series (kidney or liver transplant: implantation or time-zero biopsy, then protocol biopsies) with donor and recipient blood or a second recipient tissue. Prediction: at time zero the graft carries the donor state; later it follows the recipient. Feasibility risk: a recipient reference is usually blood, whose clock is weak (archive A35, rho 0.31). Prefer series with a second recipient solid tissue, and check the gate on the reference before anything else. This question exists only with untimed clinical material and fits the host-referenced readout directly. Scout feasibility first: number of patients with ≥ 2 timepoints and a recipient reference compartment.
5. **H5. ICU patients.** The GTEx ventilated result (clock-shaped shared state, displaced toward 0/12 h) is a prediction for living ICU patients. Scout sepsis or ICU cohorts with more than one compartment (sorted cell types or paired tissues); single-compartment blood is L1 only. Low priority unless a multi-compartment cohort exists.

## Priority 3: development and lifespan

The developmental end should be a principled negative rather than a failed positive. Human multi-organ fetal data stop around 20–22 post-conception weeks, so the arc jumps: no clock at mid-gestation, maternal entrainment at term, maturation in childhood, robust organisation in adults, local breaks in disease.

1. **D1. Descartes fetal atlas (GSE156793) as a real-data negative control.** 28 fetuses, 72–129 days post-conception, 15 organs, sci-RNA-seq3 nuclei. Rodent data suggest peripheral clocks are not yet running at a comparable stage.
   - Scout first: per organ × fetus pseudobulk counts for the 14 clock genes; clock-output genes (DBP, NR1D1/2, PER3) relative to ARNTL; donors with ≥ 3 organs.
   - Depth control (required before reading the gate): downsample an adult multi-organ atlas to Descartes counts per pseudobulk and show the gate still passes there. Without this, a failed gate is uninformative by the project's own rule.
   - Registered predictions: the clock gate fails; coupling is not above the pseudo-clock pool; a shared cross-organ state may exist on responsive (glucocorticoid / IEG) genes, reflecting maternal signals. That last pattern is the mirror image of A109 (clock-shaped state without cues vs cue-driven state without a clock).
   - Optional: age trend of clock-output expression across 72–129 days, descriptive.
   - Check download size against the data cap first (367 of 400 GB used; candidates to free: `scout_endocrine/` 14 GB after A168/A169, `m3_snapshot_backup/` 2.5 GB; propose, do not delete). Take only the processed count matrices needed for pseudobulks.
2. **D2. Mother–fetus coupling at term.** Scout series with placenta and/or cord blood (fetal) plus decidua and/or maternal blood (maternal) from the same delivery. Question: does the near-term fetus follow the mother's internal time (L2 with the mother as host)? Labour vs no labour (planned caesarean) is a design factor, not a nuisance. Check first what archive A105–A108c already did with placenta.
3. **D3. Childhood maturation from routine surgical tissue.** Scout paediatric series spanning infancy to adolescence (tonsillectomy, paediatric gut resections or biopsies, congenital heart surgery tissue). Readouts: L1 coherence and within-organ L2 (epithelium vs stroma/endothelium) against age. This bridges the fetus and GTEx, which starts at 20.
4. **D4. Ageing, made citable.** Re-verify in this repo the archive finding that the apparent age dampening shrinks from −0.73 pooled to −0.13 when matched on death type (Hardy); it is currently "not re-verified". If it holds, it is a provocative, publishable statement against published age dampening. Pair it with the A19 null stated as an equivalence bound (TOST) rather than "no effect".
5. **D5. Mouse as the developmental scaffold.** Keep A108 (late embryo) and add postnatal mouse series if available, so the human points (D1–D3) sit on a continuous mouse trajectory. A121 stays in Extended Data unless new data change its power.
