## 1. Where the paper stood, where it stands

**On 2026-11-15/16.** The target line ("what internal time controls") had no replicated headline (Q33, Q34). The
biology-paper thesis ("a robust network with specific exceptions") lost two of its exceptions (brain as a clock
statement, fetal adrenal first); Q35 recommended the Nature Methods ordering. The amplitude-coupling relation was
unexplained and appeared to contradict oscillator theory; the atlas couplings failed the stress-responsive control; the
death-type results were archive results without companions.

**Now.** A coherent account of the clock's organisation in the body, with one human result no timed design can give
(who carries the time inside organs), an explanation of the amplitude-coupling relation, and the organ arrangement
shown to be independent of alignment to the day, clock-specifically. No mechanism, no perturbation; part of the story
rests on deaths. The venue choice remains the PI's (journal choice is outward-facing): the biology case is now real;
Nature Methods remains the fallback.

## 2. Claims by tier

**Tier 1 (living or living-validated data, replicated, clock-specific).**
1. A person's tissues share one internal time that advances with elapsed hours (A251-A256). Clock-specific against
   matched genes everywhere; against stress-responsive genes only where enough pairs exist (surgical fat-skin, A285).
2. Within organs, the time is carried by stromal-vascular, epithelial and parenchymal cells and macrophages; tissue
   lymphoid cells carry no readable clock, whatever their residency (three independent atlas sources, calibrated test,
   living tissue alone; A272-A288). They share glucocorticoid-target and immediate-early states with their tissue,
   not the clock state (A275).
3. Cell types of an organ share its phase (about 0.6 h; unbiased against timed mouse colon and aorta, A273, A279);
   composition changes gain, not phase (within 0.13 h per SD; stromal-vascular content raises gain, predicted, reported
   not tested; A295).
4. Each cell class keeps the same gain relative to endothelium in every organ (tau 0.10 to 0.31, A293).

**Tier 2 (GTEx deaths, clock-specific).**
5. Tissues express the shared time with 85-fold different gain over a clock-specific personal departure of nearly
   constant size (2.6 times expression-matched genes, A294b); this explains "stronger clocks are more coupled": coupling =
   S / (S + D), and a fixed-size perturbation turns a strong clock less (oscillator theory). Measurement excluded (A291).
   The departure is random in direction between donors, about 1 h in strong and 3 h in weak tissues, independent of
   individual clock periods (slope near 0, A294b correction note), not shared among meal-driven tissues (A302), cause open.
6. The organ arrangement holds without alignment to the external day (slow deaths, rho 0.784) and without meals or
   activity (ventilated living-brain donors, 0.690), above all 200 matched and 200 responsive sets; lost in brain-dead
   donors (0.161, descriptive by decision) (A293).

**Mouse re-analysis.** Peripheral clocks are self-sustained: removing the feeding rhythm keeps 0.72 of the liver
amplitude (additive cues predict 0.15) and moves it 1.6 h; inverting it moves the liver 11.7 h (A297).

**Supported, worded with care.** Gain is probably not composition alone (argument: classes span 1.9-fold, tissues 3.1,
the same class 1.3 to 2.7 between organs; the direct test A298 was closed for data quality). Organ offsets equal across
sex, age, metabolic disease within about 1 h (A208, A231, A235). Brain and body are two internally coherent domains
linked weakly for every between-person state, not specifically the clock (A263, A289; descriptive).

**Cancer chapter (main text, user 2026-11-18; updated with A304-A305).** Tumour and adjacent normal share the patient's
clock state (A138; COFE's opposite reading is a sampling artefact, A265, A267); in tumours, fibroblasts and endothelium
keep it, immune cells do not beyond the responsive family (A227, A271): the participation rule of healthy organs. As
tumours proliferate they express less of the patient's time and do not drift from it: on the patient's own normal
sample the tumour gain falls to 0.63 in the high-proliferation half (A304 H304.1, p 0.0055, Holm 0.011, below both
companion families), the departure does not rise (powered FAIL); not explained by purity (the proliferative arm has more
stroma). TP53 and luminal A show no departure rise (FAIL); ER not powered. Tumour coupling to the body's time after
GTEx disattenuation (descriptive): lung adenocarcinoma 0.54, liver 0.38, kidney
clear-cell 0.27, prostate 0.26, breast 0.18, papillary kidney 0.12, thyroid 0.11, colon 0.06 (A304 A3). Whether malignant
cells carry a weaker clock or less of the tumour RNA comes from clock-carrying cells is not resolvable with public
single-cell data (A214; A305 not powered: 2 cohorts, MDE 0.156).

**Disease in the gain / departure view (A306).** T2D (metabolic organs), hypertension (arteries, heart) and smoking
(lung) change neither the gain nor the departure of their organ relative to the rest of the body (5 powered readouts,
MDE 0.26-0.42 on the log scale); with couplings and offsets already equal (A164, A233, A235), chronic disease leaves the
organ-level organisation intact. Lung gain lower in smokers (-0.51, p 0.068), not powered.

**Fetal: a driven state before a running clock (reframed; no new statistic).** Between fetuses, clock genes covary
in a clock-shaped way in the adrenal cortex (A244), lung and liver (A243), at one half to three quarters of the adult
size, and not in heart (powered at half the adult size) or kidney. The covariation is carried by the signal-induced
clock genes: without PER1, PER2 and NR1D1 it is not significant in the adrenal cortex (A266) or lung, and not
clock-specific in liver (A269); it is not a smoother age trend, cohort slope, steroidogenic or glucocorticoid-target
activity (A264, A269). The fetal cortex carries the ACTH receptor complex at adult or higher levels and no detectable
melatonin receptor (A266). This is the sequence the animal literature describes as maternally (systemically) driven
before autonomous (`docs/literature/fetal_clocks_animal_2026-11-18.md`): fetal adrenal rhythmic in rat and capuchin
(whole gland); mouse liver and kidney flat in vivo at E18-19 yet rhythmic as explants (cells able to oscillate, not
synchronised in the fetus); fetal rhythms phase-set by maternal glucocorticoid, melatonin and feeding; most evidence of
fetal "oscillation" rests on single signal-inducible reporters. The human data add organ and cell resolution (cortex,
not medulla; lung and liver, not heart or kidney) and show the systemic drive reaching fetal organs before a core loop
of adult-like size is detectable (three quarters of the adult size excluded in the adrenal cortex, half in lung; a
smaller entrained loop is not excluded). Not blind; no sampling times. The time-free test of a running oscillator
(spliced / unspliced phase relations) needs FASTQ mapping or an author release: the user's decision. The
glucocorticoid-pattern check (A307) qualifies the "driven" reading: in adrenal cortex and lung, PER1, PER2 and NR1D1
covary with the core genes in the clock's phase pattern beyond matched genes (H307.2 PASS x2); their link to GC-target
activity is clock-specific in lung only (cortex not beyond matched genes; liver neither), and NR1D1 does not load with
the GC targets. The signal-induced genes do not move alone: either an entrained loop or an input moving the whole
network in its clock pattern; times or spliced / unspliced counts would decide.

## 3. Not statements (tested or examined, not supported)
