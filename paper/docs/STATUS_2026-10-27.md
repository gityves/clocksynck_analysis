## 0. The method in two paragraphs

Each donor gives one sample per tissue or cell type, at an unknown time. For every donor we build clock-gene pseudobulks
per compartment (tissue or cell type). Across donors, the compartments of the same person co-vary along the shape the
circadian clock imposes on these genes: the known gene-to-gene phase and amplitude pattern, the "waveform". From this
shared covariance the method estimates a latent per-donor clock state, the **host**. It then reads each compartment
against the host with three numbers:
- **coupling:** how much of the compartment's clock variation follows the host (0 to 1);
- **relative amplitude:** how strong its clock is relative to the host;
- **phase offset:** how many hours it runs ahead of or behind the host.

No sample is ever assigned a clock time. So samples taken only during working hours, or at unrecorded times, can be
used, and constant shifts cancel. A model-free **gate** tests first whether the shared covariance between compartments
is clock-shaped. It does this by comparing against permuted gene phases ("pseudo-clocks") and against shuffled donor
pairings. If the gate fails, the data are "not estimable": we never call that "decoupled". The host is built on
**anchor** compartments, chosen by a frozen, learned rule: stromal and vascular cells in the periphery. A target is
always read on a host that excludes it.

Comparing groups needs care, because coupling rises with how widely donors are spread over the day. Group comparisons
are therefore made either at a common spread (d*) or on spread-free quantities.

## 2. What failed, and why

For each failure: **power** (the design could not have detected a plausible effect), **wrong hypothesis** (the effect
was there to be seen and is absent or opposite), or **method / design** (the test itself or the tool could not work).

### 2.1 Likely wrong hypotheses (informative failures)

- **Ventilated donors keep the healthy phase relations (H109.2).** rho 0.261, p 0.165 (registered power: 80 % at rho
  0.29, so near the limit). The second criterion is clearly not met: relations are pulled toward 0 / 12 h (p 0.001).
  The data favour "shared displacement" over "preserved clocks", and Figure 3 was reframed accordingly (your decision
  Q9 a). So this failure is mostly real, with a little power uncertainty on the correlation itself.
- **Stronger clocks couple less (A104 H104.1).** Opposite sign (rho +0.73). Wrong hypothesis; the reverse is now the
  post hoc reading.
- **Liver coupling falls specifically under daytime feeding (H111.2).** The liver falls (d -0.556), but fat and lung
  fall as much (-0.753, -0.736), and the timed reference shows no matching change. The effect is not liver-specific:
  wrong hypothesis, or a readout without specificity.
- **The cue-following versus locked split of genes is validated in mouse (H112.1).** Wrong sign (+0.230, p 0.91).
  The claim was dropped from Figure 1 and Figure 3.
- **Oncogenic programmes explain malignant incoherence (H134.2).** Estimates point the other way (-0.092, p 0.96).
  Wrong hypothesis.
- **A single brain reference compartment (A135).** No family qualifies, and the subsample check changes the winner.
  This is a property of the data (cortex has no stable reference), not a lack of power: 5 cohorts, up to 254 donors.
- **Psoriasis held-out variance and gene criteria (archive A54 H54.1, H54.3).** The delay replicates (H54.2); these two
  details did not. Probably real heterogeneity across series.

### 2.2 Power or design limits (uninformative failures)

- **Tabula Sapiens replication (A109b).** 12 donors, at most 5 shared per organ pair. The null floor is already
  0.53-0.68, and the MDE (0.104) is larger than any plausible excess. Uninformative.
- **Wall-time alignment in Hardy 4 (H109c.1).** R 0.097 vs null 0.101. We can exclude entrainment as strong as in fast
  deaths, not as strong as in ventilated donors (80 % power needs R 0.208). Power.
- **Brain death vs ventilation (A132 H132.1, H132.2).** MDEs of 0.44-0.54 h, larger than the ventilated-minus-fast gap.
  The direction is as predicted for ventilation duration (-0.19 h, p 0.155). Power.
- **Internal time vs age in living people (H130.1).** -0.34 h per decade, p 0.054 (54 subjects). Direction and size as
  in the epidemiology. Power.
- **Feeding route between digestive organs (H110.5).** Only 2 within-digestive tissue pairs; it carries no weight.
- **Stromal fraction predicts retained coupling (H110.2b).** Opposite sign, but only 7 tissues. Not read.
- **Brain disease contrasts (A141).** 8 of 12 contrasts change sign with the anchor choice no more than sampling noise
  predicts, so they are not estimable at this design. The other 4 depend on the anchors beyond noise. Brain donors die
  at any hour, and contrasts rest on small control groups. Mostly power and design. A request for brain time-of-death
  fields is drafted (Q12, not sent).
- **No hypothalamus exception in fast deaths (A143 H143.3).** Hypothalamus alignment with the body 0.582 vs about 0.66
  for the median other brain region: difference -0.082 [-0.248, 0.152], p 0.74. The interval is wide, so a moderate
  hypothalamus advantage is not excluded: mostly power. Regions spread from 0.37 (frontal cortex) to 0.81
  (hippocampus); the spinal cord follows the body (1.18). Whether that spread exceeds noise was not tested.
- **Brain-specific isolation batch effect (A143 H143.2).** Not detected (p 0.19). This failure is the desired outcome
  for the brain claim; it is limited by power (30 brain pairs), and excludes only an excess above about 0.07.
- **Which per-cell property carries coupling (A144 part 2, descriptive).** Per-cell amplitude 1.15 [0.46, 2.08] vs
  within-sample synchrony 1.88 [0.75, 2.83]; difference 0.73 [-0.48, 1.90]. 33 compartments, mostly brain: power. It
  cannot separate "stronger single-cell oscillators" from "better synchronised cell populations".
- **Tumour immune compartments, GSE266919 (A140 Q2).** The shared covariance between immune cell types of a biopsy is
  real (pairing p 0.001) but not clock-shaped (clock share 0.025 vs null 0.028). In health, lymphocytes barely couple.
  So either there is no shared clock signal there, or it is below detection. Reported as not estimable. This is the
  situation where an orderer that is not gated for clock shape can still produce concordant phases.

### 2.3 Method or design failures (the tool or the test could not work)

- **Feeding offset test in the Manella study (H111.1).** With 3 tissues the smallest attainable p is 0.167: it could
  never pass. A registration flaw, now prevented by stating the minimum attainable p in every registration (your Q9 d).
- **A circular-part estimator to separate clocks from displacement (A133).** It fails its simulation gate at n 79 (r
  with truth 0.646 < 0.8). Not added to clocksync. The same simulations helped read A132b (1.3).
- **CYCLOPS 2.0 on the tumour-immune design (A140 Q1, revised run today).** This is a failure of the published
  pipeline in simulation, not of our method.
  - Positive control: one clock shared by three compartments, samples over the full day, one non-clock patient
    factor, 1,500 seed genes, 80 models per fit as in the template.
  - **Full factor size (50 replicates):** CYCLOPS phases vs true time, median circular r 0.060 (90th percentile 0.178,
    best 0.273); 0 of 50 reach the 0.7 gate. Its three compartment phases still agree with each other (median r 0.967),
    and about 540 genes per compartment are called rhythmic on that ordering. CYCLOPS orders the samples along the
    shared non-clock factor, and the agreement between compartments comes from that factor, not from a clock.
  - **Factor at half size (14 replicates, descriptive):** median 0.079, concordance 0.979. Same picture.
  - **No factor (20 replicates, finished at 13:12 today):** CYCLOPS crashes in all 20. Without a dominant non-clock
    axis, no principal component of the 1,500 seed genes carries the 5 % of variance that CYCLOPS requires before it
    keeps a dimension. Its code then fails on an empty selection (Julia `MethodError` in `SVDReduceDimensions`).
  - Consequence: in this design the clock is too diluted among 1,500 seed genes for CYCLOPS. With a factor it orders
    by the factor; without one it cannot start.
  - Our gate on the same data passes 20 of 20 without the factor, 11 of 14 at half size and 24 of 50 at full size.
    So clocksync separates "clock present" correctly when the non-clock factor is small, but it loses sensitivity when
    a strong shared non-clock factor is present (24 of 50, below the registered 35 of 50). That is a limitation to
    state, and possibly to quantify (suggestion 4).
- **Age covariate model calibration (A19, fixed 2026-10-26).** The model-based null was miscalibrated on real count
  tables (false-positive rate up to 0.22). The permutation null is now the default; the primaries did not change.

   Keeping it in the confirmatory Holm family costs power for H138.1 and H138.4 and will very likely end in "not
