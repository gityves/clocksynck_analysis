## Answers to the specific questions

1. Organs with prenatal rhythms: adrenal (rat, capuchin; whole gland), liver (rat, Per1-based; mouse only ex vivo), SCN (late). Lung: no study found. Sheep: no clock-gene organ study found in my searches (only circadian physiology: prolactin, myometrial EMG, heart-rate studies). Kidney and heart: rhythmic only in E18 mouse explants.
2. Reporter issues: Ohta 2008 (Per1), Landgraf 2015, Inada 2014, Nikhil 2026 (all PER2::LUC) each rest on a single signal-inducible reporter. Studies with a second arm: Torres-Farfan 2011 (Bmal1 antiphase to Per2), Houdek 2015 (Bmal1, Rev-erba), Dolatshad 2010 (Bmal1 and Per2 qPCR), Umemura 2017 (RNA-seq of all core genes plus CLOCK protein).
3. Maternal signals: melatonin (rat fetal adrenal and SCN; not primate adrenal, not rat liver), feeding (rat SCN, liver; Ohta 2008), glucocorticoid (mouse fetoplacental unit; Nikhil 2026), photoperiod (Salazar 2018).
4. Heart/kidney lacking rhythms: Dolatshad 2010 (E18-19 in vivo: no variation in kidney or liver; heart weak) and Stanton 2025 (later rise of clock gene expression in heart, kidney than liver, levels only) and Umemura 2017 (heart without TTFL at E10, oscillating by E18). No study shows heart/kidney arrhythmic where liver rhythmic in vivo; the mouse in vivo data show liver also arrhythmic at E18-19.

## Conclusions

- Adrenal: the best match. Rat and capuchin fetal adrenal show Per2/Bmal1 daily variation in vivo, and rat tissue keeps rhythms in culture for 48 h. But the work is on whole gland, so the cortex vs medulla split in the human data has no animal counterpart, and the capuchin adrenal was NOT melatonin-responsive, so the driver in primate is unresolved (fetal ACTH/glucocorticoid is plausible, untested).
- Liver: animal evidence is mixed. Rat fetal liver follows maternal feeding (Per1, Ohta 2008) but did not follow maternal melatonin (Houdek 2015); mouse E18-19 liver shows no in vivo 24 h variation (Dolatshad 2010) yet oscillates ex vivo (Dolatshad 2010; Landgraf 2015). This fits "present, but driven by maternal/systemic signals".
- Lung: no animal prenatal clock-gene study found, so the human lung positive has no animal support or contradiction. Sheep fetal tissue clock data were also not found.
- Heart and kidney: the animal data do not contradict human negatives (late maturation of heart and kidney expression, Stanton 2025; heart TTFL absent at E10, Umemura 2017). But they do not specifically support a heart/kidney vs liver/adrenal split either: in mouse, E18 explants of heart, kidney and liver all oscillate, and in vivo liver and kidney are both flat at E18-19. The human negative for heart and kidney is therefore partly a stage/species question, not a settled animal result.
- Signal-induced genes carrying the covariation: consistent with the animal literature in spirit. Fetal oscillations are phase-set by glucocorticoid (Nikhil 2026), melatonin, feeding, and the fetal heart cells' PER2 readout responds to dexamethasone (Semenovykh 2026), and the fetal clock appears to be maternally driven before it is cell-autonomous (Sladek 2025 in rat SCN; Nikhil 2026). No animal study reported here explicitly separated "driven reporter" from "running loop" in a peripheral fetal organ by lesioning the maternal SCN or adrenalectomy plus reading a BMAL1/REV-ERB arm; Ohta 2008 (maternal SCN independent, via feeding) is the nearest test and is Per1-only.
- Weakest animal evidence: every mouse explant claim (Landgraf 2015, Dolatshad explants, Inada 2014, Nikhil 2026) uses PER2::LUC alone, and an explant culture handling or medium change can itself induce PER2 (cAMP/glucocorticoid/temperature/serum), so a damped bioluminescence rhythm is not proof of a self-sustained BMAL1/REV-ERB loop. Umemura 2017 (heart) is the strongest because it adds RNA-seq and CLOCK protein.
- Key negative for a "running clock" reading: in vivo mouse E18-19 liver and kidney show no 24 h variation of Bmal1, Per2 or PER2::LUC (Dolatshad 2010). This agrees with the human result that the shared covariation is induction-driven rather than a running core loop.
