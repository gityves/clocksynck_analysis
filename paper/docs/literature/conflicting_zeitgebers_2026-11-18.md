## A. Models
1. Woller, Duez, Staels, Lefranc 2016, Cell Reports 17(4):1087-1097, doi 10.1016/j.celrep.2016.09.060. ABSTRACT (HAL/Lille full text blocked).
   Model of mammalian clock + SIRT1/AMPK; feeding/fasting enters via AMPK activation patterns; reproduces damped clock/NAD+ rhythms under high-fat diet. Single metabolic input, not a two-zeitgeber conflict model, no amplitude-saturation argument seen. Does not make our point.
2. Woller & Gonze 2018, Sci Rep "Modeling clock-related metabolic syndrome due to conflicting light and food cues" (title from search snippet; volume/DOI UNVERIFIED). ABSTRACT only.
   Pancreatic clock driven by SCN (light) and glucose (feeding). Conflict gives "differential phase shift in the expression of core clock genes"; downstream insulin hypo, hyperglycaemia, loss of food anticipation.
   Correction: Woller & Gonze is NOT a Cell Reports liver paper. The liver one is Woller, Duez, Staels, Lefranc (above).
3. Woller & Gonze 2021, Biology (Basel) 10(3):207, doi 10.3390/biology10030207. OPENED (first 100k chars).
   Review of the above: with conflicting cues the clock is "twisted" (gene-specific phase shifts); insulin rhythm "is not simply shifted but also reduced in amplitude" (insulin output, not clock amplitude). Closest published statement of conflict -> amplitude loss, but for a metabolic output and not a vector-cancellation/saturation argument.
4. Grabe, Mahammadov, Del Olmo, Herzel 2022, Front Netw Physiol 1:803011 (published 18 Jan 2022), doi 10.3389/fnetp.2021.803011. OPENED (first 100k of 109k chars).
   Modified Korencic 15-ODE clock model with light on Per and Rev-Erb agonist as two zeitgebers. Entrainment range depends on the phase difference between zeitgebers; dPhi=12 h gives "zeitgeber synergy" and "1.5-fold higher amplitude" than dPhi=3 h (i.e. the opposite of cancellation, because zeitgebers act on different clock components). No discussion of amplitude cancellation or saturation. Cites amplitude-phase oscillator entrainment work (Schmal et al. 2015). Does not make our point; shows that cancellation is not generic when two drives hit different nodes of a limit-cycle model.
5. Schmal et al. 2015 and other Herzel/Myung/Bordyugov/Kim-Forger/Leise "competing zeitgeber" papers: UNVERIFIED (only mentioned in Grabe et al.; not opened). No amplitude-loss/saturation theory located for peripheral clocks.

## B. Experiments
- Damiola et al. 2000, Genes Dev 14(23):2950-2961 (PMC317100). ABSTRACT/snippet. Quote: restricted feeding "can change the phase of circadian gene expression in peripheral cell types by up to 12 h while leaving the phase of cyclic gene expression in the SCN unaffected." Tissues liver, kidney, heart, pancreas; resetting faster in liver, similar phases in all after 1 week. No oscillator/saturation interpretation seen. Lung in this paper UNVERIFIED (not found in snippet).
- Le Minh, Damiola, Tronche, Schutz, Schibler 2001, EMBO J 20(24):7128-7136, doi 10.1093/emboj/20.24.7128. ABSTRACT. Slow day-feeding resetting is due to glucocorticoid signalling (counteracting resetting); blocking it speeds resetting. Interpretation is a hormonal brake (SCN-derived counter-signal), not amplitude arithmetic.
- Stokkan et al. 2001 Science: UNVERIFIED (not searched).
- Saini, Liani, Schibler et al. 2013, Genes Dev 27(13):1526-1536, PMID 23824542. ABSTRACT. RT-Biolumicorder; hepatocyte clocks of SCN-lesioned mice synchronise to feeding faster than intact mice; "the SCN uses signaling pathways that counteract those of feeding rhythms when their phase is in conflict with its own phase." Closest experimental statement of a conflict between SCN and feeding drives, interpreted as active SCN counter-signal rather than additive cancellation. Gene-specific shift claim not verified.
- Greenwell, ... Menet 2019, Cell Reports 27(3), doi 10.1016/j.celrep.2019.03.064 (title: "Rhythmic Food Intake Drives Rhythmic Gene Expression More Potently than the Hepatic Circadian Clock in Mice"). ABSTRACT/press only (full text 403). >70% of cycling liver transcripts lose rhythmicity under arrhythmic feeding; core clock genes keep normal oscillations; authors: systemic feeding signals act independently of the hepatic clock. Supports our removal result (liver clock amplitude retained) but authors do not use a saturation/self-sustained-population argument (as far as snippets show).
- Manella, Sabath, ... Asher 2021, Nat Metab 3(6):829-842 (not Cell Rep/PNAS; Xin et al. 2021 not checked). OPENED PMC7611072 (first 100k of 136k chars). Daytime feeding: liver and WAT clocks shifted ~12 h; kidney and heart intermediate (~4-8 h); lung barely shifted; quadriceps "lost rhythmicity" for almost all clock genes; clock-gene amplitude reduced in all tissues under daytime feeding. Authors' interpretation: tissue-specific response, liver clock as homeostatic buffer via glucose. Strongest published support for our points (2) and (3): amplitude drops where drives conflict, muscle loses rhythm, tissues differ. They do not frame it as vector cancellation or b weights. (Their DF protocol is a restricted-feeding conflict; amplitude figure is a polar summary, no numbers read.)
- Heyde & Oster 2019, Sci Rep 9:20114, doi 10.1038/s41598-019-56323-z. OPENED. 28 h LD + 24 h feeding (zeitgeber desynchrony). Liver and eWAT correlate most with feeding, adrenal more with light, SCN with light. Liver phase deviation 28+-4 (Bmal1), 7+-3 (Per2), 25+-8 (Dbp) degrees between in-phase and anti-phase days (small; liver is not a pure feeding follower in this protocol); adrenal ~50 degrees. Differences not significant. No weights, no amplitude values, no oscillator interpretation. Closest to a measurement of relative weights, but qualitative.
- Mukherji, Kobiita, Damara, Misra, Meziane, Champy, Chambon 2015, PNAS 112(48):E6691-E6698, doi 10.1073/pnas.1519807112 ("Shifting eating to the circadian rest phase misaligns the peripheral clocks with the master SCN clock and leads to a metabolic syndrome"). ABSTRACT/snippet. (Correction: authors are not Schibler/Ripperger.) Rest-phase feeding shifts peripheral clocks ~12 h; shift is gradual. Not read for amplitude.
- Wefers et al. 2018, PNAS 115(30):7789-7794 (PMC6065021). OPENED via Europe PMC XML (partial). 14 men, 12 h shift protocol, muscle biopsies at only 2 time points (7 AM, 7 PM). Clock genes (BMAL1, CRY1, PER2) "kept the same diurnal pattern as in the control condition, albeit blunted for PER and CRY"; "several clock genes did not reverse"; "partial rather than complete adaptation to the inverted behavioral cycle"; muscle clock "does not realign to the new behavior." No shift in hours and no amplitude values reported (two time points only). Interpretation is misalignment/slow adaptation, not cancellation. Caution: our "b near 1, amplitude mostly lost, partial shift" reading is not stated by the authors, and with two time points amplitude loss is only suggested ("blunted") not measured.

## C. Tissue weights
- Qualitative ranking, documented: liver ~ WAT (full ~12 h) > kidney, heart (4-8 h) > lung (little) with muscle losing rhythm (Manella 2021, OPENED); Damiola 2000 liver/kidney/heart/pancreas shifted by up to 12 h (ABSTRACT); Heyde & Oster 2019 liver/eWAT feeding-following, adrenal light-following (OPENED).
- Quantitative weights (a b parameter per organ): none found. Closest are phase-shift fractions (Manella, Heyde & Oster).

## D. Population/ensemble models
- Huang, Zhang, Braun 2022 (arXiv:2212.13579, nlin.AO, preprint; "A minimal model of peripheral clocks reveals differential circadian re-entrainment in aging"). OPENED abstract. Two-population coupled-oscillator model, mean-field equations; central clock driven by light, peripheral by sympathetic/feed-fast input. Studies re-entrainment speed in aging; does not (per abstract) treat peripheral amplitude under conflicting drives. Whether it uses Ott-Antonsen: the abstract does not say (search snippet suggested it; UNVERIFIED).
- A 2023 J Comp Physiol A Kuramoto/Ott-Antonsen bimodal paper (Arnold tongue) seen in search only: UNVERIFIED, not relevant to two drives on a tissue.
- Liao & Bose 2022 Math Biosci (central driving peripheral oscillators after phase shift): snippet only, UNVERIFIED.
- No model found of a peripheral tissue as a self-sustained population with two drives of weight b, giving saturation (removal ~ no change, conflict -> cancellation).

## Verdict: what is already published vs what would be new
- Published (OPENED): feeding inversion shifts liver ~12 h while kidney/heart partially and lung little; muscle loses rhythmicity and all tissue clock amplitudes fall under daytime feeding (Manella 2021); Damiola 2000 (ABSTRACT).
- Published: arrhythmic feeding leaves liver core-clock oscillation (Greenwell 2019, ABSTRACT/press), interpreted as systemic feeding signals vs cell-autonomous clock, not saturation.
- Published: SCN counteracts feeding when phases conflict (Saini 2013, Le Minh 2001) as an active hormonal/neuronal brake; Woller & Gonze 2018/2021 model conflict as twisted clock with reduced output amplitude (insulin).
- Published: two-zeitgeber entrainment depends on phase difference (Grabe/Herzel 2022); there 12 h can even increase amplitude, so amplitude loss by cancellation is not a generic model result.
- Not found: a unified two-drive/weight-b account explaining removal vs inversion vs partial conflict by saturating self-sustained oscillator populations; a quantitative b per tissue; the mapping of human muscle to b~1 (Wefers measures only two time points).
- Possibly new: the removal-vs-inversion asymmetry as a test of saturation vs linear response, with b as an estimable tissue parameter. Caveat: only partial literature search (Stokkan, Schmal, Kim-Forger, Leise, Xin, Kornmann not checked), so "not found" is not "does not exist".
