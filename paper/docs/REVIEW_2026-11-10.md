**Bottom line.**
1. The code is sound in its algebra (EM, projection, permutation p). One estimator property matters for the paper: the
   contrast "at a common host spread" (d*) is biased when the two arms differ in host spread and the host is measured
   with noise (M1). It does not threaten the equivalence claims (their arms have similar spread) and runs against the
   one remaining disease positive (fibrotic-lung T cells), but it invalidates "d* removes the spread" as a general
   statement and must be characterised before submission.
2. The solid results are the hospital-hours organ map, the shared clock-shaped state across organs, the mouse controls,
   the GTEx equivalences (sex, age, metabolic and chronic disease) and tumour-normal pairing with its stromal and
   immune extensions. The most provocative result, loss of inter-organ order after brain death, rests on one post hoc
   contrast and one resource. The ageing advance is not yet interpretable (Q26). Several cancer verdict labels are
   stronger than their tables.
3. For Cell the story "the body keeps one internal time; it moves as a whole and breaks only when the central drive is
   lost" is important and provocative, but it currently has no independent confirmation of either its robustness arm
   outside GTEx or its break arm. Two public timed brain cohorts and one living skin cohort could supply that at low
   cost (§4). For Nature Methods the material is close to sufficient once M1, interval reporting and the API are settled.

### M1. Contrasts at a common spread are not spread-free (could change published numbers: reported, not changed)

**Mechanism.** `Dataset.contrast` fits one host on the pooled donors and regresses each arm's target on the host
posterior means (`on_host`, `_core/host.py:216`). A posterior mean is the noisy anchor estimate shrunk by the pooled
prior, λ_pooled = V / (V + σ²). The regression slope on a shrunken regressor is unbiased only if the prior matches the
group. In an arm whose own host spread is V_g, the slope is multiplied by λ_g / λ_pooled with λ_g = V_g / (V_g + σ²):
the arm with the narrower spread gets a smaller |z|, hence a smaller z² and a lower coupling even after V is replaced by
the common V0 (`dataset.py:481, 488`). The arm's own V (`_core/host.py:226`) mixes the variance of its posterior means
with the pooled posterior covariance, so the reported log V ratio is itself shrunk toward 0. Label permutation stays
valid for "the arms are exchangeable", but not for the null that d* is meant to test ("same coupling, different
sampling spread"), which is the common situation in case-control cohorts collected in different windows.

**Post hoc feasibility simulation** (not a result; script and table in
`paper/exploratory/scouts_2026-11-10/`). Two arms of 80 donors with identical loadings and noise, reference window 6-18 h,
case window narrowed; 12 seeds; truth d* = 0 and log z² ratio = 0. Mean d* (SEM):

| anchor noise ψ | case window | observed log V ratio | d* T cell | d* fibroblast (anchor) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.002 (as in the tests) | 10-14 h | -1.21 | -0.081 (0.029) | -0.008 (0.007) |
| 0.05 | 8-16 h | -0.43 | -0.013 (0.027) | +0.007 (0.014) |
| 0.05 | 10-14 h | -1.04 | -0.129 (0.038) | -0.110 (0.016) |
| 0.15 | 8-16 h | -0.33 | -0.042 (0.026) | -0.073 (0.021) |
| 0.15 | 10-14 h | -0.78 | -0.159 (0.037) | -0.189 (0.029) |
| 0.05, 0.15 | 6-18 h (equal) | 0.00 | +0.014, +0.007 | -0.007, -0.018 |

The bias is absent at equal spread, small (≤ 0.07) at |log V ratio| ≈ 0.3-0.45, and of the size of the paper's

narrowest design is about -1.43; the estimate reads -1.21 to -0.78. The unit tests never see this because their anchors
are nearly noiseless (ψ 0.002, `tests/test_api.py:23`).

### M2-M7, briefly

- **M2.** c is attenuated by the factor A162 measured (median estimate / truth 0.87-0.92 at a true c of 0.9); the A163
  correction brings the ratio to 0.96-0.97 but fails its coverage rule and was not adopted. This is known; what is missing is one sentence in every figure legend
  that shows c, and a statement that the ±0.15 bound is on attenuated d*.
- **M3.** `clock_cov` sums ‖P C_kl P‖² over compartment pairs without weighting by shared donors, and the acrophase
  null keeps the gene amplitudes. Any donor-level axis that loads on high-amplitude clock genes (PER1, NR1D1, DBP under
  stress or ischaemia) passes both tests: gate alone gave 26 / 150 and 13 / 150 false passes (A209, A209b), 0 with the
  companion. `Dataset.fit(gate=True)` therefore gives a future user the weaker rule than the one the paper uses.
- **M4.** `log(max(n, 0.5) / depth)` with variance `1 / max(n, 0.5)` is biased upward at counts near 0, and the bias is
  common to all shallow compartments of a donor: it can pass the pairing test as a shared state (the A190 depth
  confound). `decompose.logfrac` uses `log((n + 0.5) / depth)` instead; the two readouts of one paper should agree.
- **M5.** Host states are centred within strata, so their angle is a clock phase only when sampling times cover the
  cycle. GTEx fast deaths are concentrated (resultant length of time of death 0.62,
  `A234_internal_time_age/output/direction.csv`), and strata there are Hardy x sex, not age: a fixed age-related
  displacement of the clock-plane mean would read as a phase drift (§2.2, ageing). The hospital-hours "internal time"
  of A200 / A130 is a tangent projection implemented only in `paper/` scripts.
- **M6.** `factor.bootstrap` skips failed refits (`continue`) and `pd.concat` raises on an empty list; `Fit` reports
  `n_boot_failed`, the low-level function does not.
- **M7.** |complex correlation| is positively biased at small n, split halves halve n, and Spearman-Brown then inflates
  the reliability and deflates r*. A142 H142.1 holds on the uncorrected r as well; H142.2 does not (erratum of 2026-11-01).

### 2.2 What is weaker than the documents say

- **Brain death (A132b).** The registered tests failed (H132.1, H132.2). The reported contrast (Fisher-Lee ρ 0.678 vs
  0.161, difference 0.517, p 0.008, 79 donors per arm, 65 pairs) was chosen after the per-group readouts and uses one
  equal-n subsample of the 298 organ donors; A136 H136.3 is a sensitivity on the same donors, not a replication. The
  full-n readout (ρ 0.093; shrinkage Spearman 0.095) supports it more than the subsample does and should be the number
  shown. Two things are missing for a reader: whether single-organ clocks are intact in these donors (order lost with
  rhythms kept is the SCN-lesion picture; damped clocks would be a different and duller result), and donor management
  (steroid and hormone protocols, time from declaration to procurement) as an alternative cause; A136 could not settle
  the steroid arm (gate p 0.098). The decision "descriptive, cause open" is right; "first human observation consistent
  with SCN-lesion work" should carry both caveats. See Q28.
- **Ageing (A234 vs A130, Q26).** A234's advance (+0.64 h per decade body, +0.99 brain) agrees in sign with the
  chronotype literature and with PER1 / PER2 advances in older cortex (Chen 2016), but: power was 0.29 at the prior, 19
  of 233 donors are under 40, the pseudo-clock companion is uninformative by construction, and with concentrated times
  of death a fixed age-related displacement in the clock plane is not separable from a rotation (M5). The post hoc check
  in RP (adding cos / sin of time of death) controls an age-time association, not this. A130 (-0.339 h per decade,
  one-sided p 0.054, 54 subjects in hospital hours) has the same weakness more acutely and is not evidence against
  A234. Neither supports a sentence in the abstract yet; §4 (D2, D3) gives two tests.
- **GTEx robustness is not method-exclusive.** Coupling and offsets in GTEx can be estimated by CHIRAL-type methods. What
  only clocksync can do is the living, hospital-hours part: A123-type maps, tumour-normal pairing, compartments within a
  resection. The robustness claims would carry more weight with one living cohort (§4, D4).
- **Clock specificity at the 5 % pseudo line.** With 200 pseudo sets the percentile has a Monte Carlo SD of about 1.5
  points at the line. A178 showed both directions: H171.1 moved from 5.5th ("not clock-specific") to 2.8th with 1,000

  A229 H229.5 (0.378 vs q95 0.371), H127.1 (2.5th, not leave-one-gene-out robust). Any claim between the 2nd
  and 9th percentile should be re-run with 1,000 sets under one registered rule before it is worded.
- **Cancer mechanism layer.** Proliferation-linked pairing loss does not survive spread matching (A185: 93.5th
  percentile) or CPTAC (A186: 90th). The TCGA lesion passes (A210 H210.2, A213 H213.2, A215 H215.1) vanish at matched
  subtype (A217), but A217's MDEs (0.08-0.17) exceed the original effects (0.04-0.07): "explained by subtype" is not
  shown either; the honest reading is "not separable from subtype". The gastric replication rests on GSE15459 alone.
  A229's "partial human correlate of the endothelial gate" rests on two secondaries after both primaries failed.
- **Development: one organ-level positive, no between-organ statement** (corrected 2026-11-10: the first version of
  this review omitted A189). The human fetal adrenal keeps the adult phase relations of the clock genes at 13-17 weeks
  (A189 H189.1: R 0.734, Holm 0.0495, matched sets 98.5th percentile, responsive 99.5th, leave-one-gene-out 0.69-0.82;

## 3. Paper plan

### 3.1 Cell

**Title idea.** *The human body keeps one internal time: organs move together through age and chronic disease, and
come apart after brain death.*

**Relation to Q25.** This is the candidate headline of Q25 (two kinds of desynchrony). Since Q25 was written, age
(A231) and five chronic conditions (A233) moved from "not detected" to equivalence-backed, next to sex (A208) and
metabolic disease (A164 / A202). Two of its clauses remain unsupported as robustness statements: "loss of daily cues"
(H109.2 fails in ventilated donors, rho 0.261, p 0.165; order is kept in Hardy 4, a secondary) and "cancer" (pairing is
a local statement about the tumour, not a test of the patient's inter-organ coupling).

**Question.** "Internal desynchrony" between organ clocks is the standard explanation for the harm of shift work,
ageing and metabolic disease, and it comes almost entirely from rodents. It has not been measurable in people, because
tissue is taken once, in working hours, without a usable time. The paper makes it measurable and asks where it occurs.

**Abstract-level claims.**
1. A person's own compartments are a sufficient reference: relations between clocks (coupling, relative amplitude,
   phase offset) are estimable without sampling times and inside a narrow window (Fig. 1).
2. In health the organs share one clock state with fixed offsets, carried most strongly by stromal-vascular cells;
   the brain is a separate block and blood is an exception by composition (Fig. 2).
3. This organisation is unchanged by sex, age, obesity, type 2 diabetes and five chronic conditions, within stated
   bounds, for coupling and for offsets (Fig. 3). With age the body may shift as a whole (pending D2 / D3).
4. It is lost in one group: brain-dead donors, where organ clocks no longer keep their mutual order while ventilated
   donors who are not brain dead keep it (Fig. 4; descriptive).
5. Disease acts locally: a tumour's stroma and immune cells stay on the patient's time, the malignant compartment loses
   clock-gene coherence by subtype, and three published tumour-clock readings are corrected (Figs 5-6).

**Provocative question the paper leaves.** If organs do not drift apart in chronic disease, misalignment in humans is
between the whole body and the day, not between organs; the central drive, not peripheral cues, holds the organs
together.

### 3.2 Nature Methods fallback

**Title idea.** *clocksync: circadian relations between a person's tissues and cell types from single untimed samples.*

**Claims.** (1) Host-referenced inference needs neither sampling times nor 24 h coverage; (2) it is calibrated: a
two-part claim rule with no false claims in no-clock simulations, permutation-based tests, stated attenuation; (3) it
recovers known biology where population methods fail in working hours; (4) it opens three uses: organ maps from
hospital cohorts, within-resection compartments, paired tumour-normal designs.

**Figures.** 1 principle and model; 2 calibration (A209 / A209b, A154, A162 / A163, A202, power, A203); 3 benchmark
against CYCLOPS, CoFE, CHIRAL, TimeTeller (A123b, A145, A140); 4 biological controls (A111, A206, A201, A174, A200);
5 applications (A123 map; A138 / A151 / A227 pairing; one equivalence forest; brain death as an example of an
offset-order readout).

**Missing.** M1 fixed or bounded; one interval recipe (M6); the gate default (M3); a second timed human benchmark
besides A200 (D2 supplies one); waveform transfer (A139 failed: state the limits of the fixed 14-gene waveform);
API, tests and tutorials (Phase 8, PI).

## 4. Proposed hypotheses and directions, ranked

Nothing below has been run. Each needs its amendment before scoring.

**D1. Is d* free of the sampling spread at the paper's designs? (method; required for either journal)**
- *Why:* §1, M1. *Data:* none new (`cs.simulate.like` on the fitted designs).
- *Feasibility:* 200 replicates x about 10 designs, hours on 4 workers. Power is not the issue; the bias is systematic.
- *Outcomes:* bias < 0.02 at the equivalence designs → one sentence in Methods and a flag on unequal-spread rows.
  Larger → adopt the arm-specific correction, add a corrected column, keep the registered numbers.

**D2. Timed post-mortem brain: an independent age test and a human desynchrony test (public)**
- *Data:* GSE71620 (Chen 2016: 146 subjects, BA11 and BA47, ages 16-96, time of death; 31 under 40 y and 37 over 60 y);
  GSE45642 (Li 2013: six regions, 55 controls and 34 MDD, time of death). Arrays, small downloads; both are the planned
  but never registered A117.
- *Questions:* (a) does host phase minus time of death advance with age in a cohort with a full age range (replicates
  A234 H234.3 on other donors, with 31 young subjects against GTEx's 13)? (b) Are region-to-region clock relations kept
  in MDD? Li et al. could not tell a shifted patient from desynchronised regions because their reference was the time
  of death; the within-person readout does not need it.
- *Power:* (a) GTEx brain gave SE 0.30 h per decade with 196 donors and little age spread; a range of 16-96 y roughly
  doubles the SD of age, so an effect of 0.35 h per decade (the archive prior) is detectable if the two-area host reads
  time as well as GTEx's (to verify first: Fisher-Lee with time of death ≥ 0.37). (b) 34 vs 55 with six regions: run
  `power.mde` on controls first; the brain host is anchor-dependent (WP0 item 8), so register the all-region
  leave-one-out host and an L1 companion.
- *Outcomes:* (a) advance replicated → the ageing sentence stands on two resources; absent at adequate power → A234 is
  read as a GTEx-specific or displacement effect and leaves the abstract. (b) Region relations lost in MDD → first
  within-person evidence of internal desynchrony in a human disease, and the positive the Cell story lacks; kept → the
  weaker rhythms of Li 2013 are a body-versus-day shift, which fits the paper's thesis. Both outcomes are usable.

**D3. Rotation or displacement? The age effect in GTEx against time of death (no new data)**
- *Question:* a real advance shifts host phase by the same amount at every time of death; a fixed age-related
  displacement of the clock-plane state gives an apparent shift that changes sign across the cycle.
- *Design:* A234's model with an age x (cos, sin of time of death) term, or β in two opposite halves of the day;
  permutation of age within strata. Aggregates only.
- *Power:* low (233 donors, times concentrated at R 0.62, 19 under 40 y); a registered power note decides whether it is
  worth scoring. The same test applies to A130's cohort only in principle (hospital hours: no opposite half).
- *Outcomes:* rotation → supports "the body advances as a whole"; displacement → A234 becomes a state change of clock
  genes with age, not a timing change, and Q26 closes with "not interpretable".

**D4. A living cohort for the robustness arm: skin sites and layers (public)**
- *Data:* Wu 2020 (154 women aged 20-74, forearm, buttock and cheek, epidermis and dermis, untimed; arrays).
- *Questions:* coupling between layers and between body sites in living people; equivalence of coupling and of the
  epidermis-dermis offset in older vs younger women (the A231 design); the offset itself against the 0.4-0.6 h of the
  timed arm of the same paper.
- *Power:* up to six compartments per person and 154 persons is a better design than most GTEx tissue pairs; untimed
  collection in clinic hours is the method's home ground. Risk: array noise per compartment; gate first.
- *Outcomes:* equivalence → claim 3 holds in the living; a difference → the first living-cohort age effect on internal
  organisation, to follow up.

**D5. Are single-organ clocks intact in brain-dead donors? (GTEx, descriptive; needs the PI, Q28)**
- *Question:* within-tissue clock-gene coherence (L1) and per-tissue amplitude in organ donors vs ventilated
  post-mortem donors, same donors as A132b.
- *Why:* separates "organs out of step" from "clocks damped", which decides whether the SCN-lesion comparison is fair.
- *Power:* 298 vs 79 donors; L1 coherence MDEs in GTEx tissues are small. One registered descriptive table, no mechanism.
- *Outcomes:* coherence kept → the comparison stands; coherence lost → report as loss of rhythmic organisation, not of
  inter-organ order.

**D6. A deliberate desynchrony positive in mouse (public; lower priority)**
- Chronic jet lag (Kettner 2016, GSE75475) and tumour-bearing liver (Masri 2016, GSE73222: core clock offset expected
  near 0 while outputs shift, a specificity control). Useful for Fig. 1 only if D2(b) is negative and the in-house
  jet-lag data are not used; both are bulk designs and need at least two compartments per animal to be estimable.

**D7. One rule for calls at the pseudo-clock line (housekeeping)**
- Register: any primary between the 2nd and 9th percentile is rescored with 1,000 sets and reported with its binomial
  interval. Cost: minutes to hours per test. Fixes the H171.1 / H173.1 inconsistency for all such rows at once.

**Nothing more is needed** for the cancer chapter (pairing and subtype coherence are replicated; further TCGA lesion
tests cannot beat subtype confounding at these MDEs), for development (keep the adrenal and embryo results as organ-level statements, drop "shared across organs"), for psoriasis (two cohorts,
not clock-specific: supplementary), and for further GTEx phenotype contrasts (A233 already bounds five conditions).
