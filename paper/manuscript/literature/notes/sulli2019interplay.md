# sulli2019interplay
**Ref:** Sulli G, Lam MTY, Panda S, Trends Cancer 5:475-494 (2019). DOI 10.1016/j.trecan.2019.07.002. Type: review. Lab: Panda (Salk).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Feature review of the clock-cancer connection: disruption (shift work, jet lag, feeding) as risk, clock genes in tumours (TCGA/GEO), clock control of cancer hallmarks including metabolism, redox, immunity, and pharmacological clock modulators (REV-ERB, CRY, CK1 ligands). Box 2 summarises chronotherapy. No new data.
**Sampling:** not applicable. Notes explicitly that TCGA/GEO expression is single time point.

## Key results
- Shift work: long-term (more than 20 years) shift work associated with higher breast and prostate cancer risk; IARC classed circadian disruption as probable carcinogen (group 2A, 2007); the authors caution that shift work is loosely defined and confounded (alcohol, smoking, diet) and some studies show no link.
- Clock genes in tumours: "The changes in gene expression in the TCGA or GEO datasets are based on a single timepoint." They therefore do not reflect diurnal rhythm; CYCLOPS-based ordering in hepatocellular carcinoma found nearly 15% of rhythmic transcripts (including PER1, CRY1) not oscillating in cancer; metastatic melanoma has impaired oscillations versus skin.
- Mouse genetics: Per2, Bmal1, Cry mutants increase tumours in combination with p53 or Kras mutations (e.g. Per2 and Kras G12D lung tumours); effects are gene- and tissue-specific.
- Mechanism lists: clock regulates nutrient metabolism, redox, autophagy, DNA repair (hallmark framework); tumours can secrete cytokines that disrupt clocks in distant organs.
- Chronotherapy (Box 2): two randomised colorectal trials with better response and less toxicity; "larger studies have led to mixed results with small benefits in survival being observed only in men"; only 0.16% of clinical trials in 2016 took administration timing into account.

## Not addressed / limits
- Largely qualitative; no sample sizes or effect sizes for human clock-disruption claims beyond epidemiology. Drug-target part (SR9009 and similar) is preclinical.
- Does not separate tumour-intrinsic clock loss from loss of proliferating-cell signal; human tumour rhythms remain inferred.
- Clock-metabolism coupling is stated as a mechanism, not shown in humans with tissue pairs.

## Link to us
