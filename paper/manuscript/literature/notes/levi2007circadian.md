# levi2007circadian
**Ref:** Levi F, Schibler U, Annu Rev Pharmacol Toxicol 47:593-628, 2007. DOI 10.1146/annurev.pharmtox.47.120505.105208. Type: review. Lab: Levi (Villejuif) and Schibler (Geneva).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Review of the mammalian circadian timing system (SCN hierarchy, peripheral oscillators, entrainment by feeding), clock control of xenobiotic detoxification, and chronotherapeutics, with cancer chronotherapy as the main clinical example. No new data. **Sampling:** not applicable; the clinical trials cited are timed dosing in colorectal cancer, delivered by ambulatory pumps at home.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Hierarchy and feeding: "Fasting-feeding cycles accompanying rest-activity rhythms are the major timing cues" for peripheral clocks (Abstract).
- Peripheral clocks can be uncoupled from the SCN and re-synchronised within days: "become uncoupled from the master clock upon daytime feeding" (nocturnal rodents fed in the day invert peripheral phase; return within two days after ad libitum food).
- Tumour clocks are heterogeneous: "tumor rhythms appear heterogeneous with regard to clock" gene expression and pharmacology determinants by tumour type and stage; an osteosarcoma with arrhythmic clock genes had near-normal clock induced by seliciclib at the best dosing time.
- Chronotherapy of colorectal cancer: schedule delivered "from 2200 to 1000 with peak infusion rate at 0400 and oxaliplatin" 10:00-22:00 with peak 16:00 (5 days every 3 weeks), "administered without hospitalization, at home or during the usual activities of the patient".
- Two randomised multicentre trials, 278 patients: chronomodulated "achieved 51% objective tumor responses, as compared with 30% in patients receiving constant rate"; severe mucositis reduced fivefold (p < 0.001 for response).
- Phase III (564 patients, 10 countries, EORTC): similar median survival overall; "with median survival being 16.3 months in women" vs 21.4 in men; men benefited from chronomodulation, women did not.
- Toxicity can impair the clock itself; high TGFa and IL-6 associated with ablated rest-activity rhythm in colorectal cancer patients.

## Not addressed / limits
- 2007 review: pre-dates human multi-organ atlases and any single-cell data; no measure of internal organ synchrony in patients (only rest-activity and a few circulating biomarkers).
- Tumour clock statements come from mouse models and a few human series, not systematic.
- Sex difference in chronotherapy survival suggests confounding or biology not resolved.
