# fishbein2021circadian
**Ref:** Fishbein AB, Knutson KL, Zee PC, J Clin Invest 131(19):e148286, 2021. DOI 10.1172/JCI148286. Type: review. Lab: Zee (Northwestern).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Bench-to-bedside review of circadian disruption and human disease: neurologic, psychiatric, metabolic, cardiovascular, allergic and immunologic disorders; interventions (bright light, melatonin, chronotherapy). No new data. **Sampling:** not applicable; human evidence cited comes from melatonin onset (plasma / saliva / urine), actigraphy, forced-desynchrony and shift-work studies.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Definition and levels of disruption: "Disturbance of circadian phase alignment and amplitude are the most common measures" associated with adverse health consequences; disruption spans molecular, cellular, tissue and system levels, plus misalignment with behaviour and environment (Fig 1: "Common examples of chronic external-internal misalignment" are social jet lag, shift work, mistimed light).
- Human biomarkers: "Although the SCN rhythm cannot be directly measured in humans," melatonin onset (DLMO) and amplitude and actigraphy are used; no scalable tissue-level markers: authors note "limited number of" patient-reported outcomes and "scalable human biomarkers of circadian disruption".
- Bidirectional disease link: disease disrupts rhythms and disruption worsens disease (neurodegeneration, mood disorders, metabolic, CV, immune).
- Shift work (16 % of US adults work non-daytime schedules): "Shift work increases risk for poor mental and physical health and is considered" probably carcinogenic (IARC) (Public health section).
- Metabolic: "between internal timing and social timing can lead to metabolic" impairment (social jet lag, chronotype, T2D); experimental misalignment raises glucose and blood pressure.
- Cardiac: "44% of the sinus node transcriptome has diurnal variation" (cited); in mice non-24 h light cycles "induced phase misalignment between the" environment and internal timing and drove QT prolongation.
- Evidence gap: "clinical trials have been conducted with only weak treatment" recommendations (Conclusion).

## Not addressed / limits
- Cancer is not discussed in the text (only the IARC shift-work classification); no tumour clock content.
- Human internal (organ-organ) desynchrony is mostly inferred, not measured; most mechanistic support is from mice.
- Narrative review, selected examples; "circadian disruption" used as an umbrella term (the authors themselves cite the need for "clearer terminology and quantification of circadian disruption").
