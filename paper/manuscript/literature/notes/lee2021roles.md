# lee2021roles
**Ref:** Lee, Exp Mol Med 53:1529-1538 (2021). DOI 10.1038/s12276-021-00681-0. Type: review. Lab: Yool Lee (Washington State University).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Review of clock disruption and cancer, clock components in tumours, and chronotherapy (training the clock, drugging the clock, clocking the drugs). No data, no sampling design.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Hierarchy and coupling: "This molecular clockwork is shared across the brain and peripheral organ systems, constituting a body-wide circadian network"; "real-world circadian rhythms are achieved through multimode regulation of tightly coupled body clocks under daily changes in internal and external processes".
- Disruption and cancer: night shift and chronic jet lag raise the risk of breast, lung, prostate, colorectal and skin cancers; IARC classifies shift work with circadian disruption as probably carcinogenic (Group 2A, 2007 and 2019). Mouse: SCN ablation or chronic jet lag ("consisting of an 8-h advance of the light-dark cycle every 2 days") accelerates transplanted tumours.
- Clock genes in tumours: "a recent large-scale systems analysis of 32 human cancer types revealed that PERs and CRYs, among several other clock genes, are downregulated in multiple cancers"; BMAL1 and CLOCK can be tumour-suppressive or tumour-promoting depending on cell type.
- Chronotherapy: doxorubicin in the morning and cisplatin in the evening in advanced ovarian cancer gave fewer complications. Tumour proliferation can be out of phase with normal tissue: ovarian cancer S-phase peak "with a peak in the mid-to-late morning that is nearly 12 h out of phase with nontumor cell proliferation in normal tissues".

## Not addressed / limits
- No measurement of phase relations between organs or between tumour and host in humans; coupling is stated as a concept, with the SCN as the master clock.
- The cancer-clock human evidence is the pan-cancer expression downregulation (cited), not oscillation.
- Review: effects are narrated, no effect sizes of its own; time-of-day clinical results are cited, not re-analysed.
