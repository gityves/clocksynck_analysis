# filipski2002host
**Ref:** Filipski E, King VM, Li X, Granda TG, Mormont MC, Liu X, Claustrat B, Hastings MH, Levi F, J Natl Cancer Inst 2002;94(9):690-697. DOI 10.1093/jnci/94.9.690. Type: biology (mouse tumour model). Lab: Levi (INSERM) with Hastings.
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Male B6D2F1 mice, LD12:12. Host clock perturbed: bilateral electrolytic SCN lesion versus sham, before transplantable tumours (subcutaneous Glasgow osteosarcoma GOS or slower pancreatic adenocarcinoma P03). Tumour clock not measured; tumour and host rhythms not compared. Rhythm study: 75 histologically verified SCN-lesion mice vs 64 sham; tumour study: GOS 16 lesion / 12 sham, P03 13 / 13 (2 x 2 design). **Sampling:** timed; activity and temperature by implanted radiotransmitter every 10 min for days; blood from separate groups at six times 4 h apart over 24 h (3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23 h after light onset), 3-7 weeks after lesion.

## Method (if any)
Fourier spectral analysis (harmonics of the record) and cosinor analysis with fixed 24 h period (mesor, double amplitude, acrophase); two-way ANOVA, log-rank. No expression-based clock inference.

## Key results
- Behaviour: SCN lesion ablates rest-activity rhythm; "Body temperature rhythm was suppressed in 60 of the 75 mice with histologically verified SCN lesions" (Results); the other 15 kept a damped, atypically phased 24 h rhythm.
- Serum corticosterone: sham mean 28.5 ng/mL, amplitude 23.4; lesion 20.8 and 6.3 ng/mL (Table 1); "the peak was advanced to mid-light"; peak/trough about 7-fold in sham vs about 2-fold in lesion.
- Lymphocytes: amplitude 1245 vs 562 cells/mm3, acrophase 2:48 sham vs 19:42 lesion (about 420 min earlier); rhythm damped (lesion P = .08).
- Tumour growth: "Both types of tumors grew two to three times faster in mice with SCN lesions than in sham-operated mice" (abstract); day 12 GOS 1443 mg (CI 1009-1844) vs 490 mg (273-707), P = .002; P03 day 22 1447 vs 749 mg, P = .05.
- Survival: "Survival of mice with SCN lesions was statistically significantly shorter compared with that of sham-operated mice"; median 22 vs 26 days, log-rank P = .0062.
- Dose-response: "tumor growth in the mice with partial SCN destruction was similar to that in controls" (n = 4).
- Authors' interpretation: "peripheral circadian oscillators may be able to function in the absence of the central clock control of the SCN, yet with poor synchrony", and "The loss of temporal organization across tissues arising from SCN lesion-induced corticosteroid dysrhythmia may contribute to enhanced tumor growth".

## Not addressed / limits
- No tissue clock-gene expression (no peripheral or tumour oscillator measured); "poor synchrony" among oscillators is hypothesised, not shown.
- SCN lesion also removes non-clock hypothalamic functions (14 mice lost to obesity or death; lymphopenia, low corticosterone); effect is not attributable to the clock alone.
- Transplanted tumours of two lines in a mixed-strain host; blood sampled at 4 h spacing with separate animals per time (pooled cosinor); partial-lesion control only n = 4.
- Mice were in LD, so lesion mice are still light-driven for some outputs.
