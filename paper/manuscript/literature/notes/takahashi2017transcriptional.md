# takahashi2017transcriptional
**Ref:** Takahashi, Nat Rev Genet 2017 (18:164-179). doi:10.1038/nrg.2016.150. Type: review. Lab: Takahashi (UT Southwestern).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Review of the mammalian clock transcriptional-translational loop, genome-wide cistromes, RNAPII and chromatin rhythms.
Mostly mouse liver and cell lines; human content is limited to Mendelian sleep disorders and GWAS. **Sampling:** not applicable
(synthesis of timed rodent studies, mainly constant-darkness, 4-h resolution).

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Hierarchical system: "a clock located in the hypothalamic suprachiasmatic nucleus acts as a master pacemaker to synchronize or entrain peripheral clocks distributed throughout the body" (Introduction).
- Clock genes are ubiquitous: "virtually all cells express these genes and have the capacity to generate circadian oscillations".
- Rhythmic transcripts: "A substantial fraction (~5-20%) of genes expressed in any particular cell or tissue have been found to undergo circadian oscillations at the mRNA level" (Introduction).
- Three interlocked loops (CLOCK:BMAL1-PER/CRY; REV-ERB/ROR on Bmal1; DBP/NFIL3 on D-boxes) yield different phases depending on cis-elements (Fig. 2).
- Liver: only 22% of cycling genes overlap between intron and exon (Fig. 3 legend): "Venn diagram of intron and exon cycling genes showing only 22% overlap".
- Tissue specificity: "In a comparison of the occupancy of BMAL1 between the liver and pancreas, ~40% of the target genes overlapped, with disparate binding sites clustering at distal regulatory regions of tissue-specific genes".
- Human genetics (Box 2): PER2 and CSNK1D mutations in FASPD: "have been shown to be linked to FASPD and to be causative in humans".

## Not addressed / limits
- Does not discuss synchrony between organs or donors as a measurable quantity, internal desynchrony, or inference from human samples; the question "how is this hierarchy coupled" is posed but not answered.
- Disease is treated in one sentence (links to cancer, immunity); no quantitative data on cancer clocks.
- Review: numbers come from the cited primary papers (mostly Koike 2012).
