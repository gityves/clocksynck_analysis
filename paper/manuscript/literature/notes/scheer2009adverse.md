# scheer2009adverse
**Ref:** Scheer, Hilton, Mantzoros & Shea, PNAS 2009 (106:4453-4458). DOI 10.1073/pnas.0808180106. Type: clinical (laboratory forced desynchrony). Lab: Shea (Brigham and Women's / Harvard).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Independent effects of the circadian cycle and of the behavioural (sleep/wake, fasting/feeding) cycle on metabolic, autonomic and endocrine variables, and effect of misalignment. Ten healthy adults (5 female), 10-day inpatient protocol: 2 baseline days then 7 recurring 28 h "days" in dim light (~1.8 lux), 4 isocaloric meals per day. **Sampling:** timed, fully scheduled; plasma leptin, insulin, glucose and cortisol hourly for 8 days, urinary catecholamines every 2 h, daily blood pressure, sleep (polysomnography), core body temperature continuous for 10 days. Blood plasma only; no tissue or gene expression.

## Method (if any)
Behavioural cycle and circadian cycle separated by uniform distribution of sleep/eat across all circadian phases; circadian phase from the fitted core body temperature minimum. Aligned vs misaligned compares the 28 h days with wake ~08:00 versus wake ~20:00.

## Key results
- Misalignment (eating and sleeping ~12 h out of phase from habitual times) lowers leptin 17% (P < 0.001), raises glucose 6% (P < 0.001) and insulin 22% (P = 0.006), raises mean arterial pressure 3% (P = 0.001), lowers sleep efficiency (67% vs 84%).
- "Notably, circadian misalignment caused 3 of 8 subjects (with sufficient available data) to exhibit postprandial glucose responses in the range typical of a prediabetic state." Breakfast 2-h glucose 99.9 to 132 mg/dL (P = 0.025).
- Endogenous circadian rhythms (behavioural effects removed): "Cortisol had a significant endogenous circadian rhythm" (113% peak-to-trough, peak at the end of biological night, ~habitual wake), epinephrine peaks in biological day (180 degrees, ~14:30-18:30), glucose peaks in biological night (4%, ~22:30-06:30); "There were no significant circadian rhythms in leptin, insulin, or norepinephrine." (Fig 2)
- Cortisol rhythm completely inverts across the sleep/wake cycle under misalignment, i.e. the SCN-driven hormone stays on circadian time while behaviour moves ~12 h.
- Leptin suppression holds beyond sleep loss: "These results suggest that circadian misalignment itself impacts leptin beyond any effect induced by changes in sleep."

## Not addressed / limits
- n = 10 (8 for glucose analyses); young healthy volunteers; no peripheral tissue clock readout, only plasma markers and core temperature as clock proxy.
- 7 cycles in dim light with imposed 28 h schedule: a laboratory model of shift work; chronic shift work not tested.
- No statement of what tissue clocks do under misalignment.
