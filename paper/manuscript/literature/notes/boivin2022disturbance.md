# boivin2022disturbance
**Ref:** Boivin, Boudreau, Kosmadopoulos, J Biol Rhythms 2022 (37:3-28). DOI 10.1177/07487304211064218. Type: review (narrative). Lab: Boivin (McGill / Douglas).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Narrative review of circadian misalignment and internal desynchrony in real and simulated shift work, plus sleep, alertness and health outcomes. Not systematic overall, but the peripheral-clock section used two PubMed searches. "A total of 236 references were obtained, and five original studies were kept after screening titles and abstracts." (clock genes; criterion >12 h sampling in a peripheral tissue); the transcriptome/metabolome search kept four of 22. **Sampling:** the primary studies are timed, >12 h, mostly lab simulations or small field samples.

## Method (if any)
None. Summary table of peripheral-clock studies (mesor, amplitude, phase).

## Key results
- Prevalence: "Between 10% and 30% of workers are working night shifts at least once a month".
- Clocks resist night schedules. "In general, peripheral clocks remain adjusted to a day-oriented schedule, with dampened rhythms at the group level."
- Internal desynchrony between central markers (melatonin, cortisol, temperature) and PBMC clock genes (Cuesta 2017: PER1 and BMAL1 delayed ~2.5-3 h, others stayed day-adjusted after 3 days). "state of internal desynchronization between several levels of the circadian system"
- Transcriptome: "about 11.8% of transcripts in the human genome were rhythmic, and the majority of these rhythms did not adjust to a night schedule" (Kervezee 2018, simulated 4 night shifts, PBMC).
- Archer 2014: "circadian disruption produced a 6-fold reduction in circadian transcripts compared with when sleeping in phase with the melatonin rhythm".
- Field: "Koshy et al. (2019) studied 11 police officers before and after 7 days of night shifts."; Akashi 2010: hair follicle phase delayed only about 2 h versus about 7 h behavioural delay.
- Metabolites shift: "most metabolites shift by several hours when working nights" (so transcript-metabolite desynchrony).
- Cancer: "concluded that night-shift work is probably carcinogenic to humans" (IARC 2020), but "A couple of recent meta-analyses found that shift work had little to no effect on breast cancer or other types of cancer risk"; dose effect: "11% increase in risk for every 5 years of exposure" (colorectal, Wang 2015).

## Not addressed / limits
- Narrative review: no pooled effect sizes; evidence on internal desynchrony is group-level, small n (11 police officers; few nurses), few tissues (blood cells, hair, oral mucosa); no solid organs.
- "Nonetheless, there is no consensus on the definition of exposure" for shift work.
- No individual-level desynchrony measure and no link between measured desynchrony and disease.
