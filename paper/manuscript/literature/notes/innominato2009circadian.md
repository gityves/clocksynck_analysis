# innominato2009circadian
**Ref:** Innominato PF, Focan C, Gorlia T, ... Lévi F, Bjarnason GA, Cancer Res 69:4700-4707 (2009). Type: clinical (prospective companion cohort). Lab: Lévi / Bjarnason (EORTC Chronotherapy Group).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Does the rest-activity rhythm (CircAct) at baseline relate to quality of life (HRQoL) and survival in chemotherapy-naive metastatic colorectal cancer?
Companion study of EORTC 05963 (n = 564; chronoFLO4 vs FOLFOX2). 9 institutions entered 191 patients; 130 had baseline actigraphy (68% of eligible, 23% of
the trial), 96 of them also completed the EORTC QLQ-C30. Wrist actigraph (non-dominant), 1-min counts, at least 72 h continuous before course 1 or 2
(103 before treatment, 24 after course 1, 3 after course 2).
**Sampling:** 72 h continuous, full 24 h cycles, free-living; measured quantity is behaviour, not molecular clock state. Parameters: dichotomy index I<O
(rest vs wake activity; 100% = perfect) and 24 h autocorrelation r24.

## Key results
- Wide between-patient variability: mean I<O 94.3 (median 97.0), r24 mean 0.38 (SD 0.17); I<O and r24 correlate r = 0.74.
- Robust rhythm goes with good WHO performance status: median I<O 98.2 (PS 0, n = 70), 95.7 (PS 1, n = 45), 95.3 (PS 2, n = 15); r24 0.44, 0.36, 0.24. Rhythm did not correlate with age, sex, BMI, liver involvement or tumour burden measures (P > 0.10).
- HRQoL: I<O correlated with global quality of life, role/physical/social functioning, fatigue, appetite loss (|r| 0.29-0.40 in Table 2 partial correlations, adjusted for age). "I<O was confirmed to correlate with global quality of life, physical functioning, social functioning, fatigue, and appetite loss (r > |0.25|; P < 0.01)" (Abstract).
- Survival: I<O univariate HR 0.95 (0.93-0.97) per unit, P < 0.0001; multivariate HR 0.94 (0.92-0.97) after adjusting age, sex, BMI, centre, tumour site, adjuvant chemo, arm. "I<O further independently predicted for overall survival with a hazard ratio of 0.94 (P < 0.0001)" (Abstract). Bootstrap inclusion 89%. r24 lost independence (P = 0.13).
- No relation of baseline CircAct to tumour response (P >= 0.14), severe toxicity (P >= 0.31) or progression-free survival (P > 0.10).

## Not addressed / limits
- Behavioural proxy of the central pacemaker; masking by pain, analgesics (n = 26, more perturbed rhythm), fatigue and general frailty. Causality unproven: a damped rhythm may mark advanced illness rather than drive it.
- 23% of trial population; no molecular, tissue or tumour clock measured; arm (chrono vs conventional) adjusted but interaction rhythm x arm not shown.
- Per-unit HR on a 0-100 scale (I<O median 97, IQR 6.8) is not a clinically scaled effect size; no cut-off validated.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** disrupted organism-level rhythmicity predicts worse outcome in cancer patients (refs_*.md: prospective cohort in a phase III trial). Supported (n = 130, HR 0.94, P < 0.0001), but as a behavioural, not tissue-level, measure.
- **Relation to clocksync:** background and a comparison target: it is an organism-level rest-activity "coupling" proxy with survival association. Our tumour-clock-signal prognosis tests were null after proliferation (A191-A194, A198).
