# reppert2002coordination
**Ref:** Reppert & Weaver, Nature 2002 (418:935-941). Type: review. Lab: Reppert (UMass).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Review of mammalian circadian timing: SCN master clock, transcriptional feedback loops, post-translational control, photic input,
output pathways, tissue-specific circadian transcription. Rodent data (mouse, rat, hamster). **Sampling:** not applicable;
synthesis of timed rodent studies (microarray surveys of tissues over the day).

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Hierarchy: "Circadian timing in mammals is organized in a hierarchy of multiple circadian oscillators" (Fig. 1a).
- Peripheral damping: "oscillators expressed in vivo can only sustain 24-h oscillations for a few days without input from the master clock" (Fig. 1b: SCN explant rhythm persists for weeks, liver explant damps).
- SCN versus periphery is probably quantitative: the distinction "may have more to do with global differences in clock protein levels and/or kinetics than the existence of a specific element".
- Loop architecture: CLOCK-BMAL1 activates mPer, mCry and Rev-Erb-alpha; "disruption of either the mPer1 and mPer2 genes together or both mCry genes causes immediate behavioural arrhythmicity when the double-knockout animals are placed in constant conditions" (Table 1).
- Entrainment of periphery: "Thus an artificially imposed feeding schedule in rodents can completely uncouple peripheral oscillators from SCN control" (SCN to slave oscillators).
- Output: "between 2 and 10% of the analysed genes exhibit circadian oscillations in steady-state RNA levels"; "Circadian gene expression is remarkably tissue-specific (less than 5% overlap between tissues)".
- Unmet question: "Understanding the mechanisms by which single oscillators interact to form a functional oscillator at the tissue level remains one of the important challenges".

## Not addressed / limits
- No humans, no cancer, no inference method; a 2002 view (e.g. Rev-Erb-alpha loop and PER2 role in Bmal1 are still "?" in Fig. 2).
- 'Internal desynchrony' is only the feeding-uncoupling example, qualitative, not quantified (no phase offsets in hours).
- The quoted overlap and cycling-gene fractions are early microarray estimates; later RNA-seq work gives larger rhythmic fractions (cf. Takahashi 2017: 5-20%).
