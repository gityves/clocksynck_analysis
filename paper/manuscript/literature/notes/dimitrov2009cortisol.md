# dimitrov2009cortisol
**Ref:** Dimitrov S, Benedict C, Heutling D, Westermann J, Born J, Lange T, Blood 113:5134-5143 (2009). DOI 10.1182/blood-2008-11-190769. Type: biology (human). Lab: Born / Lange (Lübeck).
**Source:** PMC full text (PMC2686184), read by web fetch on 2026-11-12: abstract to the infusion experiments, Table 1 and Table 2 verbatim; the end of Results and the Discussion only as a summary. Not in the Zotero export: quotes are not checked by `check_quotes.py`.

## Question and design
Which hormones drive the daily rhythm of human blood T-cell subsets? 14 healthy men (21-30 y), two 24 h sessions from 20:00 h (regular sleep 23:00-07:00 h, or continuous wakefulness), blood every 1.5 h at night and every 3 h by day; flow cytometry of CD4+ and CD8+ naive, central memory, effector memory and effector cells; cortisol and epinephrine; infusion experiments in 10 further men; CXCR4 in 6 subjects at 09:00 and 21:00 h.
**Sampling:** timed, full 24 h, living subjects, fixed sleep schedule.

## Key results
- Total counts: "maximum values (acrophase) at approximately 02:00 h at night and minimum values (nadir) at approximately 14:00 h" (Results; Table 1: CD3+ mesor 1902.08, amplitude 387.91, acrophase 02:10 h, SEM 20 min).
- Subsets (Table 1 acrophases): naive CD4+ 02:41 h, naive CD8+ 02:09 h, central memory 02:02 and 02:16 h, effector memory 01:31 and 01:56 h; effector CD8+ 15:34 h (SEM 116 min, p < .05). Night-to-day change -41 % (naive CD4+) and -46 % (naive CD8+) (Table 2).
- Cortisol: "a minimum approximately 00:30 h and a maximum around the time of morning awakening approximately 08:00 h"; cosinor acrophase 10:40 h (Table 1).
- Lag: "Correlations were maximal when CD4+ and CD8+ T cell counts were measured with a delay of 3 hours with reference to cortisol concentrations (r = −0.65 and −0.64, respectively, P < .001)."
- Mechanism: "Naive T cells were negatively correlated with cortisol rhythms, decreased after low-dose cortisol infusion, and showed highest expression of CXCR4, which was up-regulated by cortisol." "Daytime increases in cortisol via CXCR4 probably act to redistribute naive T cells to bone marrow" (Abstract).
