# skene2018separation
**Ref:** Skene et al., PNAS 2018 (115:7825). DOI 10.1073/pnas.1801183115. Type: clinical (metabolomics). Lab: Skene (Surrey) / Van Dongen, Gaddameedhi (Washington State).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Healthy volunteers aged 22-34 y, between-group: n = 7 on a 3-day simulated night-shift schedule (sleep 10:00-18:00 after a transition nap) vs n = 7 on a simulated day shift (sleep 22:00-06:00). Plasma metabolome (targeted LC/MS, 132 metabolites), plus melatonin, cortisol and blood PER3 expression as central-clock markers. **Sampling:** timed; 24 h constant routine after the shifts, blood every 1-3 h (melatonin hourly 18:30-01:30); each person is sampled once (between-subject contrast).

## Method (if any)
Cosinor per metabolite per condition; acrophases compared as clock time, circadian time (relative to DLMO) and zeitgeber time (relative to lights-on); PCA clustering of metabolites. Assumes full-cycle sampling under constant routine, so that rhythms are endogenous or carry-over of earlier behaviour.

## Key results
- "Nearly half of these metabolites showed a 24-h rhythmicity under constant routine following either or both simulated shift schedules" (65 of 132): 27 rhythmic after both, 19 only after day shift, 19 only after night shift.
- DLMO, cortisol and PER3 phase moved only about 2 h after the night shift, i.e. the SCN pacemaker did not follow the shifted behaviour.
- "Only three of these metabolites maintained approximately the same peak time following both the simulated day-shift and night-shift schedules" (serotonin, taurine, sarcosine); 24 shifted, mostly by about 12 h (reversal).
- Abstract: "95% of the metabolites with a 24-h rhythmicity showed rhythms that were driven by behavioral time cues". Plotted on zeitgeber time, SM C20:2 aligns between schedules; on circadian time (DLMO) sarcosine aligns.
- Metabolites with reversal are mostly glycerophospholipids, sphingolipids; losses affect lysoPCs and 7 of 20 amino acids; pathway analysis points to liver, pancreas, gut.

## Not addressed / limits
- Plasma metabolites, not tissue clocks: "peripheral oscillator" is inferred, not measured. The only gene-expression marker is PER3 (blood cells, one gene).
- n = 7 vs 7, between-subject; rhythms are only observed in one 24 h window after the shifts (no baseline rhythm per person).
- Light, sleep, activity and feeding were shifted together; they cannot be separated.
- Constant-routine carry-over for 24 h only; persistence over days is not tested.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** refs row: metabolite rhythms separated into circadian vs behaviour-driven; window on peripheral oscillators. Supported, but the grouped line read out two compartments ... in the same volunteers is overstated: there is one analyte class (plasma), different volunteers per condition. The relevant internal-desynchrony evidence is clock (SCN) markers vs metabolites, which differ by about 2 h vs about 12 h.
- **Relation to clocksync:** background (central vs behavioural clock decoupling; supports a cue vs core split). Not usable as data (metabolomics).
