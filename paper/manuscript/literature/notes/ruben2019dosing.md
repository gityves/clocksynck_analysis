# ruben2019dosing
**Ref:** Ruben MD, Smith DF, FitzGerald GA, Hogenesch JB, Science 365:547-549, 2019. DOI 10.1126/science.aax7621. Type: review (Perspective). Lab: Hogenesch / FitzGerald.
**Source:** full text (the text file starts with the tail of an unrelated astronomy article on fast radio bursts, an adjacent page; the Perspective itself is complete, supplementary table of the 106 trials not included)

## Question and design
Perspective: how circadian time can improve drug dosing ("circadian precision medicine"). Contains one small systematic tally: 106 clinical trials (published over 50 years) that compared at least two dosing times and measured efficacy or toxicity (70 drugs / procedures, 15 therapeutic areas; list in the supplement). **Sampling:** not applicable for tissues; trial design is dosing at clock time, ~90 % with only two time points (morning vs evening).

## Method (if any)
None. Describes CYCLOPS (Anafi 2017) as the tool to reconstruct sample order when collection times are missing.

## Key results
- Only a few drugs carry time-of-day labels: "Only four of the 50" most prescribed drugs have an FDA-labelled dosing-time recommendation (Starts, stops, and scope).
- In the trial tally: "Among these studies, 75% found that" efficacy or toxicity depended on dosing time.
- Half-life matters: 85 % of trials with half-life < 15 h vs 39 % for longer-acting drugs: "less than 15 hours showed dosing time dependence compared with just 39% for longeracting drugs."
- Trial design weakness: "90% compared only two time points, almost" always morning vs evening; median hypertension trial 62 subjects; individual phase ignored: "Eight a.m. on the wall clock is not".
- Human tissue rhythms: single samples from many donors can reveal population rhythms, "however, time of sample collection is often not recorded."; "called CYCLOPS was developed to reconstruct sample order in the absence of sample collection times"; the CYCLOPS atlas (Ruben 2018 Science) found about half of protein-coding genes cycle.
- Inpatient care: subjects "often detached from natural environmental" fluctuations are at risk of circadian disruption; hospital layouts are being rethought.

## Not addressed / limits
- Perspective; tally is not a formal meta-analysis (no effect sizes, publication bias not assessed; 75 % is a vote count).
- No discussion of how to obtain internal phase from hospital-collected tissue other than CYCLOPS; assumes coverage over the cycle is achievable.
- No cancer-specific or internal desynchrony content beyond the chemotherapy timing mention.
