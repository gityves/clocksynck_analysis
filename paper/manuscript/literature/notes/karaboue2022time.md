# karaboue2022time
**Ref:** Karaboué A, Collon T, Pavese I, ... Lévi F, Cancers 14:896 (2022). DOI 10.3390/cancers14040896. Type: clinical (single-centre retrospective cohort, pilot). Lab: Lévi (Paris-Saclay / Montfermeil).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Does clock time of nivolumab infusion modify efficacy in metastatic NSCLC? 95 consecutive patients (second line or later, 75.8% second line), one hospital, 2015-2020, nivolumab
3 mg/kg then 240 mg q2w; 1818 infusions, median 9 per patient (3-133). Infusion slot was assigned per course by day-hospital coordinators "on a logistical basis" (not randomised).
Patients split at the median clock time of all infusions (12:54): 'morning' n = 48 (median 11:55), 'afternoon' n = 47 (median 13:34).
**Sampling:** infusions between 09:27 and 17:14 only (hospital hours); intra-patient CV of infusion time 10% (2-21%). No chronotype, actigraphy, cortisol or clock-gene measure of any patient.

## Key results
- Median PFS 11.3 mo (5.5-17.1) morning vs 3.1 mo (1.5-4.6) afternoon, p < 0.001; median OS 34.2 mo (15.1-53.3) vs 9.6 mo (4.9-14.4), p < 0.001. "Median PFS (95% CL) was 11.3 months (5.5–17.1) for the ‘morning’ group and 3.1 months (1.5–4.6) for the ‘afternoon’ one (p < 0.001)." (Abstract)
- Multivariable Cox: morning timing HR 0.26 (0.11-0.58) for PFS, 0.17 (0.08-0.37) for OS. "Multivariable analyses identified ‘morning’ timing as a significant predictor of longer PFS and OS" (Abstract).
- Objective response 37.5% vs 14.4% (p = 0.029), disease control 77.1% vs 50.0% (p = 0.006); 2-month landmark median PFS 15.8 mo (n = 42) vs 3.5 mo (n = 31), p < 0.001; consistent at 4 and 6 months and across age, sex, PS, histology, PD-L1 subgroups.
- Toxicity similar except grade 3-4 fatigue 6.3% vs 14.9% (p = 0.024) and grade 2-3 skin toxicity 31.9% vs 12.7% (p = 0.049, more in morning).

## Not addressed / limits
- Retrospective, no randomisation: "The main limitation of our study is related to its retrospective nature and to the lack of statistical randomization" (Discussion). Imbalances: afternoon patients older (p = 0.012), more often female (25.5% vs 8.3%, p = 0.025), worse PS trend (p = 0.061).
- Number of courses differs hugely (median 19 vs 6, p < 0.001): patients who respond stay on drug and accumulate more infusions, and the grouping uses the majority of all infusions, so exposure and group are entangled (reverse causation / immortal-time risk). Landmark analyses mitigate but do not remove this.
- Effect size (nearly 4-fold in median OS) is implausibly large for a timing effect with an antibody of 20 day half-life; the authors themselves note pharmacokinetics are unlikely to explain it. Single centre, small n, wide CIs.
- Dosing time is a clock hour of the hospital, not of the patient; no individual internal phase, so an effect of chronotype x hour cannot be separated.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** efficacy differing by time of day of infusion in a small observational cohort (refs_*.md). Supported as written, but must stay worded as retrospective, non-randomised; not evidence of causal chronoefficacy.
- **Relation to clocksync:** motivation. If hour-of-day matters, the relevant variable is internal time, which is unmeasured in every ICI timing study; clocksync's untimed readout is for host-referenced coupling and offsets, not for assigning a time.
