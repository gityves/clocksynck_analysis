# hunt2007riding
**Ref:** Hunt T, Sassone-Corsi P, Cell 2007;129:461-464. DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2007.04.015. Type: review (minireview). Lab: Hunt (cell cycle) and Sassone-Corsi.
**Source:** full text (4-page minireview)

## Question and design
Minireview of whether the circadian clock and the cell cycle are connected. Cites mouse (Wee1 and Cry mutants, Per2 mutant, gamma radiation), human cells (PER1-ATM, PER1-Chk2) and Neurospora; no new data. **Sampling:** not applicable. No tumour clock is measured in the work discussed.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Peripheral clocks exist and are hierarchically coordinated: "the timetable of physiological events and cell division is not synchronous among various tissues" (Introduction).
- Clock genes control cell-cycle genes: "CLOCK:BMAL1 recognize the E box"; Wee1 has three E boxes and liver regeneration after hepatectomy is drastically impaired in Cry-mutant arrhythmic mice (Matsuo 2003, mouse).
- Tumour link: "per-1 expression is downregulated in human tumors" and PER1 overexpression sensitises human cancer cells to DNA-damage apoptosis (Gery 2006, cell lines and tumour samples; tumour expression, not phase).
- Per2: after gamma radiation clock genes are induced in mouse liver; "induction is absent in mPer2 mutant mice" and the mutants are cancer prone (Fu 2002, summarised; we read Fu 2002 separately).
- Epidemiology and therapy: "A simple but striking example of how profound circadian regulation influences human physiology is the increased susceptibility that shift workers have for some types of cancer."
- Chronotherapy: "descriptive studies indicate that the administration of specific chemotherapies at one time of the day rather than another" changes clinical outcome (no numbers given).
- Interaction judged context-dependent: "the use of circadian molecules in cell-cycle control would occur under occasional and contingent physiological circumstances".

## Not addressed / limits
- Opinion piece; no numbers, no tumour-versus-host clock comparison, no timed tumour sampling.
- Model is germline mutants and cell lines; nothing on tumour clock phase, amplitude or synchrony with the host.
- States "Nothing of the kind has been proposed for the circadian clock" (a checkpoint), i.e. no theory of how a tumour clock would be reset.
