# levi1997randomised
**Ref:** Lévi F, Zidani R, Misset JL (International Organization for Cancer Chronotherapy), Lancet 350:681-686 (1997). Type: clinical (randomised trial). Lab: Lévi (Villejuif).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Chronomodulated vs constant-rate 5-day infusion of oxaliplatin + 5-FU + folinic acid (16-day intervals) in previously untreated metastatic
colorectal cancer. Randomised, multicentre (9 institutions, 3 countries), n = 186 (93 vs 93), enrolled 1991-93, stopped early at the second
interim analysis (significant response difference after 123 patients; planned n = 210, 80% power for a 20% response difference).
Intention-to-treat. Patients and nurses masked to the schedule, not the local investigator.
**Sampling:** not a sampling study. Chrono arm: drug flow rate modulated over 24 h by programmable ambulatory pump (figure 1 axis 1000 h, 1600 h,
2200 h, 0400 h; the text gives no peak hours); the control arm is flat 24 h infusion. No patient circadian marker (no actigraphy, cortisol, clock gene) measured.

## Key results
- Objective response 47/93 (51%) chrono vs 27/93 (29%) constant, difference 21.5% (95% CI 13.7-31.2), p = 0.003. "An objective response was obtained in 47 (51%) of the chronotherapy group, and in 27 (29%) of the constantrate group" (Summary).
- Severe mucositis (grade 3-4) 14% vs 76% of patients, p < 0.0001; peripheral neuropathy with functional impairment 16% vs 31%, p < 0.01. "Chronotherapy reduced five-fold the rate of severe mucosal toxicity (14% vs 76%, p<0·0001)" (Summary).
- Withdrawal for toxicity 26/93 vs 47/93 (p = 0.002); median 5-FU dose per course 3500 vs 2500 mg/m2 (+40%), i.e. better tolerance allowed a higher dose.
- Median time to treatment failure 6.4 vs 4.9 months (p = 0.006); progression-free survival 9.8 vs 7.9 months (p = 0.20).
- No survival gain: "median survival times and 3-year survival were similar in both groups"; 3-year survival 22% vs 21%, p = 0.46 for median survival (Results). Multivariate relative risk of survival for the chrono arm 1.06 (0.76-1.48).

## Not addressed / limits
- Baseline imbalance against the chrono arm (previous surgery of metastases 24% vs 8%; bone/abdominal glands 10 vs 2 patients).
- 22 of 93 control patients (24%) were switched to chrono after failure; this dilutes any survival difference. Early stopping inflates the effect size.
- The contrast is chrono-modulated vs flat delivery, not a comparison of two clock hours; it cannot say which hour is best or whether timing per se (vs shape of delivery) matters.
- No timing biomarker, so neither inter-patient phase differences nor circadian disruption could be related to benefit. Sex effect not analysed here (it emerged later, see levi2010circadian).
