# balsalobre1998serum
**Ref:** Balsalobre, Damiola, Schibler, Cell 1998. DOI 10.1016/S0092-8674(00)81199-X. Type: biology. Lab: Schibler.
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Do cultured cells that are not part of the SCN carry a circadian clock? Rat-1 fibroblasts (immortalised, >25 years in
culture) and H35 rat hepatoma cells, confluent, given a 2 h shock with 50% horse serum then serum-free medium; mRNA by
RNase protection (bulk RNA of ~1.5 x 10^7 cells per point). Rat liver sampled around the clock as in vivo reference.
Replicate numbers are not stated (single time courses, gels shown). **Sampling:** timed; in vitro time course for 72 h
after the shock (several points per day); liver over one 24 h cycle in a 12:12 light regimen (lights on 7 a.m.).

## Method (if any)
None (experimental biology). Period estimated graphically from peak-to-peak intervals of 23 mRNA cycles.

## Key results
- Serum shock triggers circadian mRNA cycles of rper1, rper2, rev-erba, dbp, tef in rat-1 cells for up to three days.
  "In rat-1 fibroblasts, up to three consecutive daily oscillations with an average period length of 22.5 hr could be recorded." (Summary)
- Period of 23 estimated cycles: 20-27 h, mean 22.5 +/- 1.7 h (Results, Fig. 2).
- Specificity: genes not cycling in vivo (tbp, b-actin, rora) do not cycle in vitro. "Transcripts that do not show circadian expression in vivo also do not cycle in vitro." (Results)
- The phase order of clock transcripts is the same in culture and in liver: rev-erba precedes dbp by ~3 h and rper2 by
  ~6 h in cells; in liver rev-erba peaks ~2 p.m., rper1/dbp/tef ~5 p.m., rper2 9:30 p.m. (Figs 3, 4).
- Not a cell-cycle artefact: generation time 15 h versus period 22.5 h, oscillation continues in serum-free medium,
  and Ara-C does not change it. "Second, circadian gene expression proceeds for 3 days in serum-free medium, which prohibits cellular proliferation." (Discussion)
- H35 hepatoma cells show at most two cycles, with the same phase order (Fig. 5); c-fos is not induced there.
- Serum induces c-fos (peak 30 min), rper1 (60 min) and rper2 (75 min) as immediate-early genes, mimicking light
  induction in the SCN (Fig. 6, cycloheximide-insensitive).

## Not addressed / limits
- Population-level mRNA only: cannot tell whether the damping after 2-3 cycles is single-cell damping or loss of
  synchrony among cells (the paper raises the possibility; resolved by nagoshi2004circadian, welsh2004bioluminescence).
- Immortalised lines and a non-physiological shock; no statement about phase relations between organs in an animal.
- Gel-based, few points per cycle; no replication statistics, no amplitude quantification across cultures.
- Does not show that peripheral clocks run in tissue or are coupled to the SCN; it only shows that they exist.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** peripheral cells carry a clock (Circadian cycle). Our refs summary is supported.
- **Relation to clocksync:** background. Establishes that a clock-gene programme with a fixed phase order exists
  outside the SCN; that fixed order is what a shared-acrophase clock-gene model assumes.

## Ideas
- The in vitro phase order (rev-erba, then dbp/tef/rper1, then rper2) versus liver is a published reference for
  testing fixed relative acrophases of core clock genes across compartments in our data.
- Cite jointly with the 2000 dexamethasone paper (balsalobre2000resetting) for the resetting cue; follow-up
  literature missing from our list: Akashi and Nishida 2000 (forskolin/TPA shocks), Yagita and Okamura 2000 (FGF).
