# roenneberg2016circadian
**Ref:** Roenneberg T, Merrow M, Curr Biol 26:R432-R443, 2016. DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2016.04.011. Type: review. Lab: Roenneberg (Munich).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Review proposing a taxonomy of circadian (mis)alignments between the SCN, peripheral clocks and environmental zeitgebers, linked to chronotype, social jetlag, shift work and disease. No new data. **Sampling:** not applicable; epidemiological data come from the MCTQ database (sleep timing questionnaire) and the cited human and rodent protocols.

## Method (if any)
Conceptual: four states per oscillator (optimal, abnormal phase of entrainment, free-running, arrhythmic) combined into "phase maps" (Fig 3, Table S1).

## Key results
- Entrainment phase varies between individuals and conditions; phase of entrainment depends on zeitgeber strength and internal day length (Fig 1).
- Internal phase relationships in rodents: peripheral clocks lag the SCN by several hours, and these can be reorganised: "the clock genes in the SCN and the liver can oscillate up to 180" degrees out of phase depending on protocol.
- Optimal entrainment is not unique: "many phase constellations may represent optimal entrainment" per chronotype and conditions; deleterious constellations (social jetlag, circadian disruption, misalignment) arise "under weak, conflicting or disrupted" zeitgebers.
- Human organ-specific phase maps are undefined: "Neither the phase map for optimal nor that for sub-optimal" entrainment has been formally defined in humans.
- Social jetlag: "only 13% of the day-working population" (MCTQ) are free of it; odds of being overweight rise 33 % per hour of social jetlag; shift work affects about 20 % of workers in the western world.
- Cancer: shift-work (breast, colorectal, prostate) epidemiology and the light-at-night hypothesis; "cancer tissues may still be arrhythmic" (observations in constant conditions; some colorectal cancer cells show rapidly damping rhythms); tumours proposed as phase-map states with certain tissues arrhythmic (1-1/4 and 2-2/4, "likely although they have not been formally demonstrated").
- Call to action: "We would profit enormously in our understanding of 'clock and health' by establishing the phase maps in healthy controls" and patients.

## Not addressed / limits
- Conceptual taxonomy without tests; no method to measure tissue-level phase maps in people; no quantitative data on organ phase offsets in humans.
- Epidemiology (shift work, social jetlag) is behavioural: sleep midpoints, not tissue clocks.
- Cancer discussed briefly, mostly through shift-work epidemiology and a hypothesis for tumour arrhythmia.
