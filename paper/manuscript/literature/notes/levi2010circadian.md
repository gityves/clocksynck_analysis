# levi2010circadian
**Ref:** Lévi F, Okyar A, Dulong S, Innominato PF, Clairambault J, Annu Rev Pharmacol Toxicol 50:377-421 (2010). DOI 10.1146/annurev.pharmtox.48.113006.094626. Type: review. Lab: Lévi (Villejuif).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Review of circadian timing of cancer drugs: rodent chronotolerance (40 drugs), cellular pharmacology, clinical trials of chronomodulated delivery, host-clock disruption, sex, mathematical models. No new data.
**Sampling:** not applicable; discusses trials with timed drug delivery and human circadian markers (rest-activity, cortisol, temperature) measured in patients.

## Key results
- Rodents: "A potentially lethal dose of any of these agents results in 2-fold to more than 10-fold changes in the incidence of toxic deaths and/or maximum body weight loss as a function of circadian timing of drug administration." (Chronotolerance, 40 drugs, Table 1).
- Clinical optimum: peak delivery 3:57 a.m. (90% limits 11:30 p.m.-9:36 a.m.) for 5-FU-leucovorin and 3:57 p.m. (11:30 a.m.-9:36 p.m.) for oxaliplatin, from the randomised optimisation design; lung cancer trial (n = 45) shifted peaks by 8 h, severe toxicity 6.7% on the reference profile vs up to 40%.
- Randomised irinotecan (n = 31, 350 mg/m2): chronomodulated (peak 5 a.m.) vs conventional ~10 a.m. infusion, mean Cmax 5.53 to 2.91 ug/ml (p = 0.00012).
- Efficacy: two randomised European trials (278 patients) chronoFLO5 vs constant rate, response 29% vs 51% (p < 0.001), no survival difference; the 564-patient chronoFLO4 vs FOLFOX2 trial: overall survival not different. "the relative risk of an earlier death on chronoFLO4 significantly increased by 38% in women and significantly decreased by 25% in men" (chronoFLO4 trial). Meta-analysis of the three trials, 842 patients: similar or worse efficacy in women, better response and survival in men.
- Hypotheses for the sex-by-schedule interaction: different circadian genotype profile, sex-dependent pharmacology, and excess toxicity in women (20-50% more) causing circadian disruption.
- Patient clocks: "Nearly a third of patients with metastatic breast or colorectal cancer displayed poor rhythms in salivary cortisol and/or rest-activity" before chemotherapy (section on personalisation); disruption an independent prognostic factor.

## Not addressed / limits
- Narrative review by the group that ran most of the trials; sex-specific results are retrospective subgroup analyses, not pre-specified. The survival null of the large trial is only partly emphasised.
- No tissue-level human clock data; tumour-clock statements rest on mRNA of clock genes in tumours (down-regulated or deregulated in breast, lung, colon, endometrium, ovary, pancreas, marrow), without phase information.
