# brown2002rhythms
**Ref:** Brown, Zumbrunn, Fleury-Olela, Preitner, Schibler, Curr Biol 2002. DOI 10.1016/S0960-9822(02)01145-4. Type: biology. Lab: Schibler.
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Can body-temperature rhythms entrain or sustain peripheral clocks? (i) Rat-1 fibroblasts under 12 h/12 h square waves
(DT 2, 4, 10 degrees C), single shifts, and mouse core-temperature profiles (telemetry, peritoneal) replayed with a computer-driven
incubator (0.1 degrees C accuracy); harvest every 4 h for 1 day. (ii) Mice in 24 h ambient temperature cycles (37/24 degrees C)
in phase or out of phase with light for 6 days, then Dbp, Per2, Cry1 in liver (RNase protection) and Dbp in SCN (in situ).
Numbers of animals not stated in the text; two independent experiments for the real-time PCR. **Sampling:** timed;
4 h sampling over one full day after multi-day entrainment.

## Method (if any)
None; real-time PCR/RNase protection, ANOVA and sine regression of the mean profiles.

## Key results
- A 4 degrees C square wave (12 h 37, 12 h 33) induces circadian expression (Dbp p = 3e-4, Per2 p = 1e-7, Bmal1 p = 0.03, Cry1 p = 0.002 versus constant); 2 degrees C is insufficient and 10 degrees C gives random fluctuations (Fig. 1A).
- Seven days of cycles give about 5-fold higher Dbp amplitude than a single temperature drop (Fig. 1B).
- Replayed natural body temperature cannot start rhythms de novo, but maintains them: "they could maintain previously induced rhythms for multiple days; by contrast, the rhythms of control cells kept at constant temperature rapidly dampened." (Abstract)
  Only one of two relative phases sustained rhythmicity, and the effect disappears about 1 day after the cycles stop (Fig. 3).
- In vivo, inverted ambient temperature cycles invert liver phase: Dbp peak at ZT23 instead of ZT9, similar for Per2 and Cry1, while the SCN Dbp peak stays at ZT5 (Fig. 4).
- Authors attribute the in vivo effect to both temperature and feeding behaviour changes (mice also eat at a different time).

## Not addressed / limits
- Liver is the only peripheral tissue tested in vivo (our "tissues" is therefore liver); body temperature and feeding are not separated in vivo.
- Natural temperature profiles do not initiate rhythms; the Dbp output gene failed to cycle under natural profiles (the authors "do not fully understand" it).
- Mouse and rat cells only; no human evidence; no amplitude or phase-shift curve for temperature.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** body temperature is a candidate systemic cue coupling clocks (Internal desynchrony).
  refs entry (entrains peripheral cells and tissues but not the SCN) is slightly overstated: natural profiles only sustain existing
  rhythms in vitro, and in vivo only liver and SCN were tested.
- **Relation to clocksync:** background. Candidate source of the shared host state; also a reason why fever or
  hypothermia in patients can shift peripheral phases.
