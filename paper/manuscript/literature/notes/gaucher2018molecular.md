# gaucher2018molecular
**Ref:** Gaucher J, Montellier E, Sassone-Corsi P, Trends Cell Biol 2018;28(5):368-379. DOI 10.1016/j.tcb.2018.01.006. Type: review. Lab: Sassone-Corsi.
**Source:** full text (review; concluding remarks present, no reference list in the extract)

## Question and design
Review of molecular links between the circadian clock and the cell cycle (Wee1, p21, p16, c-Myc, DNA-damage checkpoints, shared kinases and F-box ligases). Mostly mouse tissues, NIH3T3 and human oral mucosa; no new data. **Sampling:** not applicable (literature synthesis). Tumour clocks are not measured or compared with host clocks in any cited example of this review.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Clock controls cell-cycle genes: "c-myc is another gene that is potentially regulated by the circadian clock", with Wee1 (CLOCK-BMAL1 E boxes), p21 (ROR/REV-ERB at a RORE) and p16 (NONO/PER) as the best-documented targets (sections "Molecular Dissection of the Coupling").
- Bidirectional coupling: in NIH3T3 single cells "the cell cycle and circadian clock have been shown to be phase locked in a 1:1 ratio, with the clock reporter Rev-Erba reproducibly peaking 5 h after mitosis" (ref 44; cell line, no external resetting cues).
- Dexamethasone synchronisation of the clock clusters divisions: "Synchronization of the circadian clock by physiological cues (such as the corticosteroid dexamethasone) leads to clustering of cell divisions" (Coupling Two Oscillators).
- Coupling depends on proliferation state: "cells in organs such as the brain, liver, and heart have a low proliferation rate", so coupling is hard to show there; after partial hepatectomy hepatocytes re-enter the cycle synchronously in a clock-dependent way (ref 7).
- The clock is not required for cell division: "the circadian clock is not an obligatory component of the cell cycle" (clock-mutant mice are viable).
- Cancer link is stated, not shown: "both of these oscillators are frequently misregulated in cancer cells" (abstract); no quantitative tumour-versus-host clock data.

## Not addressed / limits
- Species and perturbation are heterogeneous (yeast, Drosophila, Neurospora, mouse, cell lines); no tumour-specific clock measurement, no tumour-host phase or amplitude comparison, no human tumour data.
- Coupling evidence is from proliferating cultures and regenerating tissue; direction and strength in solid tumours in vivo are open.
