# mctiernan2021circadian
**Ref:** McTiernan et al., Circulation: Arrhythmia and Electrophysiology 2021 (14:e009254; "Circadian Pattern of Ion Channel Gene Expression in Failing Human Hearts"). DOI 10.1161/CIRCEP.120.009254; PMC7875252. Type: biology (human heart tissue, RT-PCR). Lab: Pittsburgh (University of Pittsburgh Heart and Vascular Institute); senior authors not recorded here.
**Source:** full text (PMC author manuscript, retrieved as NCBI efetch XML; figures and the supplement (Fig s1, s2, Tables s1, s2, online methods) not read)

## Question and design
Do ion-channel and clock transcripts show a 24 h pattern in failing human left ventricle? Full-thickness transmural LV tissue from end-stage heart failure patients at cardiac transplantation, two sets: F1 n = 37 and F2 n = 45 (the abstract says 46; the text and Table 1 say 45), mostly non-ischemic (35/2 and 44/2). Third set NF: n = 27 LV samples from non-failing hearts of brain-dead donors whose hearts were not suitable for transplantation (Gift of Life Donor Program and Cleveland Clinic). Five clock genes (ARNTL, DBP, NFIL3, NR1D1, PER2) plus 19 ion-channel genes by RT-PCR (GAPDH reference). **Sampling:** a pseudo-time series from collection time, converted to hours after sunrise (HASR) for the day of collection; collection times spread over 24 h (Rayleigh and Rao tests, uniform). Brain-death to cross-clamp interval in NF: mean 22.8 h (SD 16.9; range 4-74, median 18; n = 19 with data). All donors are brain-dead; no circulatory-death donors are mentioned. The text does not state whether NF "collection time" is procurement or explant time (Fig s1 is not in the main text).

## Method (if any)
Moving-window mean (9 consecutive samples F1, 11 F2, 7 NF) then Chronos-Fit (partial Fourier, F-fit, acrophase, MESOR, % rhythm). Acrophases compared with mouse heart (Yan 2008) and human brain (Li 2013) by circular correlation.

## Key results
- Failing hearts give nearly identical acrophases in both sets (Table 2, HASR; % rhythm): NR1D1 23.92 / 21.01 (95.6 / 91.9), ARNTL 16.25 / 14.47 (91.8 / 87.0), DBP 2.95 / 3.47 (89.9 / 81.8), PER2 4.51 / 3.66 (72.6 / 68.4); NFIL3 12.68 (72.3) in one set only. **Labelling inconsistency in the source (checked by the lead, 2026-11-19):** Table 2 lists the first value of each pair as set 1 and says NFIL3 was not performed on set 1, while Table 4 gives the same values (23.92, 16.25, 2.95, 4.51, 12.68) as F2; cite the values, not the set label.
- Human vs mouse heart: "a similar temporal order of core circadian clock genes expression between failing human LV and normal mouse hearts (Circular Correlation Coefficient = 0.677)", human advanced by ~7.6 h (Results; Fig 3a). Versus human brain: ~1.56 h advanced, circular correlation 0.227 (Fig 3b).
- Brain-dead donors (Table 4, NF vs F2; acrophase HASR, % rhythm): NR1D1 4.61 (53.9) vs 23.92 (95.6); ARNTL 10.2 (62.2) vs 16.25 (91.8); DBP 3.4 (80.8) vs 2.95 (89.9); PER2 none (0) vs 4.51 (72.6); NFIL3 none (0) vs 12.68 (72.3). "Relative to the F2 set, the NF set showed marked acrophase shifts (ARNTL, NR1D1), or complete loss of circadian rhythmicity (NFIL3, PER2), in 4 of the 5 core circadian clock gene transcripts" (Results, Figure 5).
- Sample-size control: a failing subset F2/27 matched to the NF collection times keeps rhythms (Table 4: NR1D1 87.7, ARNTL 72.8, DBP 85.7, PER2 60.8, NFIL3 44.3 % rhythm), so n = 27 alone does not explain the loss. No p-values are given in the text; fit quality is reported as % rhythm.
- Ion channels in F2 (Table 3): SCN5A, KCNA5, KCNB1, SLC8A1, KCNA4 with ~70% or more variance in a 24 h pattern; KCNH2 not rhythmic.
- Authors' reading of brain death: "Organ donors, which may be maintained for over 60 hours after declaration of brain death, have alterations in their adrenal-pituitary axis function" and are "exposed to altered environmental cues of light and feeding/nutritional support" (Discussion). They state "this appears to be the first report of alterations in cardiac circadian clock gene expression in brain-dead donors."

## Not addressed / limits
- One organ only (left ventricle); no second organ from the same donor, so no comparison between organs and no inter-organ order. Cross-organ comparison is only against published mouse heart and human brain acrophases from other cohorts (population-level, other studies). The only between-organ remark is that heart clock genes appear phase advanced relative to brain, "data not shown".
- Different persons per set; cohort-level pseudo-time series, no within-person information. NF and F sets come from different centres and diseases, so brain death is confounded with set, centre and non-failing status; the NF set is also 70% female (19/27) against about 38% in F sets.
- Time of the NF samples not defined in the main text; hours after sunrise only.
- Moving-window smoothing and a single-component cosine fit; no formal test of NF vs F difference.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** in brain-dead donor hearts the core clock transcripts can be phase shifted or lose rhythmicity (against our expectation of kept amplitude in brain-dead donors); a clock readout of an organ from donors with cohort-level time is hard to compare across sets.
- **Relation to clocksync:** background; tension with amplitude kept in brain-dead donors (our GTEx result, hours to days of ICU). We believe they did not study the order between organs: confirmed for this paper (single organ, no paired organs).

## Ideas
- Their loss of NFIL3 and PER2 rhythm uses a pseudo-time of unknown definition; a within-donor, untimed read (our host-referenced approach) would not depend on it.
- Earlier donor-heart/clock references they cite: Leibetseder V et al., "Clock genes display rhythmic expression in human hearts", Chronobiol Int 2009;26:621-36 (ref 26); Ruben MD et al., "A database of tissue-specific rhythmically expressed human genes has potential applications in circadian medicine", Sci Transl Med 2018;10:eaat8806 (ref 27, postmortem GTEx, atria but not ventricles); Li JZ et al., "Circadian patterns of gene expression in the human brain and disruption in major depressive disorder", PNAS 2013;110:9950-5 (ref 31); Yan J et al., "Analysis of gene regulatory networks in the mammalian circadian rhythm", PLoS Comput Biol 2008;4:e1000193 (ref 30, mouse heart acrophases); Montaigne D et al., Lancet 2018;391:59-69 (ref 39, morning vs afternoon surgery). On brain-death physiology: Inaba K et al., J Trauma 2010;68:1362-6 (ref 40); Kotloff RM et al., Crit Care Med 2015;43:1291-325 (ref 41); Boone DR et al., PLoS One 2012;7:e46204 (ref 42, TBI and clock); Paul T, Lemmer B, Chronobiol Int 2007;24:45-61 (ref 43, ICU rhythm disturbance).
