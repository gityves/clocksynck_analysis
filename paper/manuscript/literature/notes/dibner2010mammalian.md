# dibner2010mammalian
**Ref:** Dibner, Schibler & Albrecht, Annu Rev Physiol 2010 (72:517-549). doi:10.1146/annurev-physiol-021909-135821. Type: review. Lab: Schibler (Geneva).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Review of the mammalian timing system: SCN, brain clocks, peripheral clocks, entrainment cues (light, feeding, temperature,
glucocorticoids), food-entrainable and methamphetamine-sensitive oscillators, reward system, circadian metabolism. Rodents and
cell culture, with some human genetics. **Sampling:** not applicable; synthesis of timed studies.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Architecture: "the circadian timing system is composed of virtually as many clocks as there are cells".
- SCN coupling: "the SCN oscillators never desynchronize in animals deprived of external timing cues", whereas cultured cells drift apart: "Fibroblasts kept in culture display robust oscillations, but because individual cells have slightly different periods, they desynchronize over time".
- Feeding as Zeitgeber: "Strikingly, daytime-restricted feeding of nocturnal rodents inverts the phase of gene expression in peripheral organs, thereby uncoupling peripheral clocks from the SCN".
- Many parallel signalling pathways (neuronal, endocrine, temperature, behaviour) reset peripheral phase, making dissection difficult.
- Human associations (genetic): SAD correlates with SNPs in BMAL1, NPAS2, PER2; hPER2 linked to blood glucose; MTNR1B variants and hyperglycaemia (reference list numbers 146, 255, 258-259).
- Plasma glucocorticoids oscillate "in both laboratory rodents and humans", driven by the SCN through the HPA axis.

## Not addressed / limits
- No quantitative phase offsets between organs and no human tissue data; disease links are genetic association statements.
- No discussion of cancer clocks or of inferring synchrony from untimed samples.
- 2010 review: later results (single-cell, GTEx, human peripheral phases) are absent.
