# husse2014lightdark
**Ref:** Husse et al., FASEB J 2014 (28:4950-4960). DOI 10.1096/fj.14-256594. Type: biology. Lab: Oster / Eichele.
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Does the SCN clock (not the SCN itself) synchronise peripheral clocks? Syt10-Cre;Bmal1 fl/- (SCN clock deleted, SCN
neurons and their connections intact) vs Bmal1 +/- littermates. qPCR of adrenal, liver, kidney, heart (n = 3-5 per
time point; LD and day 2 of DD); PER2::LUC explants of the same organs from the same mice (n = 10 per genotype) in LD
and on day 7 of DD; fecal corticosterone every 4 h (n = 5); 6 h jet lag. **Sampling:** 4-6 time points per day (qPCR);
explant peak = second peak in culture.

## Method (if any)
CircWave harmonic regression (peak, amplitude); Rayleigh tests of explant peak times; SD of peaks between animals and
within animal (between tissues).

## Key results
- LD keeps the organ map: "peripheral clocks remained rhythmic and synchronized to the LD cycle, and phase relationships between peripheral clocks were sustained." (Abstract). Peaks correlate with controls, slope near 1, whole map advanced about 2.4 h.
- Amplitude in LD: "peripheral clocks maintained high amplitude rhythmicity with stable phase relationships under LD conditions" (Discussion); slight reductions for some genes.
- DD: day 2 rhythms persist but dampened; corticosterone rhythm lost by day 7; explant peaks on day 7 no longer clustered across mice, with "increased internal desynchrony" between tissues of a mouse (Fig 5D-F).
- Jet lag: resetting much faster without the SCN clock (heart and kidney complete the 6 h advance within 2 days).
- Model: a light pathway that bypasses the SCN clock (autonomic efferents, light-driven behaviour and feeding, corticosterone) synchronises the periphery.

## Not addressed / limits
- Explant phases (dissection reset); only 7 days of DD; per-mouse organ phases in figures only.
- The SCN tissue and its efferents remain: not equivalent to losing the brain (contrast: SCN lesion, brain death).

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** an intact central drive keeps the organs' order; when the SCN clock is lost, light-driven signals still impose the same order (the order belongs to the drive, not to the SCN clock per se); with no drive, organs drift within days.
- **Relation to clocksync:** supports (with Izumo 2014, A319). Timescale: tissue rhythms dampen within 2 days and organs desynchronise by day 7 in DD, faster than Izumo's > 30 days: the A315 "amplitude kept" in brain-dead donors must be stated for ventilated, ICU-cued donors only.

## Ideas
- Faster resetting without the SCN clock = smaller weight of the conflicting central drive (our weight b; Saini 2013).
