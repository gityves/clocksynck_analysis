# yamaguchi2013mice
**Ref:** Yamaguchi, Suzuki, Mizoro, Kori, Okada, Chen, Fustin, Yamazaki, Mizuguchi, Zhang, Dong, Tsujimoto, Okuno, Doi, Okamura, Science 2013. DOI 10.1126/science.1238599. Type: biology. Lab: Okamura.
**Source:** full text (figure axis text is garbled in the extraction; numbers below are from running text and legends)

## Question and design
Which SCN signal slows re-entrainment after a light shift? Vasopressin receptor double knockout mice (V1a-/-V1b-/-) vs wild type (WT), infrared
or wheel-running activity (n = 10-12), 8 h advance and 8 h delay of LD; SCN clock genes (Per1, Per2, Bmal1, Dbp) by qRT-PCR in laser-microdissected
SCN collected every 4 h continuously through the shift (average of 2 mice per point); liver (and kidney) clock genes the same way; body
temperature by peritoneal probe (n = 7); Per1-luc SCN slices with cycloheximide perturbation; a three-oscillator model; pharmacological
V1a/V1b blockade in WT SCN.
**Sampling:** timed, 4 h resolution daily for about 10 days after the shift; pooled mice (n = 2) per point.

## Key results
- Behaviour, advance: WT "took 8 to 10 days for complete reentrainment to the new LD schedule" (Results); knockouts 2-4 days. Delay: WT 5-6 days, knockouts 1 day; PS50 smaller in knockouts, P < 0.001; single knockouts intermediate.
- Not a masking artefact: larger free-running shifts in DD after 1-4 days of advance; period (about 24 h), PRC and canonical clock genes in the knockout SCN normal.
- SCN in WT loses rhythmicity: "In WT mice, Per1 and Per2 expression lost rhythmicity on days 1 to 2", low-amplitude on days 3-7, recovered day 8; knockouts recovered peak time and amplitude by day 3.
- Liver keeps amplitude but shifts slowly: "clock gene rhythm amplitudes in the liver were maintained during reentrainment" while peak time needed 9-10 days (WT) vs 5 days (knockout) after the 8 h advance; kidney similar (fig. S14).
- Body temperature (peak ZT16) reentrained in 5 days (knockout) vs 10 days (WT).
- SCN leads the periphery by 1-2 days in both genotypes: "reestablishment of the central SCN clock occurred" 1 to 2 days earlier than liver or temperature, "which suggests that the SCN clock determines the recovery period of the whole body".
- Slices: stable cell phase order in WT recovers after cycloheximide (r = 0.787 vs 0.439 in knockout), i.e. weaker coupling in the mutant.

## Not addressed / limits
- Tissue offsets are shown as peak-time kinetics in days (8 h shift), not as a stable steady-state SCN-liver offset in hours; liver value is a single qPCR time series with n = 2 per point.
- Whole-body knockouts: effect of peripheral V1 receptors not separated; one genotype pair; males not stated in the text.
- The beneficial effect of weaker SCN coupling is specific to light shifts; tells nothing about health costs, which the intro does not need.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** supported. refs_circadian_desynchrony.md reads SCN internal coupling slows jet lag recovery: correct (WT SCN rhythmicity is lost for days; stronger coupling = more inertia). The paper also gives a tissue comparison: SCN back in phase 1-2 days before liver, liver 9-10 days in WT after an 8 h advance.
- **Relation to clocksync:** background; counterpoint to the "coupling c" word: here coupling inside the SCN is a resistance to perturbation, not synchrony with the periphery.

## Ideas
- Our coupling c is between compartments of one donor, not between cells of the SCN; state this distinction in the intro, with Yamaguchi as the example where tighter coupling slows adaptation.
