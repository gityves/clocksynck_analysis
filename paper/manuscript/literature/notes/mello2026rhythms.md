# mello2026rhythms
**Ref:** Mello, Masri, Lamia, J Clin Invest 136(3):e198780 (2026). DOI 10.1172/JCI198780. Type: review. Lab: Katja Lamia (Scripps) / Selma Masri (UC Irvine).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Review of the clock-cancer axis: effects of environmental misalignment and clock gene perturbation on tumour growth, clock control of cell cycle, DNA repair, hypoxia and metastasis, clock-targeted drugs, and timing of treatment. No data, no sampling design.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Context dependence: "This duality likely reflects systemic rewiring of circadian physiology and direct interactions between clock components and oncogenic pathways."
- Terminology: the three terms describe "distinct perturbations: environmentally induced desynchrony, misalignment between internal and external cues, or molecular disruption of the clock, respectively" (useful for our usage of desynchrony).
- Mouse misalignment: environmental misalignment "exacerbates tumor growth in murine models, including xenograft tumors grown from osteosarcoma or melanoma cells" but "it does not affect tumor burden in a lymphoma model driven by transgenic expression of c-MYC".
- Human tumour clocks: "Reduced coordination of circadian gene expression is a common feature of many cancers" (cites Shilts 2018); "However, some tumors retain robust circadian rhythms"; "It is unknown what determines the degree to which circadian rhythms degrade in tumors" (MYC amplification proposed).
- Chronotherapy: "patients with advanced gastric (148), kidney (149), or melanoma (150) cancers have improved outcomes with morning dosing of ICI compared with patients treated later in the day"; NSCLC results conflict "suggesting factors such as chronotype, tumor-specific circadian profiles, or patient lifestyle may influence therapeutic window".
- Gaps: "variability in individual rhythms, and the need for robust biomarkers like blood-based chronotyping"; "Prospective trials that incorporate patient chronotype and tumor-specific circadian profiles will be key to advancing the field".

## Not addressed / limits
- Immune-tumour clock links are not a focus: immune function appears in the abstract, the hallmarks figure and ICI timing, with a few mouse BMAL1 examples (colon tumour immune landscape). No account of immune cell clock phases in tumours.
- Whole-clock measures in patients are not discussed beyond one citation; no methods for phase inference.
- Time-of-day trials are retrospective and conflicting; the review names chronotype as the explanation but no method to measure it from tissue.
