# leminh2001glucocorticoid
**Ref:** Le Minh, Damiola, Tronche, Schutz, Schibler, EMBO J 2001 (20:7128-7136). DOI 10.1093/emboj/20.24.7128. Type: biology. Lab: Schibler (Geneva).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Why does food-induced phase resetting of peripheral clocks take days? Test of glucocorticoid signalling as an active brake. Mice under 12 h light-dark (ZT0 lights on) switched from ad libitum to daytime feeding (food removed at ZT12, then light phase only). Groups: sham-operated vs adrenalectomized mice, and wild-type vs GRAlfpCre mice (glucocorticoid-receptor null allele in hepatocytes only). Tissues liver, kidney (and SCN by in situ hybridization). Readouts: mRNA of mPer1, mPer2, mCry1, Dbp (RNase protection); serum corticosterone.
**Sampling:** timed. 4 h intervals over the day, on day 2 of daytime feeding, after 1 week of daytime or night feeding, and 32-64 h after a reverse switch. Corticosterone: blood from 5-7 mice per time point. Per-group animal numbers for the mRNA profiles are not given in the text.

## Method (if any)
Not a method; comparison of peak phase of mRNA accumulation across genotypes and kinetic stages. No fitted phase or amplitude estimate.

## Key results
- Brake present. On the second day of daytime feeding, sham livers and kidneys have Dbp peak and trough at about ZT14 and ZT2, only about 4 h from ad libitum or night-fed mice (text: peak and trough levels offset by about 4 h for some mRNAs); in adrenalectomized mice "much larger phase shifts are observed", with Dbp peak in liver between ZT0 and ZT4. GRAlfpCre livers on day 2 are shifted "to values close to those observed 1 week after the animals had been subjected to the new feeding regimen"; kidneys of GRAlfpCre mice are only moderately advanced. The brake acts through the hepatocyte receptor in liver.
- Full inversion in intact animals takes 7-10 days ("the phase ... becomes completely inverted after 7-10 days", citing Damiola 2000), fastest in liver, slower in kidney.
- The brake is direction-specific. Switching back to night feeding: "the night-time entrained phase of circadian gene expression is already approached 2-3 days after changing the feeding regimen" in liver (Dbp zenith ZT8-ZT12 on day 2), kidney lagging by about 2 h; glucocorticoid signalling has "little if any effect" there. Conclusion: glucocorticoid signalling "slows down exclusively phase changes provoked by a feeding regimen that is in conflict with the daily activity phase".
- Steady-state phase is not set by the brake: identical in GRAlfpCre and wild-type mice, and "glucocorticoids are not required for the phase setting of circadian oscillators in liver and kidney" with food throughout the day or at night; after 1 week of daytime feeding, corticosterone is bimodal (peak ZT20-ZT4 with zenith at ZT0; broader peak ZT4-ZT20, zenith ZT8-ZT12), both about 200 ng/ml. The second wave is attributed to the SCN, and the first to feeding.
- SCN phase of Per1 and Per2 is unchanged by feeding time or glucocorticoid status.
- Abstract: "Thus, glucocorticoid hormones inhibit the uncoupling of peripheral and central circadian oscillators by altered feeding time."

## Not addressed / limits
- Transient kinetics only (day 2); no amplitude is measured or discussed, no per-tissue amplitude.
- Liver and kidney only (heart, pancreas cited from earlier work); single genes by RNase protection; no numbers on animals per profile in the text.
- Whether the brake is SCN-driven or feeding-driven corticosterone is undecided: "It is, thus, difficult to decide whether the circadian glucocorticoid secretion controlled by the SCN or that provoked by restricted feeding is responsible".
- Does not say the brake persists at steady state; after 1 week the inverted phase is established despite the second corticosterone peak.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** supports an active, hormone-mediated brake on conflicting-cue resetting: the SCN or its output opposes behavioural entrainment during the transition (compare saini2013realtime, same lab line). It concerns the speed of phase resetting, not the steady-state amplitude.
- **Relation to clocksync:** background. The brake explanation and our saturation model make different predictions only in transients (day 2 phase), not at the 30-day steady states we analyse (Manella 30 d DF, Xin); at steady state the paper says the brake does not stop full inversion.

## Ideas
- Test that the brake cannot explain amplitude: A297 steady states (30 d) are long after the 7-10 d inversion time, so a brake accounting for a smaller liver amplitude would need a persistent effect, which this paper does not show.
- Adrenalectomy or GR-KO plus daytime feeding with a long-term amplitude readout (not available here); search for a public dataset of liver time series in GR-KO under restricted feeding.
- Reference we lack: Damiola 2000 (inversion in 7-10 days), Stokkan 2001, Balsalobre 2000.
