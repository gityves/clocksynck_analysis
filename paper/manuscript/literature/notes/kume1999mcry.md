# kume1999mcry
**Ref:** Kume et al., Cell 1999 (98:193-205). Type: biology. Lab: Reppert (with Hastings).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Mouse cell lines (NIH3T3, COS7) and mice: coimmunoprecipitation, localisation, luciferase reporters (vasopressin and mPer1
promoters), mCry1/mCry2 RNA in SCN (in situ) and skeletal muscle (Northern), SCN immunocytochemistry in wild-type and
Clock/Clock mice. **Sampling:** timed. SCN RNA: n = 4 animals per mean in DD; muscle in 12L:12D at eight time points (ZT, two
replicate blots); SCN protein every 2 h over 24 h in DD (n = 3 animals per time). Full 24 h coverage, but only for SCN and muscle.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- mPER proteins are weak repressors: "Maximal inhibition reached 55%-70% for each construct" and mPER pairs were not synergistic (Fig. 1C).
- mCry1 RNA cycles in wild-type SCN (ANOVA p < 0.05) and not in Clock/Clock; "no mCry1 RNA rhythm was apparent in the SCN of Clock/Clock mice" (Fig. 2A). mCry2 does not cycle in SCN but is lower in Clock/Clock.
- In skeletal muscle both mCry RNAs cycle: "The peak of the mCry2 rhythm preceded that of mCry1 by 6 to 9 hr" and the mCry1 rhythm is delayed relative to SCN (Fig. 2B); a peripheral phase lag, no quantitative lag estimate.
- CRY is the strong repressor: "mCRY1 and mCRY2, on the other hand, inhibited CLOCK:BMAL1-induced transcription by" more than 90% from either reporter (Fig. 3).
- mCRYs translocate mPER1/2 to the nucleus (>90% nucleus-only vs <30% alone; Fig. 6) and oscillate in SCN: "The abundance of both proteins was low in the early subjective day, rising in later subjective day to peak at CT12-CT16" (Fig. 7).
- mPER does not inhibit MOP4:BMAL1 whereas mCRY does (37-fold activation by MOP4:BMAL1, Fig. 8).

## Not addressed / limits
- The Cry1/Cry2 double-knockout arrhythmicity is NOT tested here; it is cited from another paper: "Amazingly, mice lacking both mCRY1 and mCRY2 exhibit a complete loss of circadian rhythmicity in wheel-running behavior" (Introduction, van der Horst 1999).
- Repression is shown by overexpression in cell lines with endogenous mPer/mCry background; no endogenous knockout transcriptional data.
- Only SCN and skeletal muscle in vivo; no human data.
