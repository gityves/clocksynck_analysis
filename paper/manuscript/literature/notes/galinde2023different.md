# galinde2023different
**Ref:** Galinde, Al-Mughales, Oster, Heyde, F1000Research 11:1323 (2023, v2). DOI 10.12688/f1000research.127234.2. Type: review. Lab: Oster (Lübeck).
**Source:** full text.

## Question and design
Narrative review of "chronodisruption" at four levels of organisation, mostly rodent and a few human epidemiology / laboratory studies. No data, no method. **Sampling:** not applicable.

## Method (if any)
None. It gives a taxonomy: "(1) a mismatch of external time and internal circadian rhythms (external-internal desynchrony), (2) phase incoherence between different tissue clocks (inter-tissue desynchrony), (3) phase incoherence within a tissue clock (intra-tissue desynchrony)", with (3) split into inter-cell (3a) and molecular (3b) desynchrony.

## Key results
- Inter-tissue desynchrony from mouse studies: SCN ablation removes peripheral rhythms; "Animals with an ablated SCN clock show internal desynchrony, i.e., phase incoherence between different tissue clocks, within a few days under DD conditions", prevented by time-restricted feeding.
- Feeding as zeitgeber: tissues strongly driven by feeding (liver, kidney) "can be uncoupled from the SCN inducing disturbance in intertissue synchrony" (Damiola, Stokkan).
- Human data covered are epidemiological: shift workers show higher triglyceride, abdominal obesity and dampened cortisol and testosterone rhythms; the cancer link is inconsistent ("a recent meta-analysis shows overall no significant association").
- Evidence status: "Although inter-tissue desynchrony is an established concept, experimental evidence is still sparse and, therefore, the physiological outcomes are not well understood."
- Cell level: "Exploring circadian characteristics at the single-cell level within a tissue is technically challenging and not thoroughly studied yet."

## Not addressed / limits
- It does not call for human biomarkers of internal desynchrony, nor discuss omics-based inference from human samples; no mention of CYCLOPS-type tools or GTEx-type data.
- Causality of desynchrony for disease is stated as unproven ("causality has yet to be proven").
- Inter-tissue evidence is almost entirely mouse; human sections are about external-internal misalignment.
