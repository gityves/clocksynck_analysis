# vetter2016association
**Ref:** Vetter, Devore, Wegrzyn, ..., Schernhammer, JAMA 2016 (315:1726-1734). DOI 10.1001/jama.2016.4454. Type: clinical (epidemiology). Lab: Schernhammer / Channing (Harvard).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Prospective cohort. "Prospective cohort study of 189 158 initially healthy women followed up over 24 years" in the Nurses' Health Study (NHS, 1988-2012, n = 73 623, 7303 CHD cases, mean baseline age 54.5 y) and NHS2 (1989-2013, n = 115 535, 3519 cases, mean age 34.8 y). **Sampling:** no biological samples, no circadian measure; questionnaire exposure.
Exposure: lifetime years of rotating night shift work (at least 3 night shifts per month in addition to day and evening shifts), baseline in NHS (asked once, 1988), updated every 2-4 y in NHS2. Outcome: incident CHD (nonfatal MI, CHD death, angiogram-confirmed angina, CABG, stents, angioplasty). Cox models, multivariable.

## Method (if any)
Not a method paper. Cox proportional hazards, baseline versus cumulatively updated exposure, trend tests, interaction with follow-up time and BMI.

## Key results
- NHS baseline: HR (reference no shift work) <5 y 1.02, 5-9 y 1.12, >=10 y 1.18. "multivariable HR for ≥10 years, 1.18 [95% CI, 1.10-1.26]" (5-9 y: 1.12, 1.02-1.22; P trend < .001).
- NHS2 baseline: 5-9 y 1.12 (0.99-1.26), >=10 y 1.15. "multivariable HR for ≥10 years, 1.15 [95% CI, 1.01-1.32]" (P = .01 for trend).
- Waning risk: "27% vs 13% increased risk for ≥10 years of rotating night shift work exposure" in first versus second half of follow-up in the NHS (P = .02 interaction).
- NHS2: risk falls with time since quitting. "Longer time since quitting shift work was associated with decreased CHD risk among ever shift workers in the NHS2"
- Absolute effect small. "a statistically significant but small absolute increase in CHD risk"

## Not addressed / limits
- Women, nurses only; self-reported exposure with no intensity. "The exposure assessments lacked information on intensity of night shift work and physiological measures that may be affected by shift work."
- No circadian, clock or molecular readout; mechanism (misalignment, sleep, diet, weight) not separated. Residual confounding acknowledged.
- Different rotation schedules, chronotype and permanent night work not captured.
