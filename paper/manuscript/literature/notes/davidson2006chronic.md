# davidson2006chronic
**Ref:** Davidson, Sellix, Daniel, Yamazaki, Menaker, Block, Curr Biol 2006. DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2006.09.058. Type: biology (Correspondence, 2 pages). Lab: Block / Menaker.
**Source:** full text (the extracted text starts with the end of an unrelated review; the paper begins at "Chronic jet-lag increases mortality in aged mice")

## Question and design
Does repeated phase shifting of the light cycle shorten life in aged mice? C57BL/6 males, young (8-12 months, n = 9 per group) and aged
(27-31 months, n = 28-30 per group), 8 weeks on LD 12:12 (control), a 6 h advance every 7 days, or a 6 h delay every 7 days; a second
experiment shifted every 4 days (13 advancers, 12 delayers). Readout: survival; fecal corticosterone. No clock gene or tissue phase was measured.
**Sampling:** survival over 8 weeks (daily); not a circadian time series.

## Key results
- Aged mice, weekly shifts: "On Day 56 survival was 47% in advancers, 68% in delayers, and 83% in unshifted aged mice" (Fig. 1A legend); chi-square across groups p < 0.05 at day 54; advancers vs controls p < 0.01.
- Young mice unaffected: "While younger mice fared well on this 8 week schedule (only one death occurred)" (Results).
- Faster shifting worsens advancers: "advancers died more quickly than with weekly shifts" (Results); 60% survival at day 24, delayers better (p < 0.05, day 32).
- Not stress: "Importantly, chronic stress was not implicated in this phenomenon as total daily fecal corticosterone levels did not increase" in aged mice (Results).
- Prior observation: 3 of 8 aged transgenic rats died after a 6 h advance, none after a delay.
- Speculation only: "Our results lead us to speculate that the internal desynchrony among these functional oscillations" accompanying advance may be harmful, worse in the aged (Discussion).

## Not addressed / limits
- No measurement of any peripheral or central clock; the link to internal desynchrony is an inference from Yamazaki 2000 style data, not shown here.
- Cause of death not determined; frailty versus age-related clock changes is left open by the authors; males only; one strain.
- The asymmetry (advance worse than delay) fits slower tissue re-entrainment after advances but no tissue data are provided.
