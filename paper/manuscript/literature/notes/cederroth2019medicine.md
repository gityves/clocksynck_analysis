# cederroth2019medicine
**Ref:** Cederroth CR et al. (Canlon corresponding), Cell Metab 30:238-250, 2019. DOI 10.1016/j.cmet.2019.06.019. Type: review (Perspective). Lab: Canlon (multi-lab consensus: Albrecht, Bass, Brown, Hastings, Hogenesch, Levi, Takahashi, Young and others).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Perspective on integrating circadian biology into pre-clinical and clinical drug development (chronopharmacology). No new data. **Sampling:** not applicable; the cited human work is dosing-time comparisons (morning vs evening dosing, chronomodulated infusion), not tissue sampling over 24 h.

## Method (if any)
None.

## Key results
- Human outcomes depend on time of day. Cardiac surgery: "lower risk when the surgery is performed in the afternoon" than in the morning (cites Montaigne 2018); REV-ERBa myocardial expression "greater expression of REV-ERBa in the human myocardium in the morning".
- Hospital routine conflicts with internal timing: "enforced continuous activity throughout the day and night," (maintained lighting, night feeding, drug delivery out of phase with metabolising enzymes).
- Oxaliplatin case: stopped after phase I toxicity, rescued by chronomodulated infusion (peak rate 16:00); chronomodulated vs flat infusion in metastatic colorectal cancer: "severe mucosal toxicities in only" 14 % vs 76 % of patients, and "significantly increased the rate of patients with an objective treatment response from 29% to 51%" (Levi 1997). Meta-analysis of 842 patients: overall survival improved in men, not women (Giacchetti 2012).
- Indomethacin: 33 % adverse events after morning dosing vs 7 % after evening dosing; Theo24 withdrawn after evening-dose toxicity.
- Pooled analysis of 1,077 cancer patients: "cases of circadian disruption were associated with significantly worse survival and quality of life" (cites Ballesta 2017; rest-activity based).
- Translation gap: "typically perform experiments during the daytime, but this design" is out of phase for nocturnal rodents (95 % of lab animals). "Clinically, there is no emphasis put on the timing of drug administration" (Fig 1 legend).

## Not addressed / limits
- Opinion piece: no quantitative synthesis; selective examples; effect sizes are quoted from other papers.
- Does not address how to measure internal clock state in patients beyond mentioning circadian biomarkers; nothing about untimed or hospital-hours tissue sampling, nor internal (organ-organ) desynchrony.
- Clinical examples are scheduling effects (clock time of dosing), not internal phase.
