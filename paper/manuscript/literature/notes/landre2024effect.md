# landre2024effect
**Ref:** Landré et al., ESMO Open 9:102220 (2024). DOI 10.1016/j.esmoop.2023.102220. Type: clinical (meta-analysis). Lab: Lévi / Duchemann (Paris-Saclay, AP-HP).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Does the time of day (ToD) of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) infusion affect survival? Study-level meta-analysis (PubMed, Cochrane, congress abstracts to Aug 2023) of 13
retrospective reports: NSCLC 47%, renal cell carcinoma 24%, melanoma 20%, urothelial 5%, oesophageal 4%; anti-PD-1/PD-L1 98%, anti-CTLA-4 18%; nine countries. Human cohorts only; no tissue, no clock measurement.
**Sampling:** infusion clock time, hospital hours: "ICI infusion ToD is usually spread between 08:00 and 18:00". Early/late split by study-specific cut-offs (12:00-14:00 in 6 studies, 16:00-16:30 in 7).

## Method (if any)
Hazard ratios (HR) of early vs late groups pooled in RevMan 5.4 (fixed or random effects by heterogeneity); funnel plots; no individual-patient data.

## Key results
- "Thirteen studies involved 1663 patients (Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0-1, 83%; males/females, 67%/33%)"; 1037 early vs 626 late.
- OS: "the early ToD groups had longer OS (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.42-0.58; P < 0.00001)"; PFS HR 0.51 (0.42-0.61, 7 studies); response rate also better (6 studies).
- NSCLC only: OS HR 0.53 (0.34-0.80, P = 0.003), PFS HR 0.55 (0.44-0.69); renal cell carcinoma and melanoma OS also favour early (Fig 4).
- Cut-off subgroup: "studies with a cut-off between 12:00 and 14:00 (HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.33-0.53; P < 0.00001)" vs 16:00-16:30 HR 0.58 (0.47-0.72), subgroup P = 0.05.
- Dropping the one outlier study gives HR 0.52 (0.44-0.61, Fig S5); "all the results showing an impact of ToD consistently favored early injections".

## Not addressed / limits
- "All 13 retained study reports consisted of retrospective evaluations of ToD ICI administration effects": slot availability, frailty, number of cycles, wealth and patient preference confound; authors cite a "potential associative bias with the number of administered cycles".
- Infusion clock time is not internal time: chronotype, shift work, season, latitude and individual clock phase are not measured. No tumour or host clock data. Cut-offs differ across studies; publication bias is not formally tested beyond funnel plots; 3 of 16 reports discarded.
- No sex analysis, no immune-related adverse events. Heterogeneity between studies is acknowledged but I2 not quoted in the text.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** refs line 80 (13 studies, 1663 patients, HR 0.50 OS, 0.51 PFS, retrospective, hypothesis-generating): supported. (NSCLC, RCC, melanoma, urothelial, oesophageal; "etc." in our wording is fine.)
- **Relation to clocksync:** background and motivation. Clinical timing effects are defined by wall-clock time, while the clocksync premise is that patients' internal phase is unknown; supports a hospital-hours constraint (infusions all 08:00-18:00).

## Ideas
- Per-patient internal-phase information would test whether ToD effect is explained by chronotype; needs a cohort with infusion time and a clock read-out (none in our data). Not done in registered experiments (grep ICI/infusion: none).
- Primary studies to cite: Qian 2021 (melanoma, ICI timing), Karaboué 2022 (NSCLC, ICI timing), both in refs 25-26 of this paper.
