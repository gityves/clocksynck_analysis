# schernhammer2001rotating
**Ref:** Schernhammer, Laden, Speizer, Willett, Hunter, Kawachi, Colditz, J Natl Cancer Inst 2001 (93:1563-1568). DOI 10.1093/jnci/93.20.1563 (from the page URL). Type: clinical (epidemiology). Lab: Colditz / Channing (Harvard).
**Source:** full text

## Question and design
Prospective cohort, Nurses' Health Study, 10 years follow-up (June 1988 to May 1998). Of 103 613 nurses answering the 1988 questionnaire, 85 197 answered the shift question; 78 562 analysed. **Sampling:** questionnaire only, no samples, no circadian measure.
Exposure: "Information was ascertained in 1988 about the total number of years during which the nurses had worked rotating night shifts with at least three nights per month" (categories never, 1-2, 3-5, 6-9, 10-14, 15-19, 20-29, 30+ y; collapsed to 0, 1-14, 15-29, 30+). Outcome: 2441 incident, mostly record-confirmed, breast cancers. Logistic regression, RR with 95% CI, adjusted for breast-cancer risk factors.

## Method (if any)
Not a method paper.

## Key results
- 2441 cases. "we documented 2441 incident breast cancer cases"
- Person-years: never 40.4%, 1-14 y 52.2%, 15-29 y 5.6%, >=30 y 1.8%.
- Multivariate RR: 1-14 y 1.08 (95% CI 0.99-1.18), 15-29 y 1.08 (0.90-1.30), >=30 y 1.36 (1.04-1.78); P trend .02. "Women who had worked 30 or more years on rotating night shifts had a 36% greater risk of breast cancer"
- Premenopausal: 1-14 y RR 1.23 (0.97-1.55), >=20 y 1.66 (0.81-3.40), trend P .12. "Among premenopausal women, we observed an increased breast cancer risk of 23%"
- Interpretation by melatonin suppression by light at night, not by clock genes.

## Not addressed / limits
- "Although we did not validate self-reported duration of rotating nightshifts" (cohort self-reports otherwise accurate).
- Only the top category is significant and holds 1.8% of person-years; the CIs of the lower categories include 1.0.
- Exposure is lifetime years at baseline, not schedule intensity; no circadian phase, no clock-gene or tissue data; 10-year follow-up.
- Later meta-analyses disagree (see boivin2022disturbance, which cites meta-analyses finding little to no effect on breast cancer).
- Women only, nurses.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** refs_circadian_desynchrony claim (n 78 562, increased breast cancer risk after 30+ years) is supported; the elevated RR is modest (1.36, lower CI 1.04), not general to all durations.
- **Relation to clocksync:** background for "Cancer interaction": evidence that chronic circadian disruption raises cancer risk is epidemiological and contested; no desynchrony measurement exists.

## Ideas
- Motivates the paper: no human study links a measured internal desynchrony to cancer; clocksync on tumour versus normal and on healthy donors is the missing measurement (state as gap, do not claim causality).
- Missing references: Wegrzyn 2017 (nurses, >=20 y night work, breast cancer, started young), Travis 2016 and Dun 2020 (meta-analyses finding little effect), IARC 2020 (night shift work probably carcinogenic), all cited by Boivin.
