# balsalobre2000resetting
**Ref:** Balsalobre, Brown, Marcacci, Tronche, Kellendonk, Reichardt, Schutz, Schibler, Science 2000. DOI 10.1126/science.289.5488.2344. Type: biology. Lab: Schibler.
**Source:** full text (the extracted file begins with the end of an unrelated Report; the paper starts at "Resetting of Circadian Time in Peripheral Tissues by Glucocorticoid Signaling")

## Question and design
Can glucocorticoid signalling reset peripheral clocks without touching the SCN? Rat-1 fibroblasts (100 nM
dexamethasone, 1 h; time course 44 h); mouse liver, kidney, heart after intraperitoneal dexamethasone-phosphate
(2 ug/g) at 10 zeitgeber times (ZT1, 3, 5, 8, 10, 11, 14, 18, 21, 23), then 36-60 h of tissue sampling; GR mRNA in situ in
SCN; liver-specific GR knock-out (GRAlfpCre). RNase protection, single profiles per condition (n of animals per
point not stated in the text). Male and female mice 8-24 weeks, 12:12 light. **Sampling:** timed; injections
around the 24 h day; sampled every few hours for 1.5-2.5 days.

## Method (if any)
Apparent phase response curve (aPRC): phase shift of Dbp and Rev-erba mRNA peaks as a function of injection ZT.

## Key results
- Dexamethasone induces circadian expression of Per1, Per2, Per3, Cry1, Rev-erba, Dbp in rat-1 cells, as serum does (Fig. 1).
- "the glucocorticoid hormone analog dexamethasone induces circadian gene expression in cultured rat-1 fibroblasts and transiently changes the phase of circadian gene expression in liver, kidney, and heart." (Abstract)
- GR mRNA is not detectable in the SCN at any of the times examined (ZT2-ZT22); dexamethasone does not shift SCN phase (data not shown).
- Phase shifts in liver: delay when injected at ZT14 and ZT21, advance at ZT1, little change at ZT5 (Fig. 3). No dead zone:
  "no extended dead zone can be observed in the aPRC of liver" (Results): peripheral phase can be changed at any time of day.
- In liver-specific GR knock-out, DBP is delayed by about 3-4 h in kidney and heart but not liver: the effect is tissue autonomous (Fig. 4).
- Liver clock phase is unchanged in the knock-out under normal conditions: "glucocorticoids cannot be the only signals setting the phase of peripheral clocks."

## Not addressed / limits
- Pharmacological dose of a synthetic analogue, not the physiological corticosterone rhythm; corticosterone itself
  did not shift liver or lung in stokkan2001entrainment (at ZT5, the zero-effect time here).
- Rodents; liver, kidney, heart only; resets are transient; SCN non-response is "data not shown".
- Does not show that glucocorticoids are the physiological coupling signal between SCN and periphery.

## Link to us
- **Intro claim(s):** a hormonal cue can set phase in peripheral tissues independently of the SCN (Internal
  desynchrony / coupling routes). Our refs summary is supported (rat/mouse, peripheral not SCN).
- **Relation to clocksync:** background. Steroid treatment (dexamethasone, prednisone) is a plausible driver of
  tissue-specific phase offsets in patients.
