# peters2026itimes
**Ref:** Peters S et al. (ETOP-Roche i-TIMES), conference abstract LBA2, European Lung Cancer Congress, Copenhagen, 25-28 March 2026. No journal article found. Type: clinical (pooled patient-level analysis of eight randomized trials, retrospective by infusion time). Lab: ETOP / Roche (presenter S. Peters, CHUV).
**Source:** secondary report only (Lung Cancers Today news article, 27 March 2026, which quotes the abstract LBA2); the abstract itself and the slides were not accessed. ESMO's own news item could not be retrieved. Numbers need checking against the abstract.

## Question and design
Does the time of day of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) infusion affect overall survival (OS) in lung cancer, "and secondarily in other solid tumors using data from randomized clinical trials"? Propensity-score matched analysis within eight international randomized trials (Roche-sponsored phase 2 or 3, at least one ICI arm as monotherapy or combination, OS a primary endpoint). The trials were randomized for treatment, not for infusion time; time of day is an observed covariate. Trials are not named in the report. **Sampling:** n/a (clinical); timing from the start time of the first two cycles: early up to 12:00, late after 12:00; patients with at least two ICI cycles and timing data.

## Key results
- Primary cohort n = 3,060, median follow-up 42.6 months: early 1,244 (44%), late 964 (32%), mixed 852 (28%).
- Full cohort: median OS 18.5 months early vs 15.7 months late; OS hazard ratio late vs early, stratified by study, 1.088 (95% CI 0.982-1.207).
- Matched cohort (matching by trial on sex, PD-L1 <1% vs >=1%, liver and brain metastases): 1,550 patients, 775 per group, 567 deaths each; median OS 17.3 months (95% CI 15.8-19.2) early vs 16.0 months (95% CI 14.8-17.7) late; HR 1.039 (95% CI 0.925-1.168). "The upper bound of the 95% CI was below the prespecified limit" (the numeric margin is not given in the report).
- Conclusion (authors): the study "met its primary objective, demonstrating non-inferiority of late versus early administration" for OS; timing within the day is "unlikely to be a critical determinant of outcomes" in lung cancer.
- PFS not reported in the source.

## Not addressed / limits
- Retrospective with respect to timing (confounding by who is treated in the morning is only partly handled by matching on four covariates); two-cycle classification; midday cutoff; lung cancer only; industry-sponsored trials.
- Whether the retracted LungTIME-C01 trial is included: the report does not mention it; as the eligibility is Roche-sponsored trials and LungTIME-C01 was a single-centre Hunan trial, it is almost certainly not included (our inference, not stated).
